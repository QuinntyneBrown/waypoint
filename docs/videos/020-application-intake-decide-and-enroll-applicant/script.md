# 020 · Deciding on an application and enrolling the applicant

## Welcome

Once an application has been reviewed, someone has to decide. This video shows how a program administrator, Pat Morgan, turns an application into a decision. Pat can decline it gently, or approve it and place the young person in a cohort with a mentor. Only program administrators can make these decisions.

[pause 2s]

## A minor without consent

Pat opens the applications page and chooses a review. The first application belongs to Imani Clarke. She is fifteen, and her guardian, Ruth Clarke, filled in the form. Imani's status is needs consent, and the review window explains why. It says guardian participation consent is required. This minor cannot be approved into active participation until a valid consent record is linked.

Look at the approve button. It is still available, because Pat can approve the application and create the youth account. But Waypoint will not let a young person under eighteen take part in the program until a guardian's consent is on record. Pat does not have to remember this rule. The notice on the screen states it.

[pause 2s]

## Declining softly

Pat decides there is no place for Imani this term. Pat types a decision note, no place is available this term, and presses decline. Waypoint asks one more question. It says, decline this applicant without creating an account? It also says the optional note stays on the application record.

Pat confirms. A green message says the application was declined, and the status changes to declined. Nothing is deleted. The application and the note remain on record, and no account is ever created. That is why the program calls a decline soft. The note is optional, so Pat could just as well leave it empty.

[pause 2s]

## Approving and enrolling

Now for Amara Bell, who is eighteen and has no consent problem. Pat sets her interest track, then chooses a cohort and a mentor. Both are required. Without them the approval cannot go through. The mentor list is limited to mentors assigned to that cohort, so Pat cannot pair Amara with someone who is not part of it.

Pat adds a note, excellent fit for this cohort, and presses approve. The confirmation names the cohort and the mentor. It asks, enrol this applicant in this cohort with this mentor? It also warns that the applicant contact will receive an approval email.

Pat confirms. The page says Amara was approved and enrolled. Behind that message, Waypoint creates her youth account and enrolls her in the cohort under the mentor Pat chose. Every approval and every decline is written to the audit log.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

A minor without guardian consent can be approved, but cannot take part until the guardian's consent is recorded. A decline is soft. The application stays on record, the note is optional, and no account is created. An approval needs a cohort and a mentor. It creates the youth account, enrolls the young person, and sends the applicant contact an email. Only program administrators decide, and every decision is audited.
