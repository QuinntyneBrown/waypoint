# 003 · Who is allowed to do what

## Welcome

Signing in proves who you are. It does not decide what you may do. This video shows how Waypoint checks, on every protected action, whether the person asking is allowed, and what people see when the answer is no.

[pause 2s]

## No sign-in, no access

Imagine someone gets hold of a link to a page for staff, but they are not signed in. When they open it, Waypoint does not show the page. It sends them to the sign-in page. Behind the scenes, any request for protected data from someone who is not signed in is turned down.

[pause 2s]

## The wrong role

Now Amara Bell, a youth, is signed in. She types the address of an administrator page into her browser. Waypoint answers with a page that says, you don't have access to that. It explains that the area is for a different role, and gives her two ways forward. She can go back to her own home, or switch to a different account.

This is the important part. The check is made on the server, every time. Hiding a button on screen is never the only protection, because a person can always type an address by hand.

When a signed-in person tries something their role does not allow, the attempt is also written to the audit log, so the program can see it happened.

[pause 2s]

## Only your own people

Roles are not the whole story. Grace Adeyemi is a mentor, and she is allowed to see youth who are assigned to her. Suppose she somehow learns the identifier of a youth who is not in her group, and opens that youth's record. She gets the same page, you don't have access to that. Knowing the identifier is not enough.

The same idea applies everywhere. A youth sees only their own records. A guardian sees only their own child's shared summary. A volunteer sees only their assigned session. And a program administrator can see every cohort, though private check-in notes stay restricted.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Not signed in means not allowed in. Signed in with the wrong role means a clear no, and the attempt is logged. A mentor can only reach the youth assigned to them, even if they know another youth's identifier. The server decides every time, so a hidden button is never the only protection.
