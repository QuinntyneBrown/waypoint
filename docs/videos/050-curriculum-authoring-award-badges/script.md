# 050 · Awarding badges

## Welcome

A badge means most when it is given with care. This video shows how a manual badge is awarded to a young person, what Waypoint records when it happens, and how automatic badges differ.

[pause 2s]

## Finding the badge

Pat Morgan, a program administrator, has defined a manual badge called servant heart. It is described as recognition a mentor gives by hand. On the badge builder page, its card shows the word manual, an awards count of zero, and a button called award badge.

Automatic badges do not have this button. They are earned when a youth meets a rule, so there is no one to choose.

[pause 2s]

## Choosing the youth

Pat presses award badge, and a small window opens. Its title names the badge, and it asks for one thing, a youth recipient. Pat opens the list and chooses Amara Bell.

Nothing happens yet. The window has two buttons, confirm award and cancel, so Pat can still back out or pick someone else.

[pause 2s]

## Confirming the award

Pat presses confirm award. A green message says, the badge was awarded to Amara Bell. Back on the card, the awards count has gone from zero to one.

Behind the scenes, Waypoint captures who gave the badge and when. That record is written to the audit log, so the program can always see who recognized whom. A mentor, as well as a program administrator, is able to award a manual badge.

[pause 2s]

## Automatic awards

Automatic badges work the other way. When a youth meets the rule, such as finishing a module or passing a quiz, Waypoint awards the badge on its own. It does so exactly once, and a notification is sent. A youth can never collect the same automatic badge twice.

And a badge that has been archived can no longer be given to anyone new, but youth who already earned it keep it.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Only manual badges have an award button. Pat picks the youth, then confirms. Waypoint records who awarded the badge and when, and writes it to the audit log. Automatic badges are given once, by rule.
