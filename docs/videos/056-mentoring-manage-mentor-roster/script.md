# 056 · The mentor dashboard and roster

## Welcome

A mentor looks after a group of young people. Each morning, the mentor needs to know three things. How is my group doing, what is waiting for me, and who needs attention. Waypoint answers all three on the mentor dashboard, and it shows each mentor only their own youth. This video walks through the dashboard and the roster.

[pause 2s]

## The dashboard

Grace signs in, and lands on her dashboard. At the top, four numbers. My youth, pending reviews, average progress, and at risk. Under pending reviews, Waypoint also shows how many are overdue, in red, so late work stands out.

These numbers are worked out only over the youth assigned to Grace. A youth who belongs to another mentor is never counted, and never shown.

Below the numbers, a short list of her youth, with their progress and an At risk tag where it applies. Further down is a preview of the reviews queue. Each item shows the youth, the kind of work, and whether it is due or overdue, with a button that goes straight to grading.

[pause 2s]

## The roster

Grace presses View all youth. The My youth page lists everyone assigned to her. For each youth she sees the cohort, progress, when they were last active, and their status.

At the top there is a search box, and four filters. All, On track, At risk, and New. Grace presses At risk, and the list narrows to the youth who need attention first.

[pause 2s]

## When nothing matches

Suppose Grace searches for a name that is not on her roster. The list does not just go blank. A message says no youth match that filter, and a button clears the filters.

Now think of a new mentor who has not been given anyone yet. The dashboard does not show an error. The numbers are zero, and a message says, no youth assigned yet. Assigned youth will appear when the caseload is ready.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

The dashboard shows four counts, for the mentor's own youth only. Overdue reviews are called out. The roster can be searched, and filtered by All, On track, At risk, and New. Empty results always come with a clear message, and a way back. A mentor never sees youth who are not assigned to them.
