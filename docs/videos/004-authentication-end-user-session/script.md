# 004 · Signing out and session expiry

## Welcome

In a youth program, a lot of people share computers. So how a session ends matters as much as how it starts. This video shows two ways a session ends: when a person signs out on purpose, and when Waypoint signs them out after they have been away.

[pause 2s]

## Signing out

Amara Bell has finished for the day on a shared computer. In the menu on the left, at the very bottom, there is a button that says, sign out. She presses it.

Waypoint takes her to the sign-in page. Behind the scenes, her session is closed, and the saved sign-in is removed from that computer. If the next person on that computer tries to go back to one of Amara's pages, Waypoint simply sends them to the sign-in page. Nothing of Amara's is left to see without signing in again.

[pause 2s]

## When a session expires

Now suppose Amara does not sign out. She is writing a reflection on a lesson, and she gets called away. She has typed a few sentences and picked how she is feeling. She is not clicking or typing, and after a while, Waypoint signs her out for her own safety.

When she returns, she sees a page that says, you've been signed out. It explains that this happens after a period of inactivity, and it says that any work in progress has been saved. There is one button, sign in again.

[pause 2s]

## Picking up where you left off

Amara presses sign in again and enters her email and password. Waypoint takes her back to the same reflection page. A message at the top says, draft restored after session expiry. What she typed, and how she said she was feeling, are still there.

So expiry protects the account without punishing the person. The session ends, but the draft is kept for the person it belongs to.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Sign out closes the session and leaves nothing of the account on the computer. An idle session ends by itself, and the person sees a clear page that says they were signed out. Work in progress is kept, and after signing in again it is restored.
