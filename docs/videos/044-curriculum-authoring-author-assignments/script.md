# 044 · Authoring assignments

## Welcome

Lessons teach, and assignments let a young person show what they learned. This video shows how a program administrator builds an assignment in the curriculum builder: what a youth can hand in, how the work is scored, and when it is due.

[pause 2s]

## The assignment form

Meet Pat Morgan, a program administrator. Pat opens the curriculum builder, picks a track, and finds the module the assignment belongs to. Pat presses add assignment, and a form opens.

Two things are required. The first is a title, and Pat types, serving others. The second is a task description, which tells the youth exactly what to do. Pat writes, describe one concrete way you served someone this week.

Next comes a group of boxes called allowed submission types. A youth may hand in a text response, a photo, a link, or a file. Pat ticks text response and link. At least one type must be chosen, so an assignment can never be created that nobody can answer.

[pause 2s]

## The rubric

Below that is the rubric. A rubric is a list of criteria, and every criterion carries a point value. Pat adds the first criterion, specific and reflective, and gives it five points. Then Pat presses add rubric criterion and writes a second one, connects to Scripture, worth three points.

Pat does not add the points up. Waypoint does that, and it keeps the total with the assignment.

[pause 2s]

## Due rules

The same form holds the due rules. Pat picks a due date and sets a grace period of two days. There are two more choices. Allow late submissions lets work come in after the due date, and the mentor can see which work was late. Require mentor approval means the assignment is not complete until the mentor approves it.

[pause 2s]

## Saving and publishing

When Pat presses create assignment, the new assignment appears in the module as a draft. It shows its title and its total, eight points. A draft is not yet in front of any youth.

Pat then presses publish assignment. A green message says, assignment published and assigned to enrolled youth. From that moment, the work is assigned to the youth in that module's cohorts.

Only a program administrator can edit the curriculum. Anyone else who tries is turned away.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

An assignment needs a title, a task description, and at least one way to submit. Each rubric criterion has points, and Waypoint keeps the total. Due rules cover the due date, the grace period, late work, and mentor approval. A draft stays hidden until it is published.
