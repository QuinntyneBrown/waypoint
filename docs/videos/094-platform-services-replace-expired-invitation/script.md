# 094 · Replacing an expired invitation

## Welcome

In Waypoint, nobody signs themselves up. People join through an invitation link, and for safety, that link does not last forever. This video shows what happens when someone opens a link that has expired, and how they ask for a new one.

[pause 2s]

## An old link

Pat Morgan, a program administrator, invited Amara Bell to join as a youth. Amara was busy, and she did not open the email in time. When she finally clicks the link, Waypoint does not let her create an account with it.

She sees a page headed, this invite link has expired. Underneath, it explains why. For your security, invite links are single use, and they expire after fourteen days.

[pause 2s]

## Request a new invite

The page offers a button, request a new invite. Amara presses it. Waypoint passes the request on to the person who invited her, which in this story is Pat.

Then Amara sees a short confirmation. It says, if a replacement can be sent, the person who invited you has been notified. The wording is careful on purpose. The page gives the same answer whether or not a replacement is possible, so nobody can use this page to find out which email addresses or invitations exist.

There is also a button, back to sign in, for anyone who already has an account.

[pause 2s]

## Other link problems

An expired link is one of a few cases that look similar. If the link has already been used, the page says so, and reminds the person that their account exists and they can sign in with the password they created. If an invitation was cancelled, the page says the invite is no longer active. And a link that is not valid at all says that too.

In every case, the person lands on a clear page with a way forward, not a broken form.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Invite links are single use and expire after fourteen days. An expired link shows a plain explanation and a button to ask for a new one. The person who sent the invitation is notified, and the page does not reveal whether a replacement can be sent.
