# 101 · The staff activity report

## Welcome

Mentors are the heart of the program, and a mentor with too many young people cannot give each one enough time. The staff activity report shows how the work is shared. This video shows what it contains.

[pause 2s]

## Opening the staff view

Pat Morgan, a program administrator, opens the reports hub and chooses the staff activity tab. A line explains it. Caseload, check-ins, and reviews within the current report filters. That means the staff view follows the same filters as the rest of the hub. Pat has filtered to one mentor, Grace Adeyemi.

[pause 2s]

## One card per mentor

Each mentor gets a card. It shows the mentor's name and email, and then three counts. Caseload is how many young people the mentor looks after. Check-ins is how many check-ins the mentor has recorded. Reviews is how many reviews the mentor has completed.

Under the counts is a load balance label, and the target range for that mentor. For Grace the label says under target, because her caseload is below the target range. The range is shown as a number of youth, so nobody has to remember it.

If a mentor goes over the maximum, the label turns into a red warning, so Pat can move some of their youth to a mentor with room. Assigning mentors has its own video.

[pause 2s]

## When there is nothing to show

If the filters match no staff at all, the page does not show an empty grid. It says there is no staff activity for these filters, and asks Pat to change the report scope.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

The staff view shows caseload, check-ins, and reviews for each mentor. A load balance label compares the caseload with the target range, and overload is flagged in red. It follows the hub filters, and an empty scope gets a clear message.
