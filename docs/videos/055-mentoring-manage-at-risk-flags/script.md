# 055 · Keeping watch on at-risk youth

## Welcome

Some young people quietly drift away. They stop logging in, they miss a session, or they fall behind the rest of their cohort. Waypoint watches for these signals and puts the youth on a list, so a mentor can step in early. This video shows how a mentor works with that list.

[pause 2s]

## The at-risk list

Grace opens At-risk. The page lists every active flag for her youth. David Osei is on the list. His card says why he is there, in plain words. Missed one session. Below the reasons it says who flagged him and when.

A youth can land on this list in two ways. Waypoint raises a flag by itself when a signal is met, such as inactivity, a missed session, or progress well below the cohort. Or the mentor raises one by hand. At the top of the page, Grace can pick a youth, write a reason, and press Raise flag. That is for a concern the automatic signals did not catch.

[pause 2s]

## Taking action

Each card gives Grace four ways to respond. She can view the youth's record. She can log a check-in. She can send a message. Or she can unflag.

She chooses Message. A box opens right on the card, with the youth's name on it. Grace writes, checking in after your missed session, and sends it. A line at the top confirms that the message was sent, and David receives it in his thread.

[pause 2s]

## Resolving a flag

Later, David is back on track. Grace presses Unflag. Waypoint does not just remove the flag. It asks her to confirm, and to give a resolution reason. The reasons are Reconnected, Handed to director, Left program, and Flagged in error. Grace picks one, and confirms.

The message says the flag is resolved, and David leaves the list. If nothing else is flagged, the page says there are no active flags. If the signals return, the youth can be flagged again.

Raising and resolving a flag are both visible to the program director, and both are written to the audit log.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

The list shows each youth's reasons, and who flagged them and when. Flags come from automatic signals, or from the mentor. From each card the mentor can view the youth, log a check-in, message, or unflag. Unflagging needs a resolution reason. The director can see every flag, and every change is audited.
