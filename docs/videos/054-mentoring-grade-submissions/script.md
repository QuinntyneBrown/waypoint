# 054 · Grading a submission

## Welcome

When a young person turns in an assignment, a mentor grades it against a rubric. The mentor can approve it, ask for a revision, or save a draft and come back later. This video follows Grace as she grades one of Amara's assignments.

[pause 2s]

## The submission

Grace opens Reviews and presses Review on Amara's assignment, a service reflection. The page has two sides. On the left is the submitted work, exactly as Amara wrote it. Amara described how she helped welcome a new person.

On the right is the rubric. Each criterion has a box for points, and the allowed range is written beside it, here zero to ten. As Grace types a score, the total is worked out for her. Each score has to stay within its range.

[pause 2s]

## Asking for a revision

Grace thinks the work is a good start, but not finished. She gives it seven points, and presses Request revision. Waypoint stops her. Feedback is required. A revision always comes with a note, so the youth knows what to improve.

Below the feedback box there is a choice, notify guardian about the outcome. It starts switched on, and Grace can turn it off. Once she has written her feedback, the submission becomes Needs revision, and Amara can send it in again.

[pause 2s]

## Saving a draft

Sometimes Grace is not ready to decide. She writes her feedback, and presses Save draft. A message says the draft was saved. Her scores and comment are kept, but nothing changes for Amara. The assignment keeps the same status, and Amara sees nothing yet. When Grace comes back, her draft is waiting.

[pause 2s]

## Approving

On another day, Amara hands in her improved work. Grace gives it ten out of ten, and the total shows ten of ten. She presses Approve, and confirms.

The submission becomes Approved. The rubric points, and any badge attached to the assignment, are applied. Amara is told in near real time. Grace returns to the review queue, and the assignment is gone from it.

Only the assigned mentor and program staff can grade, and each grading action is written to the audit log.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Score each rubric criterion within its range, and the total is worked out for you. Approve applies points and badges, and tells the youth. A revision needs a note, and the guardian is notified unless the mentor turns that off. A draft keeps your work without changing what the youth sees. Every grading action is audited.
