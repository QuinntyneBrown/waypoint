# 063 · Viewing the consent record

## Welcome

When a guardian signs a consent, Waypoint keeps a record of it. This video shows what that record contains, how a guardian can have a copy sent to their email, and what they see if no record exists yet.

[pause 2s]

## The record

Ruth Clarke signs in and opens the Consent page. The Participation consent card is her record of the consent she signed for Imani.

The card shows who signed, and how they are related to the young person. It shows the date and time of the signature. The method is a typed e-signature, which means the guardian typed their full name to sign. It shows the date until which the consent is valid. And it shows a reference number, which is unique to this record. A green badge says Active.

If anyone ever asks what was agreed and when, this card is the answer.

[pause 2s]

## Emailing a copy

Under the details there is a button, Email me a copy. Ruth presses it, and a green message confirms that her consent record has been emailed.

The email is sent to Ruth's own address. It repeats the details. The young person's name, the reference, who signed and when, the method, the valid dates, whether media consent was given, and the current status. She can keep it for her own files.

[pause 2s]

## When there is no record

Some guardians have no young person linked to their account yet. For them, the page does not show an empty form. It says, no consent record yet, and explains that a participation and media consent record will appear once a youth is linked.

And as always, a guardian can only open their own record. Anyone else's is refused.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

The consent record shows who signed, when, by what method, until when, and the reference number. A guardian can email themselves a copy at any time. With no linked youth, the page says so plainly.
