# 045 · Authoring badges

## Welcome

A badge is a small recognition a young person can earn on their journey. This video shows how a program administrator defines badges in the badge builder, and how a badge is either earned automatically or given by hand.

[pause 2s]

## An automatic badge

Pat Morgan, a program administrator, opens the badge builder. At the top is a form called new badge definition.

Pat types a name, foundations finisher, and an icon name, award. Then comes a description, earned by finishing a whole module. The next choice is the criteria kind, and Pat picks automatic.

Once the kind is automatic, two more choices appear. The first is the automatic rule. Waypoint offers five. Module completed, lesson completed, quiz passed, assignment approved, and a five session attendance streak. Pat chooses module completed, and then picks which module it applies to under rule target.

There is one more optional choice, a mapped competency. If the badge should count toward a skill in the progression framework, Pat picks that skill here. Otherwise it stays on no competency mapping.

[pause 2s]

## Saving it

Pat presses save badge, and a card appears. It says auto, shows the name, and lists the rule, the competency, and a count of awards. The count starts at zero.

From now on, when a youth meets that rule, Waypoint gives them the badge on its own, exactly once, and sends a notification. Nobody has to remember to hand it out.

[pause 2s]

## A manual badge

Not every kind of recognition can be measured. Pat makes a second badge, servant heart, and chooses manual. The automatic rule and the rule target disappear, because there is nothing to measure. A manual badge is given by a person.

Its card has a button called award badge, and you will see how that works in the next video.

[pause 2s]

## Archiving a badge

A badge can also be retired. Pat presses archive badge on the first card, and a message confirms it. The form itself says what this means. Archived badges remain on youth records but stop earning. Youth who already earned the badge keep it, and no new youth can earn it.

Only a program administrator can change the badge definitions.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

A badge has a name, an icon, a description, and a kind. Automatic badges follow one of five rules and can map to a competency. Manual badges are given by a person. Archiving stops new awards but never takes a badge away from a youth who earned it.
