# 088 · Planned maintenance

## Welcome

Every system needs a little care from time to time. When Waypoint is taken down for planned maintenance, people should not see a broken page or a strange error. This video shows what they see instead.

[pause 2s]

## What the server says

When the program puts Waypoint into maintenance mode, the server answers every request with a status that means, service unavailable, together with a hint about when to try again. That is what the requirement describes. It is the way the server tells every browser and every app that this is a planned pause, not a failure.

[pause 2s]

## What Amara sees

Meet Amara. She is signed in and working in Waypoint when maintenance begins. She loads a page, and the portal notices that answer from the server. It takes her to a calm page of its own.

The small heading says, scheduled maintenance. The title says, we will be right back. The message says, Waypoint is down for a short bit of maintenance. Nothing you have done is lost. Please check again shortly.

Think about why those words matter. A young person who sees a technical error may worry that they broke something, or that their work is gone. This page tells them in plain words that nothing is lost, and that the pause is planned.

[pause 2s]

## Checking again

The page has one button. It says, try again. When Amara presses it, the page reloads, and Waypoint asks the server again. If maintenance is still going, she can wait a little longer and try once more.

She has only one job here, which is to wait. The page does not ask her to fix anything, or to contact anyone.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

During planned maintenance the server answers with service unavailable, and the portal shows a page that says, we will be right back. The page promises that nothing is lost. One button, try again, asks the server whether it is ready. People are never left looking at a broken screen.
