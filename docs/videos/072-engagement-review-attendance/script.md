# 072 · Reviewing attendance

## Welcome

Once attendance is recorded, it needs to be read. This video shows how staff review attendance by session and by youth, see who needs attention, and narrow the view to the group and dates they care about.

[pause 2s]

## By session

Meet Pat Morgan, a program administrator. Pat opens the attendance administration page. It says, review turnout by session or attendance patterns by youth.

The first view is by session. Each row is one session, with its cohort and date. Then come three numbers: how many were present, how many were marked out of how many youth, and the attendance rate. At the end is a status. A session can be saved, or it can be marked low turnout, so Pat can spot a session that needs a follow-up. Each row also has an edit button, which opens that session's attendance sheet.

[pause 2s]

## By youth

Pat switches to the by youth tab. Now each row is a young person. In our example, Amara Bell attended two of three sessions. The row also counts her late, excused and absent sessions, gives her attendance rate, and shows a trend. Hers says, improving.

Next to her name there is a label that says, below seventy percent. Youth under the threshold are flagged this way, so staff can reach out early, before a pattern turns into a problem.

[pause 2s]

## Filtering and exporting

Above the table are filters. Pat chooses a cohort, a start date and an end date, and presses apply filters. The history and the rates are worked out again for exactly that group and those dates.

There are also two export buttons, export CSV and export PDF. They produce a file that follows the same filters, so what Pat downloads matches what Pat sees.

Mentors have the same history page for their own cohorts, and the program director can open it for the whole organization.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Review attendance by session or by youth. Each session shows its present count, rate and a status, including low turnout. Each youth shows their counts, a rate and a trend, and a flag below seventy percent. Filters narrow the view, and the exports follow them.
