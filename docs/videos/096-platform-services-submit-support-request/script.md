# 096 · Asking the program for help

## Welcome

Sometimes a person is stuck and the help articles do not answer their question. This video shows how someone sends a support request in Waypoint, and what they see once it is sent.

[pause 2s]

## The support form

David Osei cannot find a lesson his mentor assigned. He opens the contact support page. Next to the title there is a button, browse help articles, in case the answer is already written down.

The form is short. First he picks what his question is about from a list. Then there is a box labeled, how can we help. Below that is his reply email, which Waypoint fills in from his account, so nobody has to type it again. Last, there is an optional screenshot. It accepts a J P G or a P N G, up to five megabytes, and a note reminds him to avoid including private information.

[pause 2s]

## A description is required

David tries to send a message that says only, too short. Waypoint does not send it. The box turns red, and a message says, please describe your issue, at least ten characters.

The description, the category, and the reply email are all required. A request that says nothing would not help the person who answers.

[pause 2s]

## Sent

David writes a proper message. He says that he cannot find the lesson his mentor assigned him this week. He presses send message.

The form is replaced by a page that says, thanks, we have got your message. It adds that the team will follow up at the email he gave, and that replies usually come within a couple of days.

Behind the scenes, Waypoint saves the request and emails it to the program support inbox, which belongs to the director. David gets his acknowledgement on screen straight away.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Support is one short form. The description, the category and the reply email are required, and a screenshot is optional. After sending, the person sees a clear acknowledgement, and the request is saved and sent to the program support inbox.
