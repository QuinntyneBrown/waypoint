# 110 · Protecting work you have not saved

## Welcome

Everyone has lost work at some point. A page closes by mistake, or a link is clicked too soon, and the words you just wrote are gone. Waypoint tries to stop that. This video shows how it warns you before you leave a page with unsaved work, and how it brings your draft back.

[pause 2s]

## Writing a reflection

Amara Bell is writing a reflection after a lesson. She has chosen how she feels, thoughtful, and she has typed her answer. I can listen before I try to solve the problem. She has also written one action she will take. She has not submitted anything.

Without thinking, she clicks the back to lesson link. Waypoint stops her. The browser asks, you have unsaved reflection changes. Save your reflection draft before leaving? She can select OK to save the draft, or cancel to stay on the page and keep writing. The same warning appears if she tries to close the tab or the browser window.

[pause 2s]

## Coming back to it

Amara chooses to save the draft. Later, she opens the reflection again. At the top, a green message says, draft restored. Her mood is selected, and her words are in the box, exactly as she left them. She can carry on, share it with her mentor if she wants to, and submit when she is ready.

[pause 2s]

## Other places it applies

This protection is not only for reflections. On the attendance page, Grace Adeyemi, a mentor, has marked a young person present and added a note. Beside the save button, the page says, unsaved changes. Once she saves, it changes to, changes saved. If she tried to leave with unsaved changes, she would get the same warning. The same is true when a mentor is grading a submission.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

If you have unsaved work, Waypoint asks before you leave, whether by a link or by closing the page. You can save a draft, or stay and keep going. When you come back, your draft is restored. And pages with unsaved changes say so, so you always know where you stand.
