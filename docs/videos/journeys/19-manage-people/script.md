# 19 · Manage people

## Welcome

A program grows quickly. People join, change roles, forget passwords, and sometimes step back. This video follows Pat Morgan, a program administrator, as Pat finds people in the directory, reads a young person's record, and manages an account, including the actions that Waypoint makes hard to do by accident.

[pause 2s]

## Finding someone

Pat opens People from the menu. The page is called people directory, and its subtitle says, search and filter everyone in the program. At the top are a search box, a cohort list, and a row of role buttons. All roles, Youth, Mentor, Guardian, Volunteer, and Administrator.

Below is a table with a name, an email address, a role, a cohort, a status, and when the person was last active. Results come in pages, and a line at the bottom says how many people there are in all.

Pat types into the search box, and the table narrows as Pat types. Then Pat clicks the Youth button, and the list drops to three people, Amara, David, and Imani. Search, role, and cohort all work together. If nobody matches, the page says, no people found, and offers clear filters and invite people.

[pause 2s]

## A person's record

Pat opens a youth's record. At the top are the person's name, their cohort and role, and a badge showing that the account is active. Below is a row of six tabs. Profile. Guardian and consent. Progress. Attendance. Notes. And Account.

On the progress tab, Pat sees how many lessons the youth has finished, in words and as a bar. One of two lessons is complete, which is fifty percent. The guardian tab shows the guardian contact and the consent record. The notes tab holds check-in summaries only, and private prayer requests are never shown.

[pause 2s]

## Changing a role

Now Pat opens the record of Sam Whitfield, a volunteer, and chooses the Account tab. Sam has been asked to help as a mentor. Pat picks Mentor in the role list and presses change role.

A window opens, headed change account role. It names the old and new role. A warning says that this staff role grants access to young people's records and protected program data, that the change takes effect immediately, and that all current sessions will be revoked. Only when Pat presses confirm role change does anything happen, and the change is written to the audit log.

[pause 2s]

## Deactivating an account

When someone steps back, Pat presses deactivate account. The window explains that the person will be signed out and unable to sign in, and that progress, attendance, notes and reporting records are retained. It also says Pat can reactivate the account later.

After Pat confirms, the status reads deactivated, and a reactivate account button takes the place of the old one. Sending a password reset is just as direct. One press, and a reset link goes to the person's email address.

Removing a person is different. The window says it cannot be undone, and Pat must type the person's full name before the button works.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Search by name or email, and filter by role and cohort. One record has six tabs. A role change warns first and takes effect straight away. Deactivation is reversible and keeps every record. Removal cannot be undone, and asks for the person's name.
