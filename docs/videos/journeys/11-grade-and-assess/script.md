# 11 · Grade and assess

## Welcome

A young person's work is not finished when they hand it in. A mentor looks at it first. This video follows Grace Adeyemi, a mentor, through one round of assessment for Amara Bell. Grace grades an assignment, approves a quiz score, and records how far Amara has grown in one competency.

[pause 2s]

## The review queue

Grace opens Reviews. Everything waiting for her is in one list. Each line names the youth, the kind of work, and whether it is overdue. Amara has two items. A service reflection, which is overdue, and a quiz, which has no due date. Each has a Review button. At the top, a button says Approve all quiz scores, with a count beside it.

[pause 2s]

## Grading a submission

Grace presses Review on the service reflection. The page has two sides. On the left is the work, exactly as Amara wrote it. On the right is the rubric. Each criterion has a box for points, with the allowed range beside it, here zero to ten. As Grace types a score, the total is worked out for her.

She has three choices. Save draft keeps her work, and changes nothing for Amara. Request revision sends the work back, and always needs a note, so Amara knows what to improve. Approve accepts it. Below the feedback box there is a choice to notify the guardian about the outcome, and it starts switched on.

Amara's answer is strong, so Grace gives it ten out of ten, writes a short comment, and presses Approve. Waypoint asks her to confirm. The submission becomes Approved, the rubric points and any badge attached to the assignment are applied, and Amara is told. The item leaves the queue. Only the assigned mentor and program staff can grade, and every grading action is written to the audit log.

[pause 2s]

## Approving a quiz score

Back in the queue, Grace opens the quiz. Waypoint scored it automatically, but that score does not count yet. The page shows each question, the answer Amara picked, and which answer was correct. On the right is the auto score, forty percent, and the pass mark, seventy percent, with a note that it is below the pass mark.

Grace can approve the forty percent as it is, or type her own score. It must be a whole number from zero to one hundred. Perhaps Amara explained her answer in person, so Grace enters seventy five, and confirms.

The page now shows an approval record. It keeps the auto score, forty percent, and the approved score, seventy five percent, with Grace's name and the time. Only the approved score counts toward progress, and it is the only score Amara sees.

[pause 2s]

## Assessing a competency

Finally, Grace opens Amara's competencies page. Each competency has a card with a level and a box for notes. A level has four steps. Not started, Developing, Proficient, and Mastered.

For Community initiative, Grace sets Proficient, and writes, took initiative welcoming a new participant. She presses Save assessment. The card now shows who made the assessment, and when. The summary count at the top goes up by one, and Amara's progress view and reports always use the latest assessment.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Score each rubric criterion within its range, and the total is worked out for you. A revision always needs a note, and a draft keeps your work private. A quiz score only counts once a mentor approves it, and both numbers are kept for audit. Each competency level carries the mentor's name and the time.
