# 32 · Manage personal settings

## Welcome

Everyone in Waypoint can shape their own account. They can keep their details current, decide which messages they get, and make the screen comfortable to read. This video follows Amara Bell, a youth, through her settings.

[pause 2s]

## Profile

Amara opens her settings, and the profile tab comes first. She sees a display name and an email address, both marked as required. Below them are pronouns, which are optional, with a hint showing what to type, and a profile photo. Her name and email are already filled in, so she changes only what she wants.

She types her pronouns and presses save profile. A short message appears, profile updated. Name and email must always be present, and the email must look like an email address. The photo must be a real JPG or PNG up to five megabytes, and it is stored privately.

The same page holds change password. A new password must meet the password rules and match its confirmation before the button can be pressed.

[pause 2s]

## Notifications

Next Amara opens the notifications tab. It says, choose how you would like to hear from Waypoint, and that changes save automatically.

The page lists kinds of events. Lesson reminders, mentor messages, session reminders, work graded, badges earned, at-risk alerts, approval needed, and announcements. Each has a short description and two boxes. In-app shows the message inside Waypoint. Email is the optional extra channel.

Announcements are program-wide news, and for Amara the in-app box starts out empty. She decides she wants them, and ticks it. There is no save button. The change is stored at once, and a message confirms it. Notification preferences saved. When a person has turned a channel off for an event, Waypoint does not send on that channel.

[pause 2s]

## Display

Last, Amara opens the display tab, called display and accessibility. Small print tires her eyes, so she presses the bigger A twice. The text size reads one hundred and twenty percent, and the words grow straight away. Under the buttons, the page says, changes preview instantly across the app.

Below that is a theme control with three options. System, which follows her device, light, and dark. She chooses dark, and the whole screen changes at once. There are also switches for high contrast, reduce motion, and underline links, and a reading font option.

Amara presses save settings and sees a confirmation. After a reload, the text size is still one hundred and twenty percent, because it is stored with her account and not only in one browser tab.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Profile holds the name, email, pronouns, photo and password. Notifications are chosen per event, with separate in-app and email boxes, and they save the moment they are changed. Display settings preview live and are kept once saved. Every person manages only their own settings.
