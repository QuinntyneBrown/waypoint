# 31 · Configure organization

## Welcome

Every program has a name, a place, a contact, and rules about guardian permission. This video follows Pat Morgan, a program administrator, as Pat sets those organization-wide settings once, and then follows one of them to the guardian who reads it.

[pause 2s]

## Organization identity

Pat opens Organization settings. The first panel is called organization identity. A note under the heading says that the platform colour palette remains fixed for accessibility. So the organization can set who it is, but not how the screens are coloured.

The panel has five fields. Organization name, display name, logo address, city, and contact email. Pat enters the full name, a shorter display name, the city, and the contact email. A note under the email explains that it is shown to guardians on consent forms and emailed records, so it should be an address someone actually reads.

[pause 2s]

## The consent policy

Beside it is the guardian consent policy. It says that these settings drive term access and youth media safeguards. There are three controls. The consent statement is the wording a guardian reads before giving permission. Pat writes, I give my child permission to take part in the Waypoint program this term.

Require re-consent each term means a prior-term record will not unlock participation once a new active term begins. Require media consent for youth media means photos and video require a current guardian permission. Pat ticks both.

[pause 2s]

## Saving

Pat presses save organization settings. A green message appears. Organization settings saved and recorded in the audit log. Every change to the settings is written to the audit log, and only a program administrator can make one. The server checks that on every request, not just the screen.

[pause 2s]

## Terms and tracks

Lower on the same page is the panel called terms and cohort cycles. Pat enters a term name, a start date, an end date, and a status of upcoming, active, or completed, and presses create term. The term appears in the list with its dates and a badge showing its status.

The panel below it, track availability, lists every published track with a pause button. Pausing a track keeps all existing progress, and only blocks new enrolment and new work assignment.

[pause 2s]

## What the guardian sees

Now meet Ruth Clarke, a guardian. Her child has asked Waypoint for consent, and Ruth opens the secure link from her email. The page is headed with the display name Pat chose, Waypoint guardian consent. Under the heading, the participation statement appears exactly as Pat wrote it. Beneath it is a line that begins, questions, followed by the contact email Pat entered.

So Ruth sees the organization's own words, and she knows where to send a question, without anyone editing the form. If Pat changes the policy next term, the next form reflects it.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Identity, consent policy, terms, and track availability all live on one page. Saving is recorded in the audit log, and only a program administrator can do it. Pausing a track never loses progress. Guardians see the statement and the contact email on their consent form.
