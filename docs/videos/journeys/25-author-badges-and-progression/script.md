# 25 · Author badges and progression

## Welcome

Waypoint measures growth, not only finished lessons. This video follows Pat Morgan, a program administrator, as Pat sets up the two things that make growth visible. The progression framework is the map of stages and skills. Badges are the recognition a young person can earn along the way.

[pause 2s]

## Stages and competencies

Pat opens the progression framework page, which keeps growth stages in order, and presses add stage. A small form asks for a stage name and a description. Pat types, Explorer, and describes it as beginning the pathway and learning the habits. Pat adds a second stage, servant leader.

A competency is one skill or habit that belongs to a stage. On the Explorer stage, Pat presses add competency and types, reads Scripture daily. Then Pat adds, prays with a partner. On the servant leader stage, Pat adds, leads a small group.

[pause 2s]

## Changing the order

Stages can move. Each stage has a button to move it up and a button to move it down. Pat moves servant leader up, and a green message says, framework order saved. Waypoint uses this framework to work out each youth's current stage, and it feeds the competency assessments that mentors complete. So a careful framework here makes everything downstream clearer.

[pause 2s]

## An automatic badge

Now for badges. Pat opens the badge builder, and fills in a form called new badge definition. The name is foundations finisher, the icon is award, and the description is, earned by finishing a whole module. The criteria kind is automatic.

Once the kind is automatic, two more choices appear. The automatic rule offers module completed, lesson completed, quiz passed, assignment approved, and a five session attendance streak. Pat chooses module completed, and picks which module it applies to. Pat can also map the badge to a competency from the framework, so the two work together.

Pat presses save badge, and a card appears, showing the rule and an award count of zero. From now on, when a youth meets that rule, Waypoint gives them the badge on its own, exactly once, and sends a notification.

[pause 2s]

## A manual badge

Not every kind of recognition can be measured. Pat makes a second badge, servant heart, and chooses manual. The rule choices disappear, because a manual badge is given by a person. Its card has a button called award badge.

Pat presses it, chooses Amara Bell as the youth recipient, and presses confirm award. A message says the badge was awarded to Amara Bell, and the count on the card goes from zero to one. Waypoint records who gave the badge and when, and writes it to the audit log.

A badge can also be retired. An archived badge stops earning, but youth who already earned it keep it.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

A stage has a name, a description, and a place in the order, and competencies belong to a stage. An automatic badge follows one of five rules. A manual badge is awarded by a person, and the award is recorded. Archiving never takes a badge from a youth who earned it. Only a program administrator edits any of this.
