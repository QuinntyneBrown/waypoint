# 13 · Author quizzes and assignments

## Welcome

Lessons teach. Quizzes check that a lesson landed, and assignments let a young person show what they learned. This video follows Pat Morgan, a program administrator, as Pat builds one quiz and one assignment in the curriculum builder, including the rules that govern them.

[pause 2s]

## Setting the quiz rules

Pat opens the curriculum builder, finds a module, and presses add quiz. The first form is about rules, not questions. Pass mark is the score a youth needs, and Pat enters eighty. Attempts allowed is how many tries a youth gets, and Pat enters three.

Feedback mode decides what a youth sees afterward. Pat picks after all attempts. Two boxes follow. Shuffle questions mixes the order for each youth. Required for module completion means the module is not finished until the quiz is passed. Pat ticks both and presses create quiz.

[pause 2s]

## Adding questions

A quiz editor opens, with a form for questions. Pat types the question text, what did Jesus say he came to do. Question types are multiple choice, true or false, multiple answer, and short answer. Pat chooses multiple choice.

The answer options go in one per line. Pat lists, to be served, to serve, and to be admired, and marks the correct one by number. Pat enters two, and presses add question. The correct answer is always set by the author, and when a youth takes the quiz, the score uses exactly the answers and pass mark that Pat saved.

[pause 2s]

## Building the assignment

Now the assignment. Pat presses add assignment in the same module. Two things are required. The first is a title, serving others. The second is a task description, which tells the youth exactly what to do. Pat writes, describe one concrete way you served someone this week.

A group of boxes called allowed submission types lets a youth hand in a text response, a photo, a link, or a file. Pat ticks text response and link. At least one type must be chosen, so nobody can create an assignment that cannot be answered.

[pause 2s]

## Rubric and due rules

Below that is the rubric, a list of criteria that each carry a point value. Pat adds, specific and reflective, for five points, and connects to Scripture, for three. Waypoint adds up the total itself.

The same form holds the due rules. Pat sets a due date and a grace period of two days. Allow late submissions lets work come in after the due date. Require mentor approval means the assignment is not complete until the mentor approves it.

[pause 2s]

## Publishing

When Pat presses create assignment, it appears in the module as a draft, showing its total of eight points. Then Pat presses publish assignment. A green message says, assignment published and assigned to enrolled youth. A draft is hidden from every youth, until it is published.

Only a program administrator can edit the curriculum.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

A quiz has rules for pass mark, attempts, feedback, shuffling, and whether it is required. Every correct answer is set by the author. An assignment needs a title, a task description, and at least one way to submit. Rubric points are totalled for you. Drafts stay hidden until they are published.
