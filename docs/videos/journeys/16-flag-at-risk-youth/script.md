# 16 · Flag at-risk youth

## Welcome

Some young people quietly drift away. They stop logging in, they miss a session, or they fall behind their cohort. Waypoint watches for these signals, so a mentor can step in early. This video follows Grace Adeyemi as she works with the at-risk list, for David Osei.

[pause 2s]

## The at-risk list

Grace opens At-risk. The page lists every active flag for her youth, and shows a count. David Osei is on the list. His card gives the reasons in plain words, such as missed one session. It also says who flagged him, and when.

A youth can land here in two ways. Waypoint raises a flag by itself when a signal is met, such as inactivity, a missed session, or progress well below the cohort. Or the mentor raises one by hand.

[pause 2s]

## Responding

Each card gives Grace four ways to respond. She can view the youth's record. She can log a check-in. She can send a message. Or she can unflag.

She chooses Message. A box opens right on the card, with David's name on it. Grace writes, checking in after your missed session, and sends it. A line at the top confirms that the message was sent, and David receives it in his thread.

[pause 2s]

## Resolving a flag

Later, David is back on track. Grace presses Unflag. Waypoint does not just remove the flag. It asks her to confirm, and to give a resolution reason. The reasons are Reconnected, Handed to director, Left program, and Flagged in error. Grace picks Reconnected, and confirms.

A message says the flag is resolved, and David leaves the list. With nothing else flagged, the page says there are no active flags.

[pause 2s]

## Raising a flag by hand

Sometimes a concern is not caught by any automatic signal. At the top of the page, Grace picks a youth, writes a reason, and presses Raise flag. Here she writes, safeguarding follow-up after a conversation at the session. A message confirms the flag was raised, and David is back on the list, with her reason on his card. If the signals return after a flag is resolved, the youth can be flagged again.

Raising and resolving a flag are both visible to the program director, and both are written to the audit log.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

The list shows each youth's reasons, and who flagged them and when. Flags come from automatic signals, or from the mentor. From each card the mentor can view the youth, log a check-in, message, or unflag. Unflagging needs a resolution reason. The director can see every flag, and every change is audited.
