# 01 · Complete lessons

## Welcome

This is the heart of Waypoint. A young person opens their curriculum, works through lessons in order, and sees what opens up next. This video follows Amara Bell through one lesson from start to finish, including the parts that are locked, and a coding lesson.

[pause 2s]

## Finding her place

Amara chooses Curriculum from the side menu. The page says, follow your modules in order and see what unlocks next. At the top are her tracks, and below them the modules of the track she picked.

The first module, Foundations, shows thirty three percent. Inside it, the lessons are listed in order, each with a small status. Her coding lesson says done. Living the Word says not started.

[pause 2s]

## What is locked

Under the lessons sits the module quiz, and it is locked. It tells her why. Complete all lessons in this module. The next module, Deeper Practice, is locked too, and a note says to complete Foundations first.

So Amara never has to guess. The rule is on the screen, and the server enforces the same order, so a locked item cannot be reached by another route.

[pause 2s]

## Reading the lesson

Amara opens Living the Word. The page says this is step two of three, and that it takes fifteen minutes. She reads the scripture, a short section on what she will learn, and the teaching. Above it are text size buttons and a bookmark button, and Waypoint remembers her choices.

Notes written for leaders are never shown to a youth.

[pause 2s]

## Marking it complete

At the bottom she presses, mark lesson complete. Waypoint asks her to confirm, and then records the lesson as complete.

She lands on a reflection page. It asks, where can you practise neighbour love this week? A reflection is a separate step, and it is the subject of another video.

[pause 2s]

## What opens next

Amara goes back to Curriculum. Living the Word now says done, and Foundations has moved up to sixty seven percent. The module quiz is no longer locked. It says not started, which means it is ready for her. Completing the lessons is what opened it.

[pause 2s]

## A coding lesson

Some lessons are for building. In a coding lesson, Amara follows numbered steps, edits a small web page in the code editor, and sees the result at once in a live preview. Her work is saved as she goes.

At the bottom of the page she can send her project to her mentor for review, with an optional note. Her current code becomes the read only copy that goes to the mentor.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

The curriculum shows modules in order, with the reason for anything that is locked. A lesson shows scripture, teaching and a reflection question, and never leader notes. Marking a lesson complete opens what comes next. Coding lessons add a live preview and a way to send the project to a mentor.
