# 109 · Protecting personal information

## Welcome

Waypoint holds information about young people, and about their families. That information belongs to them, and the program holds it in trust. This video shows how Waypoint limits what it keeps, who can see it, and how a person can ask to see, correct or remove it.

[pause 2s]

## What Waypoint keeps

Waypoint keeps only what it needs to run the program. Access is limited to staff and to the mentor assigned to a young person. The more sensitive fields are protected where they are stored, so they are not sitting in plain sight in the database. And when personal details are written to the system's logs, secrets and sensitive information are meant to be left out.

Amara Bell can see for herself what is held. On her privacy and data rights page, a card called your stored account data lists her name, email, role, account status, pronouns, the date she joined, and whether she has a cohort and a mentor on record.

[pause 2s]

## Asking for something

Next to it, there is a form, make a data-rights request. Amara chooses a request type, for example, access a copy of my data. She adds a few details and presses submit request. A green message appears. It says, request received, keep reference, followed by a reference number. Her request can be tracked, and the action is recorded.

A note on the page explains that removal and consent requests may need follow-up, so that safeguarding and legal records are handled correctly.

The button to download her complete data export starts out greyed out. It turns on only after staff have fulfilled and verified an access request. That way, nobody can pull a full copy of someone's data without a check.

[pause 2s]

## The staff side

Pat Morgan, a program administrator, opens the privacy requests page. Each request shows who asked, the type, the reference, the time it was made, and its status. Pat reviews a request, and fulfils it once the right checks are done. The page asks staff to apply only verified corrections. Removal and consent withdrawal are completed on the server, in a way that keeps the records the program is required to keep.

The design also expects old data to be removable or anonymised once it is no longer needed, while keeping reports that cannot identify anyone.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Waypoint keeps only what it needs, shows people what it holds, and limits who can see it. A person can ask for access, correction, removal or withdrawal of consent, and every request has a reference. Staff verify before they fulfil. Exports are unlocked only after that check.
