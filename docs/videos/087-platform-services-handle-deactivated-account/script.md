# 087 · When an account is deactivated

## Welcome

Sometimes a program has to switch off a person's access, for example when someone leaves. Waypoint calls that deactivating an account. This video shows what the person sees when they try to sign in afterwards, and where Waypoint sends them.

[pause 2s]

## A deactivated account

Meet David. His account was deactivated by Pat, the program administrator. Pat does this from the people area of the portal, with the deactivate account button on the person's page.

Some time later, David goes to the sign-in page, types his email and his password, and presses sign in. The details are right, but the sign-in is refused.

[pause 2s]

## What David sees

Waypoint does not show David a confusing error. It takes him to a page of its own. The small heading says, account inactive. The title says, this account is inactive. And the message explains. Your account is not active right now. Please contact your program director to restore access.

[pause 2s]

## Ways forward

The page offers two buttons. One says, contact the director. It opens the contact page of the public site, so David can reach the person who can restore his access. The other says, back to sign in, in case he typed the wrong account.

Notice what the page does not do. It does not let him in, and it does not show him anything from his account. The sign-in is refused, and the way back is through a person.

Pat can also switch an account back on. After that, David can sign in as usual.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

A deactivated account is refused at sign-in, even with the right password. The person sees a clear page that says the account is inactive. That page points to the director, through the contact the director button. Access comes back only when the program restores the account.
