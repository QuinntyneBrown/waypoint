# 084 · Making Waypoint easier to read

## Welcome

People read screens in different ways. Some need larger text. Some want stronger contrast, or fewer moving things. Waypoint has a display settings page for this. This video shows how it works, and how a choice follows the person around the app.

[pause 2s]

## The display page

Meet Amara. She finds small print hard to read at the end of a long day. She opens her settings and chooses the display tab. The page is called, display and accessibility.

At the top is text size. It shows the current size as a percentage, with two buttons, a smaller A and a bigger A. Below it are four switches. High contrast, for stronger borders and text. Reduce motion, to minimise animations and transitions. Underline links, to always underline links for clarity. And reading font, which uses a serif font for body text.

[pause 2s]

## A live preview

Amara presses the bigger A twice. The number reads one hundred and twenty percent, and the words grow at once. The page says so right under the buttons. Changes preview instantly across the app.

She then switches on high contrast and underline links. The borders and the text become stronger, and every link gets its underline. She does not have to guess what a setting will look like. She sees it right now, before she commits to it.

[pause 2s]

## Saving and keeping it

The choice becomes hers when she presses, save settings. A short message appears. Display settings saved.

The choice is stored with her account on the server. So when Amara reloads the page, the text is still at one hundred and twenty percent, and the high contrast is still on. It is not only a setting in one browser tab.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Display settings cover text size, high contrast, reduce motion, underline links, and reading font. Every change previews live across the app. A change is kept after the person presses save settings, and it comes back after a reload. The goal is simple. Each person can make Waypoint comfortable for their own eyes.
