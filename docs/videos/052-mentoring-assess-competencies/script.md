# 052 · Assessing competencies

## Welcome

Waypoint tracks more than lessons and quizzes. It also tracks the qualities a young person is growing into, called competencies. Only a mentor can say how far along a young person is. This video shows how a mentor records that, and where it shows up.

[pause 2s]

## The competency tracker

Grace is Amara's mentor. From Amara's record she opens the competencies page. At the top there is a summary. It shows how many competencies are at proficient or mastered, how many competencies are in the framework, and Amara's current stage.

Below the summary, every competency has its own card. The card has a level, and a box for notes.

[pause 2s]

## Setting a level

A level has four steps. Not started, Developing, Proficient, and Mastered. Grace picks the competency called Community initiative, and sets the level to Proficient. Then she writes a note, in her own words. Took initiative welcoming a new participant.

She presses Save assessment. A message confirms that the competency was saved. The card now shows who made the assessment and when. Waypoint keeps the mentor's name and the time with every saved level, so everyone can see who made the call.

[pause 2s]

## The summary updates

Grace scrolls back to the top. The count of proficient or mastered competencies has gone up by one. The summary and the cards always agree, because the summary is worked out from the saved assessments.

And the result does not stay on the mentor's screen. When Amara looks at her own progress, or when a report shows competencies, it uses the latest assessment. If Grace changes the level next month, the new level is what everyone sees.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

Mentors set a level for each competency, from Not started to Mastered, and add notes. Each saved assessment keeps the mentor and the time. The summary count updates as soon as an assessment is saved. Amara's progress view and reports always show the latest assessment.
