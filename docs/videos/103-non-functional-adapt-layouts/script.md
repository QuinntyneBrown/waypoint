# 103 · Adapting to every screen size

## Welcome

Young people often use Waypoint on a phone. Staff often use it on a laptop or a large screen. Waypoint is built to be usable on all of them, from the smallest phone to a wide desktop monitor. This video shows what changes as the screen gets smaller, and what stays the same.

[pause 2s]

## On a phone

Amara Bell opens Waypoint on her phone. The page is a single column. Everything is stacked, one thing under the next, so she scrolls down and never has to scroll sideways. The page is never wider than her screen, so no button sits out of reach.

At the bottom, a bar holds the main places she goes, home, my journey, curriculum, messages and profile. On a laptop, those same places sit in a menu down the left side. Same places, different arrangement, chosen to fit the screen.

[pause 2s]

## On a laptop

Here is the same home page on a wide screen. There is room to put things side by side. The next step sits next to a Bible verse, and the progress and schedule cards share a row. The content is the same as on the phone. Only the arrangement has changed.

[pause 2s]

## Staff pages and wide tables

Pat Morgan is a program administrator, and staff pages hold more information. On a laptop, the dashboard spreads its numbers across several columns. On a phone, the same numbers stack into one column, each in its own card, so they stay easy to read.

Tables need extra care, because a table with many columns does not fit on a phone. In the people directory, each person becomes a small card, and every line is labelled. Name, email, role, cohort. Nobody has to guess which number belongs to which column.

Some views are wide by nature, like the progress board, where every youth is compared across every module. On a small screen, a wide view like that scrolls sideways inside its own box. The page around it stays still.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

On a phone, pages are one column and nothing forces a sideways scroll. The main places move to a bar at the bottom. On wider screens, content spreads into columns. Tables turn into labelled cards on a phone, and wide views scroll inside their own box.
