# 001 · Signing in to Waypoint

## Welcome

This is Waypoint. Before anyone can see a lesson, a cohort, or a young person's progress, they sign in. This short video shows what that looks like, and how Waypoint keeps the door safe.

[pause 2s]

## Signing in

Meet Amara. She is eighteen, and she has just joined the Fall cohort. She opens Waypoint and sees a simple page. It says, welcome back. Sign in to continue your journey.

She types her email and her password, and presses sign in. Waypoint checks who she is, and takes her straight to her own home page. Amara is a youth, so she lands on the youth home. Grace, her mentor, would land on the mentor home. Each person goes to the place made for their role.

Every sign-in is written to the audit log, so the program always knows who came in, and when.

[pause 2s]

## Keep me signed in

Under the password there is a box that says, keep me signed in. If Amara leaves it empty, closing the browser ends her session. If she ticks it, Waypoint remembers her for longer. Right beside the box, it warns her. Not recommended on shared or public devices. That matters in a youth program, where a lot of sign-ins happen on a shared computer.

[pause 2s]

## When something goes wrong

Now suppose someone types a wrong password, or an email Waypoint has never seen. The page gives the same plain message for both. It says, invalid email or password. That way, nobody can use the sign-in page to find out who is in the program.

If someone keeps guessing the wrong password, Waypoint counts every miss. After five, the account locks, and the page says so. The lock lasts fifteen minutes, and then Amara can simply try again. While it lasts, two ways back in stay open. She can reset her password, or she can contact her mentor.

Behind the scenes, the sign-in page also slows down anyone who tries again and again, so the door cannot be broken open by guessing.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

One page, one email, one password, and everyone lands in the right place. A wrong guess reveals nothing. Repeated guesses lock the account for a while, and shared computers come with a warning.
