# 011 · Seeing only what your role allows

## Welcome

Waypoint holds sensitive information about young people. So each person sees only the data that belongs to their role, and that rule is enforced on the server, not just on the screen. This video walks through four people, and shows what each of them sees.

[pause 2s]

## A mentor

Grace is a mentor. When she signs in she lands on her mentor dashboard. It shows her youth, her pending reviews, and her at risk count. These are the youth assigned to her, and only them. A mentor does not see a roster of every young person in the program, and asking for a youth who is not assigned to her is refused, even if she knew that youth's identification number.

[pause 2s]

## A youth

A youth sees only their own records. Imagine David, who is a youth, tries to open a mentor page by typing its address. He sees a page that says, you do not have access to that. It explains that the area is for a different role, and offers a button back to his own home, or a way to switch account. Hiding a button is never enough, so the server checks the role on every request, and the attempt is logged.

[pause 2s]

## A guardian

Ruth is a guardian. Her home page shows her own child at a glance: overall progress, attendance, and badges earned, with what is coming next. She sees a summary only. She never sees lesson content, quiz answers, private reflections, or the child's account details.

[pause 2s]

## A volunteer

Sam is a volunteer. His page is called Today's session. It shows the one session he is assigned to, with its place and time, and a button to take attendance. The attendance list shows names only, with present or absent. Sam does not see at risk flags, hours, progress, grades, records, or messages.

[pause 2s]

## Program administrators

Pat is a program administrator. Pat can see every cohort. Even so, the private notes inside a mentor's check-in stay restricted.

[pause 2s]

## Things to remember

A mentor sees assigned youth. A youth sees their own records. A guardian sees a summary of their own child. A volunteer sees one session and a names only roster. An administrator sees all cohorts, but not private check-in notes. The server decides, every time.
