Mentor / 08

Review and grade submitted work

Reviewing is the mentor's core job in Waypoint. This page takes you from the dashboard tile that tells you work is waiting, through scoring an assignment against its rubric, to the moment a young person can finally see a quiz score. It also covers the two checks that will stop you, and what to do when someone else has changed the review while you had it open.

Mentor W-UG-08

What review work is, and where it comes from

Waypoint gives you two kinds of thing to assess, and they behave differently.

A submission

An assignment or a coding project that a young person has sent in. You score it against a rubric — a fixed list of criteria, each worth a set number of points — write feedback, and then either approve it or send it back for revision. Nothing is scored automatically.

A quiz assessment

A quiz attempt that Waypoint has already marked. The score exists, but it is invisible to the young person until you approve it. Your job is to look at the answers, accept the auto-scored result or override it, and release it.

You only ever see your own youth. Every review screen is scoped to the young people assigned to you. If you reach a review for someone who is not on your caseload, the request is refused with This youth is not assigned to you.

Reading the dashboard summary

Dashboard is the first item in your sidebar and the page you land on when you sign in. Its eyebrow reads Mentor dashboard and its heading greets you by name, above the line See how your assigned youth are doing and what needs review.

Four tiles sit across the top.

Tile What the number counts
My youth The young people currently assigned to you. This is your whole caseload, not just those with work outstanding.
Pending reviews Everything waiting on you — submissions and quiz assessments together. Underneath it, a second line reads 0 overdue, with the real count in place of the zero. Any number above zero is the one to act on first.
Average progress The mean curriculum progress across your caseload, shown as a whole percentage.
At risk How many of your youth currently carry an active at-risk flag. Flags are covered in Keep track of your youth.

Below the tiles, My youth previews up to four young people with a progress ring each, and Reviews queue previews the five oldest items waiting. Open queue beside that heading takes you to the full queue, which is where the rest of this page happens.

What you should see

With nothing waiting, the preview shows No reviews waiting and the line New submissions and quiz assessments will appear here. With no caseload at all, the youth preview shows No youth assigned yet and Assigned youth will appear here when your caseload is ready.

Working the review queue

Choose Reviews in the sidebar. The page carries the eyebrow Assessment, the heading Reviews, and the line Review pending assignments, coding projects, and quizzes. While the list is loading you will briefly see Loading reviews…

  1. Open the queue Mentor

    Choose Reviews in the sidebar, or Open queue on the dashboard.

    What you should see

    One row per waiting item. If nothing is waiting, the page shows No reviews waiting with the line You are caught up with your assigned youth.

  2. Read a row

    Each row names the young person, then the kind and title of the item on one line — for example Quiz · Who is my neighbour? — then Submitted with the date and time it arrived.

  3. Check the due badge

    Every row carries exactly one badge, and it decides your order of work.

    Badge Meaning
    Overdue The due date has passed. Shown in the error colour, so it stands out from the rest of the list.
    Due 4 Mar 2026 There is a due date and it has not passed yet. The real date replaces the example.
    No due date The work was assigned without a due date. It will never become overdue, so it is on you to pick it up.
  4. Open the item

    Choose Review at the end of the row. A submission opens the submission review screen; a quiz opens the quiz assessment screen. Both carry a Back to reviews link at the top left.

Preview limitation

Status text that should only be announced to screen readers is visible as ordinary text on this screen and on both review screens. You may see a bare sentence such as Quiz assessment approved. sitting near the top of the page after you act. It is not an error and nothing is broken — read it as the confirmation it is, and carry on.

Reviewing a submission

The submission screen splits in two. The left side, Submitted work, is what the young person sent. The right side, Rubric and outcome, is where you work.

1Heading block Reads Assignment or Coding project, then the youth's name, the assignment title, and Submitted with the date.
2Submitted work The task description, then whatever was submitted: written text, an image, a link, a downloadable file, and any Youth note.
3Rubric and outcome One score box per criterion, a running total, the feedback box, the guardian checkbox, and the three action buttons.

Read what was submitted

Submitted work always starts with the task description, so you can grade against what was actually asked. Below it, only the parts the young person supplied appear:

  • Typed work, shown with its own line breaks preserved.
  • An uploaded photo, labelled for screen readers as Evidence submitted by the youth.
  • A link, as Open submitted link. It opens in a new tab.
  • A file, as Download followed by the file name. While the file is still being fetched you will see Preparing and the file name instead — wait for the link to appear rather than reloading.
  • A closing note from the young person, prefixed Youth note:

Score against the rubric

Each criterion is one labelled number box. The label is the criterion's own wording followed by its range in brackets — the pattern is Explains the passage in their own words (0–5), where the description and the maximum come from the assignment. The box will not accept more than the maximum.

Directly beneath the last criterion, a running total shows your awarded points over the total possible, as 12 / 20. It updates as you type, so you can see where a submission is landing before you commit to anything.

Write the feedback and decide about the guardian

Feedback for the youth is a free-text box holding up to 4,000 characters. Whatever you write here is what the young person reads, so write it to them, not about them.

Below it sits the checkbox Notify guardian about the outcome. It starts ticked. Leave it ticked and the linked guardian is told the outcome along with the young person; clear it and only the young person is told. It has no effect at all when you save a draft, because a draft notifies nobody.

Choose an outcome

Three buttons sit at the foot of the card. They are, left to right, Save draft, Request revision and Approve.

  1. Save draft

    Stores your scores, feedback and guardian choice without telling anyone. There is no confirmation dialog — it saves straight away.

    What you should see

    You stay on the review, the screen reloads with your work in place, and the status line reads Draft saved. The youth-visible status has not changed.

  2. Request revision

    Sends the work back for another attempt. Waypoint asks Request a revision and notify the youth? Choose OK to go ahead, or Cancel to stay on the review with everything intact.

    What you should see

    After you confirm, you are returned to the queue. The submission moves to NeedsRevision and leaves your queue until the young person resubmits.

  3. Approve

    Accepts the work at the score you gave. Waypoint asks Approve this submission and notify the youth? Choose OK to go ahead, or Cancel to stay.

    What you should see

    After you confirm, you are returned to the queue and the submission moves to Approved. Approval is what can trigger a badge, so this is the moment the young person's progress moves.

Both decisions notify the young person. That is what the confirmation dialogs are telling you, and it is why they exist. Only Save draft is silent.

The two checks that will stop you

Waypoint refuses two specific combinations before it sends anything. Both appear as a red message at the top of the screen, and neither one loses your work.

Feedback is required when requesting a revision.

You chose Request revision with the feedback box empty or containing only spaces. Sending work back without saying why is not allowed. Write what needs to change, then choose Request revision again. The confirmation dialog does not appear until the check passes.

Score every rubric criterion before approving.

You chose Approve with at least one score box left blank. A zero is a score; an empty box is not. Fill every box — entering 0 where the work earned nothing — and choose Approve again.

Neither check applies to Save draft. You can save a half-scored draft with no feedback at any point and come back to it.

What a draft does and does not change

This is worth being precise about, because it is the difference between thinking out loud and telling a young person something.

Drafts are stored on the server, not in your browser, so a draft you save on one device is there when you open the same review on another.

Leaving a review with unsaved grading

Waypoint tracks whether the review on screen differs from what was last saved. If it does and you navigate away — a sidebar link, the back link, anything — it stops you first with a browser confirmation: 

You have unsaved grading changes. Save your grading draft before leaving? Select OK to save the draft, or Cancel to stay.

Choose OK and Waypoint saves a draft for you and then lets you leave. Choose Cancel and you stay exactly where you were. Closing the browser tab or reloading the page raises the browser's own generic "leave site?" prompt instead — the wording there comes from your browser, not from Waypoint.

If the draft save fails while you are trying to leave, you stay on the review and the failure is shown at the top of the screen. Deal with it before navigating away.

When the review changed while you had it open

Two people can open the same review. A program administrator can also grade. If the copy on the server has moved on since you loaded the page, your save is rejected rather than silently overwriting the other version, and an amber callout appears at the top of the screen:

This review changed on the server. Your scores and feedback are still here. Load the latest review to reconcile.

Read the middle sentence literally: nothing you typed has been thrown away, and it is still on screen. Beneath the message sits a single button, Load latest review.

  1. Copy anything you must keep

    Loading the latest review replaces what is on screen with the server's version. If your feedback wording matters, copy it somewhere first.

  2. Choose Load latest review

    The screen reloads with the current scores, feedback and guardian setting, and the amber callout disappears.

  3. Reconcile, then decide again

    Compare what you had against what is now there, merge the two by hand, and choose your outcome. Your save will now be accepted.

Nothing forces you to press the button. You can keep working on the stale copy — but every save attempt will be rejected the same way until you reload, so there is no point in delaying.

Approving a quiz score

A youth sees no quiz score until you approve it

Waypoint marks a quiz the moment it is submitted, but that result is held back. Until a mentor approves it, the young person sees that the attempt was recorded and nothing more — no percentage, no pass or fail. A quiz sitting unapproved in your queue is a young person waiting to find out how they did.

Opening a quiz from the queue gives you the eyebrow Quiz assessment followed by the young person's name, the quiz title as the heading, and Attempt with its number and the submission time. A badge on the right shows the attempt's current status.

Read the answers

The left column, Submitted answers, lists every question as its own card headed Question 1, Question 2 and so on, each carrying Correct or Incorrect. Inside a card, every option is listed, and the relevant ones are tagged Youth answer and Correct answer — an option can carry both, which is how you spot the ones they got right.

Some attempts have no stored question detail. Those show No question detail available with the line The auto-scored result can still be reviewed. You can still approve; you simply have less to look at.

Approve or override the score

The right-hand card is Score approval. It shows Auto-scored as a large percentage with a badge reading either Pass or Below pass mark, and beside it the Pass mark the quiz is set to.

  1. Accept the auto-score, or type an override

    Override score (optional) is a number box under those figures, with the help text Leave blank to approve the auto-score percentage. Leaving it empty is the normal path. Type a whole number from 0 to 100 only when your reading of the answers differs from the automatic marking.

    What you should see

    Anything that is not a whole number in that range is refused with Override score must be a whole number between 0 and 100. before anything is sent.

  2. Choose Approve score

    Waypoint asks Approve 80% as the score this youth will see? with the score you are actually releasing in place of the example — your override if you typed one, the auto-score if you did not. Read the number in the dialog before choosing OK; it is the last chance to catch a typo.

    What you should see

    The screen reloads, the status line reads Quiz assessment approved., and the override box is replaced by an Approval record panel showing Auto-scored and Approved percentages side by side with your name and the time.

Once that record exists the score is released and cannot be re-approved from this screen — the box and button are gone. The young person now sees the approved percentage, and it is the approved figure, not the auto-scored one, that they and their guardian see everywhere else.

Approving every waiting quiz at once

When your queue contains quizzes, a Approve all quiz scores button appears at the top right of the Reviews page with the count in brackets, as Approve all quiz scores (7). It is hidden entirely when no quizzes are waiting.

This releases every score unread

The bulk action approves each waiting quiz at its auto-scored result. You do not see the answers, and you cannot override any of them afterwards from the quiz screen. Use it when you have already satisfied yourself the marking is right — not as a way of clearing a backlog you have not looked at.

Choosing the button asks you to confirm, naming the count and what will happen: 

Approve all 7 pending quiz assessments at the auto-scored result?

With exactly one waiting, the wording reads quiz assessment in the singular. Choose OK and the queue reloads with those items gone; the status line reports how many were approved. Choose Cancel and nothing happens at all.

The count comes from the queue Waypoint has loaded, which holds up to 100 items. If you have more waiting than that, run the bulk action, then run it again on what is left.

Where your review shows up afterwards

Grading is not the end of the trail. What you did appears in several other places, and knowing where saves you re-explaining it.

Where What is visible there
The young person's view The outcome, your feedback, and — for a quiz — the approved percentage. Not your draft, and not a score you have not approved.
The youth record Under Submissions, each submission with its current status, linking straight back to the review. See Keep track of your youth.
The guardian's view Approved quiz outcomes, and — if you left the checkbox ticked — notification of a submission outcome. Guardians never see quiz answers. See Give consent and follow progress.
The audit log An entry for the decision, visible to program administrators. See Lifecycles and the evidence they leave.
