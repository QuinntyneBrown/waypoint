Keep track of your youth

Grading tells you how the work is going. This page is about the young people behind it: finding them on your roster, reading their record, writing up a conversation, deciding whether that write-up stays private, raising and clearing an at-risk flag, recording competency levels, and putting the next piece of work in front of them.

Mentor W-UG-09

Your caseload, and where it ends

Everything on this page is scoped to the young people assigned to you. That scope is not a filter you can widen — it is enforced on every request, and Waypoint refuses anything outside it with This youth is not assigned to you.

The same rule applies whichever way you arrive:

  • Opening a youth record by pasting or guessing an address.
  • Logging a check-in against someone else's youth.
  • Raising or clearing a flag on someone else's youth.
  • Setting a competency level for someone else's youth.
  • Assigning work to a list that includes someone else's youth — which is refused as One or more recipients are not assigned to you.

If you believe a young person should be on your caseload and is not, a program administrator has to reassign them. There is nothing you can do from a mentor account to change it. See Roles and permissions for who can do what.

The roster: My youth

Choose My Youth in the sidebar. The page carries the eyebrow Your caseload, the heading My youth, and the line Search and filter youth assigned to you. While it loads you will see Loading your roster…

Narrowing the list

Two controls sit above the table. On the left, Search youth is a search box showing Search by name… until you type in it. It matches on name only, and it filters as you type after a short pause — you do not press anything to run it.

On the right sits a row of four filter pills. Exactly one is active at a time.

Pill Shows
All Your whole caseload. This is the starting state every time you open the page.
On track Youth without an active at-risk flag.
At risk Youth carrying an active at-risk flag, whether it was raised automatically or by a person.
New Recently added youth. These also carry a New badge beside their name in the table.

Search and pill combine. Searching for a name while At risk is active shows only matching youth who are also flagged, which is easy to mistake for someone having vanished.

What you should see

With no matches, the table is replaced by No youth match that filter and the line Try another status or clear your search to see your roster. A Clear filters button underneath resets the search box and returns the pill to All in one go.

Reading the table

The table has five columns.

Column What it holds
Youth The name, as a link into their record, plus a New badge where it applies.
Cohort Their cohort, or Not assigned if they are not in one yet.
Progress A progress bar and the same figure as a whole percentage.
Last active The date they were last active. This is the figure the inactivity signal watches.
Status Their status word, shown in the error colour when they are at risk.

The youth record

Choosing a name opens that young person's record. Above the heading sits Back to My youth; the eyebrow is their cohort, or Cohort not assigned; the heading is their name; and beneath it the line Mentor-only coaching record — which is the plain statement that this screen is for you, not for them.

If they are currently flagged, a red banner sits directly under the heading, reading At risk: followed by the reason, and then who flagged them and when.

The six tabs

A strip of tabs runs across the record, labelled Youth record sections for assistive technology. One panel shows at a time, and Profile is selected when you arrive.

Profile

Two panels side by side. Profile gives Email, Pronouns and Cohort, showing Not provided or Not assigned where there is nothing. Guardian contact gives the guardian's Name, Email, Phone and Emergency contact — or the single line No guardian contact is linked. Note that Profile is one tab holding two panels, so the record has seven named panels across six tabs.

Progress

Completed lessons out of total lessons, written as 7 of 20 lessons complete, with a progress bar.

Attendance

Each session with its recorded status and date, or No attendance recorded.

Check-ins

A Log or view check-ins button that opens the check-ins page already filtered to this young person, then a short list of past check-ins showing type, whether each is private or shared, and its date. Empty, it reads No check-ins recorded.

Submissions

Each submission as a link straight into its review, with its status and date. Empty, it reads No submissions yet. Reviewing is covered in Review and grade submitted work.

Competencies

An Open competency tracker button and a summary of current levels and notes, or No competency assessments yet.

Preview limitation

The tab strip on this record does not respond to arrow keys. Left and right arrows, Home and End do nothing here. Move between the tabs with Tab and activate one with Enter or Space. Only the youth My progress tabs support arrow keys.

Logging a check-in

A check-in is your written record of a conversation with a young person. Choose Check-ins in the sidebar, or Log or view check-ins from their record — the second route pre-selects them for you, in both the form and the history filter.

The page carries the eyebrow Mentor journal, the heading Check-ins, and the line Record conversations, actions, and planned follow-up. The form, headed Log check-in, is on the left; Check-in history is on the right.

  1. Choose the young person and the date Mentor

    Youth is a dropdown starting at Choose youth, listing only your caseload. If you have exactly one youth assigned, they are chosen for you. Date defaults to today; change it when you are writing up an earlier conversation.

    What you should see

    Saving without choosing anyone is refused with Choose a youth before saving the check-in. Clearing the date is refused with Choose a check-in date.

  2. Pick the type

    Type offers three options: 1:1 for a face-to-face conversation, Call for a phone or video call, and Session for something noted during a group session. The type is what you filter the history by later, so it is worth getting right.

  3. Write the four note fields

    All four are optional, and each holds up to 2,000 characters.

    Engagement / mood

    How they seemed and how engaged they were.

    Goals discussed

    What the two of you agreed they are working towards.

    Prayer requests

    What they asked to be prayed for. The field carries its own help text: Private unless you explicitly enable sharing below. Read the next section before you rely on that.

    Action items

    What either of you agreed to do before next time.

  4. Set the next follow-up

    Next follow-up is a date. It is a note to yourself about when to come back to this young person.

  5. Record the guardian contact

    Guardian was informed is a separate checkbox at the foot of the form. It records that you told the guardian; it does not send them anything and it does not share the check-in with anybody. Each saved check-in reports its state as either Guardian was informed. or Guardian was not marked informed.

  6. Save

    Choose Save check-in.

    What you should see

    The status line reads Check-in saved., the note fields and both checkboxes clear ready for the next entry, and the history on the right reloads filtered to the young person you just wrote about.

Reading the history

Check-in history has two dropdowns above it — Filter by youth, which starts at All youth, and Filter by type, which starts at All types. Each entry is a card headed with the young person's name, then the type and date, with a badge on the right saying whether it is private or shared. Inside, your notes appear under short labels: Engagement:, Goals:, Prayer:, Actions: and Follow-up:, with empty fields simply left out.

What you should see

With no entries matching, the column shows No matching check-ins and Adjust the youth or type filter. While it is fetching, it shows Loading check-in history…

Preview limitation

Screen-reader status text is visible on this page, so a bare sentence such as Check-in saved. appears as ordinary text near the top after you save. The same happens on the at-risk screen. Nothing is wrong; it is a confirmation that should have been announced rather than shown.

The sharing decision, and what it exposes

A check-in is a private mentor note by default. One checkbox changes that: Share this check-in with the youth and guardian. It starts unticked, and it resets to unticked after every save, so sharing is always a fresh decision rather than a setting you can forget you left on.

Tick it and a callout appears immediately underneath:

All entered fields, including prayer requests, will be visible to the youth and their linked guardian.

Take that sentence at face value. There is no per-field sharing. You cannot share the goals and hold back the prayer requests, and you cannot share with the young person but not their guardian. Sharing is all of it, to both of them.

Because prayer requests are frequently the most sensitive thing on the form, the practical advice is simple: write the private record, and if there is something the family should see, share a separate check-in that contains only that. Two entries are cheap. An unintended disclosure is not.

Safeguarding concerns do not belong in a shared note. Use a private check-in and an at-risk flag, and follow your organisation's own escalation route. Everything a mentor writes is visible to program administrators either way.

At-risk flags

Choose At-risk in the sidebar. The page carries the eyebrow Safeguarding follow-up, the heading At-risk youth, and the line Review inactivity, missed-session, and cohort-progress signals, then record your action. While it works, it shows Evaluating at-risk signals…

Flags Waypoint raises for you

Three signals are evaluated automatically. Each has a threshold set for your organisation, and each writes its own reason onto the flag:

  • Inactivity — the young person has not signed in for longer than the threshold. The reason names the days elapsed and the threshold.
  • Missed sessions — they have been marked absent at least as many times as the threshold allows. The reason names the count and the threshold.
  • Progress gap — their progress has fallen further below their cohort's average than the threshold permits. The reason names the gap and the threshold.

A card raised this way carries the eyebrow Automatic signal. Where more than one signal is firing, all of them are listed on the card.

Raising a flag yourself

The card at the top of the page is Raise a manual flag, with the note Use this when a concern is not captured by an automatic signal. It has two fields — Youth, starting at Choose youth, and Reason, up to 1,000 characters — and the button Raise flag.

What you should see

Leaving either field empty is refused with Choose a youth and enter a reason before raising a flag. On success the reason box clears and the list reloads with a new card carrying the eyebrow Mentor-raised concern.

One active flag per young person. If they already have one, raising another is refused with This youth already has an active at-risk flag. Resolve the existing flag first, or add what you have learned as a check-in instead. Re-entering exactly the same reason as the existing flag is treated as a no-op rather than an error.

Acting on a flag

Under Active flags — with a badge counting them — each card shows the young person's name, who flagged them and when, an At risk badge, and the reasons as a list. Four buttons sit underneath.

View youth

Opens their record.

Log check-in

Opens the check-ins page with them already selected.

Message

Opens a message box inside the card, labelled Message followed by their name. Write up to 4,000 characters and choose Send message, or Cancel to close it. An empty message is refused with Enter a message before sending. It goes to their normal mentor message thread.

Unflag

Starts clearing the flag, described next.

Clearing a flag

Choosing Unflag opens a small panel inside the card reading Confirm this flag can be removed. You must pick a Resolution reason from four options, and the reason you choose is recorded permanently.

Reason Use it when
Reconnected You have re-established contact and the concern has eased. This is the default.
Handed to director The concern is now with a program administrator. Clearing the flag does not hand it over on its own — do that separately.
Left program The young person is no longer participating.
Flagged in error The flag should not have been raised. Use this honestly — it is the only reason that says nothing happened.

Choose Confirm unflag to clear it, or Keep flag to back out with the flag untouched. After clearing, the list reloads without that card, and the young person stops matching the At risk pill on your roster.

A resolved automatic signal stays suppressed while the situation that caused it holds, so clearing a flag is durable — it will not simply reappear the next time signals are evaluated. A genuinely new or different signal can raise a fresh flag.

What you should see

With nobody flagged, the page shows No active flags and the line No assigned youth currently need at-risk follow-up.

Recording competency levels

Competencies are the leadership skills your program tracks separately from curriculum progress. Reach them from a youth record with Open competency tracker.

The page carries the eyebrow Mentor assessment, the young person's name followed by competencies as the heading, and the line Record the latest observed level and supporting notes for each competency. Back to youth record returns you.

Three figures sit across the top:

  • Proficient or mastered — how many competencies have reached the top two levels, out of the total.
  • Framework coverage — how many competencies exist in total.
  • Current stage — the stage they are working through and how complete it is, or Not configured if no progression has been set up.

Below that, one card per competency shows its stage, its name, and a badge with the current level. Each card has a level dropdown and a notes box holding up to 2,000 characters, then Save assessment. Under the notes, either Latest: with who last assessed it and when, or Not assessed yet.

Level What it says
Not started Nothing observed yet. Every competency begins here.
Developing Beginning to show it, with support.
Proficient Doing it reliably on their own.
Mastered Doing it well enough to help someone else do it.

Levels move in either direction — nothing stops you recording a drop — and each card saves on its own. Guardians see the current level for each competency on their progress screen, so write the notes accordingly.

What you should see

Where no framework has been configured, the page reads No competencies configured with the line A program administrator can add the progression framework.

Assigning work

Assign work sits under the Program group in your sidebar. The page carries the eyebrow Curriculum, the heading Assign work, and the line Add a published lesson, module, or project to individual journeys or a whole cohort.

Only published content can be assigned. Items still in draft or in review do not appear in the lists here at all. If something you expect is missing, it has not been published yet — ask a program administrator.

  1. Choose what you are assigning

    What are you assigning? offers three kinds: Lesson, Module and Project / assignment. Your choice filters the Item dropdown underneath it. With nothing published of that kind, the help text reads No published followed by the kind and items are available., and the Review & assign button is disabled.

  2. Choose who it is for

    Who is this for? gives two choices. Selected youth lists your caseload as checkboxes, each showing their cohort and an At risk badge where it applies — tick as many as you need. Whole cohort replaces the list with a Cohort dropdown starting at Choose cohort, where each cohort shows its youth count in brackets.

  3. Set the due date and reminder

    Due date is a date. Reminder offers No reminder, 1 day before due and 2 days before due. A reminder is scheduled for each recipient separately.

  4. Add a message, if you want one

    Message to youth shows Add a personal note… until you type, holds up to 4,000 characters, and carries the help text Each recipient receives this in their mentor message thread. It is a real message in the thread they already use, not a system notice.

  5. Check the summary

    The Summary panel on the right keeps up as you work, showing Item (or Choose an item), Recipients as a count of youth, and Due (or Not set). Read the recipient count before going further — it is the easiest thing to get wrong.

  6. Review, then assign

    Choose Review & assign. A dialog opens headed Assign with the item title, the due date beneath it, and a sentence saying how many journeys it will be added to. It also tells you if your message will be sent right away and if a reminder will be scheduled.

    What you should see

    Two buttons: Cancel, and Assign to followed by the recipient count and youth. Pressing Escape closes the dialog without assigning anything, and so does clicking outside it.

If any recipient is not on your caseload, the whole assignment is refused with One or more recipients are not assigned to you. and nothing is assigned to anyone.

Preview limitation

Some icons on the assign-work and check-ins screens render as blank space rather than a symbol. The labels beside them are correct, so read the words rather than looking for the picture.

A workable weekly rhythm

Nothing forces an order on you, but these screens fit together in one obvious way: Dashboard, then clear the review queue, then At-risk, then check-ins, then competencies, then Assign work. Reviews first, because a young person waiting on a quiz score cannot see how they did until you act. Flags next, because they are time-sensitive in a way that a competency level is not.

For what each of these actions leaves behind in the record, see Lifecycles and the evidence they leave. For what a guardian can and cannot see of any of it, see Give consent and follow progress.
