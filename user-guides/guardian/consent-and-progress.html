Give consent and follow progress

This page is written for parents and guardians, and it is meant to be read on its own from beginning to end. It explains what you are being asked to agree to and why, how to sign it, the four separate choices you make about photographs, how to change your mind later, and — just as importantly — what Word Up will never show you about your young person.

Guardian WU-UG-13

What Word Up is, and what it is asking of you

Word Up is the software a youth leadership program uses to run itself. Young people work through lessons in it, take quizzes, hand in assignments, and get feedback from a mentor. Staff use it to take attendance, keep track of how everyone is doing, and send news.

You are being asked for two things, and it helps to keep them apart in your mind from the start:

Participation consent

Your permission for your young person to take part in the program at all. This is required. Without it, they cannot begin.

Media consent

Your permission — separately, and entirely optional — for the program to use photographs or video of your young person. Saying no to all of it changes nothing about their participation.

Before you enter anything real, read this. Word Up is a development project. No independent security, privacy, safeguarding, or accessibility review has been completed, and no release is approved for production use or for storing real participant information. If someone has asked you to sign a consent form in it, ask them directly whether this is a live program or a trial.

Why consent stops everything until it is given

In many systems, a consent form is paperwork that follows along behind. In Word Up it is a gate. Until a valid participation consent exists for a young person under 18, they cannot start lessons — not because a member of staff is holding them back, but because the software will not let them through.

If your young person signs in before you have signed, they see a page headed A guardian needs to sign off, explaining: Before you can start lessons, a parent or guardian needs to give participation consent. It only takes a minute — send them a secure link, or have them sign now. They have two buttons there: Notify my guardian, which emails you a link, and My guardian can sign now, for when you are sitting beside them.

One exception is worth knowing: a young person with no guardian linked to their account is treated as an adult and is not gated this way at all.

Signing from the secure link

The most common route is the one where you do not have a Word Up account at all. Your young person presses Notify my guardian, and you receive a link. You do not need to sign in, create a password, or install anything — you open the link and fill in a form.

The link is one-time and it expires

The page tells you the exact expiry date and time. Once you have used it, or once it has expired, it stops working and shows Consent link unavailable with the reason. That is not a fault. Ask your young person to send a new one.

  1. Open the link and check it is the right young person Guardian

    The page is headed with your organisation's name followed by guardian consent, above the words Review participation and optional media choices. A blue box at the top names the young person after Youth:, tells you which email address the request went to, and when the link expires. If the name is not the young person you expected, stop and contact the program.

  2. Read the participation statement

    A panel headed Participation statement holds the exact wording you are agreeing to. Programs can set their own; where they have not, it reads I give permission for this youth to participate in Word Up activities. If the program has given a contact address, it appears just underneath, after Questions:. Use it if anything is unclear — that is what it is there for.

  3. Enter your name and relationship

    Guardian full name and Relationship to youth are both required. Relationship is free text — "Mother", "Father", "Grandmother", "Legal guardian" are all fine. These two go onto the permanent record of who agreed to what.

  4. Tick the participation consent

    The one required tickbox reads I give permission for this youth to participate in Word Up. Nothing is submitted until this is ticked. This is the box that opens the gate.

  5. Make your media choices, or skip them

    Below a heading reading Optional media choices sit four more tickboxes. Every one of them is optional. They are explained in full in the media consent section below — it is worth reading that before ticking anything.

    Some organisations show an extra note here: Organization policy: youth media requires recorded guardian permission. Participation consent remains separate from these optional media choices. It is telling you the same thing this page is: leaving all four unticked does not affect participation.

  6. Sign and submit

    The last field is Type your full name as your signature, with the note Typing your name creates an electronic signature with date and time. Then choose Sign and submit consent.

    What you should see

    The form is replaced by a panel headed Consent recorded, reading Thank you. The youth’s access updates automatically, and a copy has been sent to the guardian email. Underneath it, after the word Reference:, is a code. Keep it — it identifies this exact consent record if you ever need to ask about it.

"Access updates automatically" means what it says: your young person does not need to wait for a member of staff to do anything. The gate opens as soon as your signature is recorded.

Preview limitation

Some icons on this consent page render as blank space instead of a small picture. The words beside them are correct and complete, so nothing is missing from what you are agreeing to.

Signing and managing consent from inside Word Up

If the program has given you your own guardian account, you sign in and everything is managed from one screen instead. Choose Consent — the last of four items in the menu, alongside Home, My Youth and News. On a phone the same four appear as buttons along the bottom of the screen.

The page is headed Consent and contact, under the small label Guardian settings, with the line Manage your own details and your youth’s consent record. Everything on it is described in the sections that follow.

If no young person has been linked to your account yet, this screen shows No consent record yet and the line A participation and media consent record will appear once a youth is linked. There is nothing you can do from here until the program links you — contact them.

Your participation consent record

Once consent has been given, it is kept as a record you can see at any time, under the heading Participation consent. A badge beside that heading reads either Active or Withdrawn. Five details are listed underneath.

Line What it tells you
Signed by The name that was typed as the signature, and the relationship given with it.
Signed The date and time it was signed.
Method How it was given — for example through the secure link, or recorded by a member of staff.
Valid until The date this consent stops counting. Read the note below about renewal.
Reference The same code you were shown when you signed. Quote it if you contact the program about this record.

Consent can need renewing

Valid until is not decoration. Consent expires, and how soon depends on how your organisation has set Word Up up:

  • Where the organisation asks for fresh consent each term, the record is valid until the end of that term. You will be asked again for the next one.
  • Otherwise, the record is valid for one year from the day you signed it.

When it lapses, the gate closes again — your young person loses access to lessons until a new consent is recorded. Nobody has done anything wrong when this happens; it is the date doing its job. Watching the Valid until date is the simplest way to avoid a surprise mid-term.

Email me a copy

The Email me a copy button sends the consent record to your email address. Word Up confirms with Your consent record has been emailed.

Use it whenever you want your own copy for your files, and use it before you withdraw consent if you want a record of what was in place beforehand. You can press it as often as you like; it changes nothing.

Media consent: four separate choices

Media consent is where most confusion happens, so it is worth going slowly. There are four tickboxes, they are not a scale from least to most, and they do not replace one another. Each one turns on a different permission.

Allow media use

The master switch. With this unticked, nothing is permitted, no matter what else you tick. Ticking it on its own still permits nothing — it only opens the door for the three below.

All photos and video

Photographs and video of your young person, including pictures where they are the subject on their own. This also covers group pictures.

Group photos only

Pictures and video where your young person appears as part of a group, and nothing where they are pictured alone. Choose this instead of the one above if that distinction matters to you.

First-name captions

Naming your young person by first name in a caption. This is entirely separate from the picture permissions. If you leave it unticked, any permitted picture must go out unnamed.

Two combinations are the ones people most often mean to choose:

What you want What to tick
No pictures at all Leave all four unticked. This is the position you start from, and it is a complete answer.
Group pictures are fine, but do not name them Allow media use and Group photos only. Leave All photos and video and First-name captions unticked.
Any picture is fine, and a first name is fine Allow media use, All photos and video and First-name captions.

Choose Save media consent to record a change. Word Up confirms with Media consent saved. You can come back and change these at any time, as often as you like, without affecting participation in any way.

Media permission depends on participation consent. If participation consent has lapsed or been withdrawn, no media permission applies either, whatever the tickboxes say. The two are separate decisions, but media consent cannot outlive the participation consent it hangs from.

Withdrawing consent

You can withdraw at any time, without giving a reason and without asking anyone. The button is Withdraw consent, beside Email me a copy on the participation consent card. It only appears while consent is active.

Word Up asks you to confirm first:

Are you sure you want to withdraw participation and media consent? This may restrict your youth’s program access.

Read the two things that sentence is telling you. First, this withdraws both participation and media consent together — there is no way to withdraw only one of them here. Second, restricting program access is the expected consequence, not a side effect: your young person will lose access to lessons.

Choose Cancel in that dialog and absolutely nothing happens. Choose OK and the withdrawal is recorded.

  1. Get a copy first, if you want one

    Press Email me a copy before withdrawing, so you have a record of what was in place.

  2. Withdraw and confirm

    Choose Withdraw consent, read the question, and confirm.

    What you should see

    The badge on the consent card changes from Active to Withdrawn, the Withdraw consent button disappears, and a green message confirms Consent withdrawn. The program team has been notified.

  3. Expect the program to be in touch

    Staff are told automatically — you do not need to send an email as well. Your young person's lesson access stops.

The withdrawn record itself is not deleted. It stays visible to you, showing when consent was given and that it was later withdrawn, because that history is what proves the program acted correctly at the time. To start again, a new consent has to be given — either through a fresh secure link or recorded by the program.

If you want your young person's personal information removed altogether rather than just their consent withdrawn, that is a different and broader request. Ask the program directly; the process is described in Privacy, consent and the audit trail.

Your contact details and emergency contact

The left half of the Consent and contact screen is headed Contact details and holds six fields about you. Keeping them accurate matters more than it looks: this is how the program reaches you, and what a mentor sees on your young person's record.

Name and Email

Both required. The email address is where consent copies and program notifications go.

Relationship

Your relationship to the young person.

Phone

Your day-to-day number.

Emergency contact name and Emergency contact phone

Who to call if something happens at a session and you cannot be reached. Fill these in even if they duplicate your own details — a blank emergency contact shows staff Not provided at the moment they most need a number.

Choose Save contact details to store changes. Word Up confirms with Contact details saved.

Following how your young person is doing

With consent in place, two screens show you how things are going. Neither lets you change anything — the progress screen says so in its own label, Read-only family view.

Home

Home is headed Your youth at a glance, with your young person's name beneath it, and three large figures across the top.

Figure What it means
Overall progress How far through the curriculum they are, as a percentage.
Attendance rate The share of sessions they have attended, as a percentage.
Badges earned How many badges they have been awarded so far.

Below the figures, a card headed What’s next names the next session coming up, or says No upcoming sessions. A View full progress button takes you to the detailed screen. If nothing has been linked to your account yet, the page instead reads No linked youth yet with the line Your youth’s read-only progress will appear here once the program links your accounts.

Preview limitation

Some icons on this home screen render as blank space instead of a picture. The wording is unaffected.

My Youth

My Youth in the menu opens the full picture, headed Child progress. It is a stack of plain panels:

Modules

Each part of the curriculum with its status and how complete it is. Empty, it reads No modules assigned yet.

Attendance history

Each session with its date and whether they were marked present, late, excused or absent.

Approved quiz outcomes

Quiz results with a score, the word Passed or Keep going, and the date. Only results a mentor has approved appear here — see the note below.

Leadership competencies

The leadership skills the program tracks, each with the level their mentor has recorded.

Earned badges and Still to unlock

What they have been awarded, and what is still ahead of them.

Upcoming schedule

Sessions still to come, with dates and times.

Shared mentor updates

Notes from their mentor that the mentor has chosen to share with you. Empty, it reads No mentor updates have been shared. Most mentor notes are private and never appear here.

A quiz score you cannot see is not a score being hidden from you. Word Up marks a quiz straight away, but holds the result back from everyone — including your young person — until their mentor has looked at it and approved it. If a quiz has been taken and no result has appeared, it is waiting on a mentor, not on you.

News

News holds News and announcements — program and cohort updates relevant to your family. Unread items carry an Unread badge, and a Mark all read button appears when there are any. Empty, it reads No announcements yet with the line Program and cohort news will appear here.

Preview limitation

The bell icon at the top of the screen does not open a notifications page for guardians — pressing it takes you to News instead. That is where your updates are, so nothing is lost, but do not go looking for a separate notifications list. There is not one for guardians.

What you will never see

This is the section to read if you are weighing up whether to consent. Word Up deliberately keeps some things away from guardians. These are not settings a member of staff can switch on for you, and they are not gaps waiting to be filled in a later version.

You will never see Why
Private reflections After a lesson, a young person may write a personal reflection. These are private by default. They can choose to share one with their mentor; there is no option anywhere that shares one with a guardian.
Quiz answers You see approved quiz outcomes — the title, the score, and whether it was a pass. You never see the questions, which options they picked, or which ones they got wrong. Only their mentor sees that.
Private mentor check-in notes A mentor writes up conversations, including things like prayer requests. Those notes are private unless the mentor deliberately shares a particular check-in — and when they do, the whole of that check-in becomes visible to both you and your young person, not a selected part of it. Anything not shared stays out of your view entirely.
Any other family's information You see your own linked young person and nothing else. No other participant's name, progress, attendance or contact details is reachable from a guardian account, by any route.

The reasoning is the same in every case: a young person needs somewhere they can be honest with a mentor, and a program needs to be able to promise that. If you have a concern about something in those private spaces, the route is a conversation with the program — not a screen in the software.

What you can always see is the consent record itself: who signed, when, by what method, until when, and its reference. That is the part that documents your own decision, and it is yours to keep a copy of at any time.

If something does not work

A few things go wrong often enough to be worth naming, with what to do about each.

The consent link will not open

If you see Consent link unavailable, the link has already been used or has expired. Ask your young person to send a new one from their Notify my guardian button. If the message instead reads This consent link is incomplete. Ask the youth to send a new link., the address was cut short somewhere — usually by an email program breaking it across two lines. A fresh link fixes it.

Your young person still cannot start lessons

Check the Valid until date on the consent record and the badge beside Participation consent. If it says Withdrawn, or the date has passed, a new consent is needed. If it is Active and in date, contact the program — the problem is not the consent.

You cannot see any young person

Your account has not been linked yet. Only the program can do that.

You have been signed out

Word Up signs you out after 30 minutes without activity, with no warning beforehand. Sign in again and carry on; nothing is lost.
