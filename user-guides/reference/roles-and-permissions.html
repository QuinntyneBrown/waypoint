Roles and permissions

Waypoint decides what you can do in two passes: your role opens a door, and then a scope rule decides which records behind that door are yours. This page gives both — the five roles, the eight policies, the complete capability matrix, and the exact wording of every refusal you can meet.

Everyone W-UG-17

The five roles

Every account holds exactly one role, and it never holds two. The values below are the ones actually stored, which is what you will see in an export, an invitation, or an audit entry.

Stored value Shown in the account menu as What the role is for
Youth Youth Leader A young person in the program. Works through lessons, writes reflections, takes quizzes, submits assignments, and talks to their mentor. Sees their own records and nobody else's.
Mentor Mentor Carries a caseload. Reviews and grades submitted work, approves quiz scores, logs check-ins, sets competency levels, raises and resolves at-risk flags, assigns work, schedules sessions and takes attendance.
ProgramAdministrator Director Runs the program. The only role that can manage people and accounts, decide applications, author curriculum, import and export data, handle privacy requests, and read the audit log.
Guardian Guardian A parent or carer. Gives and withdraws consent for their own children, and follows the progress those children have chosen to share.
Volunteer Volunteer Helps at sessions. Sees the names on a session they were assigned to and marks who turned up. Nothing else.

Two words that are not roles

Lead Mentor

A per-cohort designation, not a sixth role. Every cohort names one lead mentor and may have supporting mentors alongside; the same person can lead one cohort and support another. The stored role is still Mentor, and being a lead grants no extra permission anywhere else in the product. The same mentor cannot be both lead and supporting on one cohort — The lead mentor cannot also be a supporting mentor.

Public

Not a stored role at all. "Public" simply means a visitor who is not signed in. There is no public account, no public password, and nothing to assign. The public site, the four public forms, the invitation-accept page and the guardian consent link are the only things reachable without signing in.

The eight policies

Waypoint does not scatter role checks through the product. It names eight permissions, decides once which roles hold each, and then every screen and endpoint asks for a permission by name. If you want to know whether you can do something, find the policy.

Policy Held by Opens
YouthParticipation Youth — and a valid guardian participation consent Lessons, the curriculum, the journey, quizzes, reflections, assignments and submissions, coding lessons, and My progress
ManageUsers Program Administrator People and roles, cohorts, mentor assignments, organisation settings and terms, and privacy request fulfilment
ApproveApplications Program Administrator The application queue, and the approve and decline decisions
EditCurriculum Program Administrator Tracks, modules, lessons and lesson media, quizzes and questions, assignments and rubrics, badges, and the progression model
ImportExportData Program Administrator Roster import — both the preview and the commit
ViewAuditLog Program Administrator The audit log and its CSV and PDF exports
InviteUsers Program Administrator Mentor Creating, listing, resending and revoking invitations, singly or in bulk
RecordAttendance Program Administrator Mentor Volunteer Saving an attendance register

Six of the eight are administrator-only. That is the shape of the product: mentors get exactly two shared permissions — inviting people and taking a register — and everything else they do is scoped to their own caseload rather than granted by a policy.

The consent gate on youth participation

YouthParticipation is the only policy that asks a second question after the role check. Being a Youth is necessary but not sufficient: Waypoint also checks, on every single request, that a valid guardian participation consent exists.

A youth with no linked guardian is not consent-gated

If no guardian account is linked to the young person's record, Waypoint treats them as an adult: participation is allowed without any consent record, and they control their own media permissions. The gate exists for minors with a guardian on file, not for every account that happens to hold the Youth role.

A consent refusal is not an ordinary refusal, and the product does not treat it like one. The response carries X-Waypoint-State: consent-required, and instead of the generic Error 403 page the young person is taken to A guardian needs to sign off, which offers Notify my guardian, My guardian can sign now and Sign out. The fix is a signature, not a different account.

Media consent works the same way but on top: it is only ever valid while participation consent is valid, and it is refused with Guardian consent for individual photos or video is required. and X-Waypoint-State: media-consent-required. What makes a consent record valid at a given moment is set out in Guardian consent validity.

Scope: which records are yours

Passing the role gate gets you into an area. It does not give you everything in it. A second layer of rules decides which records inside that area belong to you, and it is enforced when the request is handled — not in the navigation, so you cannot get round it by typing a different address.

Administrators are scoped too

The one place the widest role is deliberately narrowed is its own account. An administrator cannot delete themselves — You cannot remove your own account. — and a removal that needs a second pair of eyes is refused with Another administrator must approve your removal request. That is the only protection against a single account being able to erase the person holding it.

What happens when you are denied

A refusal is always a 403. What you see depends on which kind it was.

Kind of refusal What you see
Your role does not hold the policy The Error 403 page, headed You don’t have access to that, reading This area is for a different role. If you think this is a mistake, switch to the right account or head back to your home.
The role is right but the record is not yours The specific scope message from the tables above, shown where you were working rather than as a whole page.
Guardian participation consent is missing A guardian needs to sign off — not the 403 page. See The consent gate.
The account has been deactivated This account is inactive, with Your account isn’t active right now. Please contact your program director to restore access.

Every denial is written down

Every 403, of every kind, writes a ProtectedActionDenied entry to the audit log naming who was refused and which method and path they asked for. That is deliberate: a pattern of refusals is exactly the sort of thing a safeguarding review wants to be able to see. It also means there is no way to probe the product quietly.

Two things a refusal is not. It is not a 404 — Waypoint tells you that you are not allowed rather than pretending the thing does not exist. And it is not a bug report: if you believe you should have access, the fix is a role change or an assignment by a Program Administrator, not a retry.

Changing someone's role

A Program Administrator changes a role from the person's record. Three things happen, in this order, and none of them is delayed.

The audit entry records the move as previous -> new, so the history of who held which role and when is reconstructible. Rules around the change:

  • Only the five stored roles are accepted — anything else gives Unknown role.
  • A removed person's role cannot be changed — A removed person's role cannot be changed.
  • Setting the role a person already holds does nothing at all: no session revocation, no audit entry.
  • Nothing the person created moves with them. Work they submitted, notes they wrote and registers they took stay attached to them under the old role.

Changing a role is not a way to hand over a caseload

Turning a mentor into a volunteer does not reassign their youth — it just stops them reaching them. Reassign the caseload from Mentor Assignments first, then change the role.
