Accessibility and display preferences

Word Up lets you change five things about how it looks and eight things about how it contacts you, and those choices follow you to any device you sign in on. This page covers all of them, the keyboard behaviour that genuinely exists, what assistive technology is told — and, plainly, where the current release falls short.

Everyone WU-UG-18

Display and accessibility preferences

Open the account menu in the top bar, choose Profile & settings, then the Display tab. The page is headed Display & accessibility. Everything on it is available to every role — youth, mentor, program administrator, guardian and volunteer alike.

Control Its sub-label What it actually changes
Text size Changes preview instantly across the app. Scales body text between 90% and 150%. Everything sized relative to body text scales with it.
High contrast Stronger borders and text. Darkens borders and muted text so edges and secondary text separate more clearly from the background.
Reduce motion Minimise animations and transitions. Cuts animations and transitions to effectively nothing, and turns off smooth scrolling.
Underline links Always underline links for clarity. Underlines every link, everywhere, instead of relying on colour alone to mark them.
Reading font Use a serif font for body text. Switches body copy to a serif face. Some readers find serifs easier over long passages.

Text size in detail

Text size is not a slider. It is a pair of buttons — A− and A+, labelled Decrease text size and Increase text size for assistive technology — with the current value shown between them as a percentage. Each press moves by 10, and the range is 90% to 150%. Pressing past either end does nothing; there is no wrap-around and no error.

Previewing, then saving

Every control on this page previews immediately — the moment you toggle a switch or press a text-size button, the whole application redraws behind the settings page so you can judge the result in context. Nothing is stored until you press Save settings. A successful save shows the toast Display settings saved.; a failed one shows Couldn’t save your settings. Please try again.

  1. Open the Display tab

    Account menu → Profile & settings → Display.

    What you should see

    A page headed Display & accessibility with the eyebrow Settings, a row of tabs reading Profile, Notifications, Display, Privacy, and the five controls below.

  2. Change what you want

    Press A+ or A−, and flip any of the four switches. Watch the page around you change as you go.

  3. Press Save settings

    Until you do, the preview is only in this browser tab and will be lost on reload.

    What you should see

    The toast Display settings saved.

Your settings follow you

Display preferences are stored on the server, against your account — not in this browser's storage. Sign in on a different computer, a phone, or a shared device in the room, and Word Up re-applies them as soon as you are signed in. There is nothing to set up twice, and clearing your browser data does not lose them.

Preview limitation

The settings sidebar always highlights Profile, even when you are on Notifications, Display or Privacy. The tabs above the content show where you really are; trust those and ignore the sidebar highlight.

There is no light or dark theme

This is worth saying plainly, because it is the setting people look for first. Word Up has one appearance. There is no light mode, no dark mode, no automatic switch that follows your operating system, and no high-contrast theme beyond the High contrast toggle described above. The five preferences on the Display tab are the complete list of what you can change about how Word Up looks.

This is a decision, not an omission or a bug. Asking for a dark theme is a scope change rather than a fix.

The dark toggle in this guide's header is not a Word Up feature. This user guide is a separate static document with its own chrome, and its light/dark switch belongs to the guide alone. It has no effect on the product, and nothing like it exists inside Word Up.

Notification preferences

The Notifications tab, headed Notifications, opens with Choose how you’d like to hear from Word Up. Changes save automatically. — and it means it. There is no save button on this page. Each checkbox writes as you tick it.

There are eight categories, each with two independent channels: In-app (the bell and the notifications list) and Email. Turning one off does not affect the other.

Category Description shown under it In-app default Email default
Lesson reminders Nudges to keep your journey moving. On Off
Mentor messages When your mentor sends you a message. On Off
Session reminders A reminder before sessions you plan to attend. On Off
Work graded When a mentor reviews your submitted work. On Off
Badges earned When you earn a new achievement badge. On Off
At-risk alerts When an assigned youth needs follow-up. On Off
Approval needed When submitted work is ready for your review. On Off
Announcements Program-wide news and updates. Off Off

Announcements is the one category that starts off. Every other in-app channel is on when your account is created, because each of the other seven is about something that needs you to act. Announcements are program-wide news, so they are opt-in. Email starts off for all eight; turn on whichever ones you want by post as well as on screen.

The list is the same for every role. Some categories will never produce anything for you — a youth will not get At-risk alerts, and a volunteer will not get Work graded — but the switch is there either way.

Each tick saves on its own and confirms with the toast Notification preferences saved. If a save fails you get Couldn’t save that change. and should try the tick again.

Notifications are in-app and email only. There is no browser push channel, no SMS, and no quiet-hours scheduling — if you want to stop hearing about something at night, turn the category off.

Keyboard behaviour

Word Up is built from ordinary links, buttons and form controls, so Tab, Shift + Tab, Enter and Space work throughout in the way you would expect from any web page. Beyond that, there are exactly three pieces of custom keyboard behaviour. There is no global shortcut scheme and no command palette.

Keys Where they work What they do
Ctrl + Enter The message composer, for youth and for mentors Sends the message without reaching for the send button. The help text under the box says so: Ctrl + Enter also sends your message. for a youth, Ctrl + Enter also sends. for a mentor.
← → Home End The tab strip on the youth My progress page — and nowhere else Left and right move to the neighbouring tab and wrap round the ends; Home goes to the first tab and End to the last.
Esc Any dialog Closes the dialog and returns focus to whatever opened it.

Inside a dialog

Dialogs in Word Up are properly modal. When one opens, focus moves into it — to the control the dialog nominates, or to its first focusable control. Tab is trapped: tabbing past the last control wraps to the first, and shift-tabbing before the first wraps to the last, so you cannot tab out into the page behind. Closing the dialog — with Esc, a button, or anything else — puts focus back on the control that opened it.

Only a few screens use a real dialog: the applications review dialogs, and the roster-import confirmation. Most of what looks like an overlay elsewhere is an ordinary page region.

Preview limitation

Three tab strips do not respond to arrow keys — the mentor youth record, the administrator reports hub, and attendance history. Only the youth My progress tabs do. On those three, use Tab to move between the tabs and Enter to choose one.

Preview limitation

The signed-in portal has no skip link. The public site has one — Skip to content — but the portal does not, so a keyboard user has to tab through the header and the whole sidebar before reaching the page content on every navigation. (This guide has one; the product does not.)

What a screen reader is told

Word Up announces three kinds of change without you having to go looking for them.

Toasts

Every toast lands in a single shared region marked as a live region and labelled Notifications. It announces politely, so a toast waits for you to finish what you were reading rather than interrupting. This is how you hear Display settings saved. and its equivalents.

Progress and confirmation

Status lines that change while you work — the attendance save message, the guardian consent confirmation, the message-thread loading state, the text-size percentage — are marked as polite status regions, so their new text is read out when it appears.

Errors

Genuine error callouts are marked as alerts, which announce immediately rather than politely. Field-level validation messages appear next to their field and are associated with it, so they are read when you reach the field.

Beyond that: every navigation region carries its own label, so they can be told apart — Main for the sidebar, Primary for the mobile bottom bar, Settings for the settings tabs. Icon-only buttons in the top bar are labelled Notifications, Account and Menu. Toggle switches carry the same label as their visible heading, and the text-size buttons are labelled Decrease text size and Increase text size rather than "A−" and "A+".

Preview limitation

Screen-reader-only status text is currently visible on screen. Announcements that should be invisible to sighted users render as ordinary text on mentor reviews, submission review, quiz review, check-ins, flags, attendance history, youth messages and youth notifications. It is not an error and nothing is wrong with your account — the text is doing its job, it is simply not hidden as it should be.

Preview limitation

The three reading-size buttons on a lesson all show the letter A. They are, in order, small, medium and large, but nothing on screen distinguishes them — only their labels do, so a screen reader can tell them apart and a sighted user cannot. Left to right is smallest to largest.

Preview limitation

The attendance status buttons have no visual selected state. Present, Late, Excused and Absent look identical whether or not you have chosen them; only assistive technology reports the pressed state. Use the tally counts above the list to confirm what you marked.

Preview limitation

Some icons render as blank space rather than a symbol. It affects sign-in, forgot password, reset password, guardian consent and guardian home, mentor assign-work and check-ins, volunteer today and schedule, youth curriculum and home, onboarding, and the guardian consent-link page. The buttons still work and are still labelled; they just look empty.

How far this has been checked

Two things are true at once here, and both matter.

What is tested automatically

The repository runs an automated accessibility check against WCAG 2.1 level A and AA rules as part of its browser test suite, and that suite is a gate in continuous integration. A change that introduces a detectable violation on a covered page does not merge. The same suite also asserts that no page scrolls sideways at the document level, which is the usual symptom of a layout that breaks at large text sizes.

What has not been done

No independent accessibility review

No independent accessibility review has been completed. An automated rule set catches a real but limited slice of problems — it cannot judge whether a label is meaningful, whether a flow is usable with a screen reader end to end, or whether the reading order makes sense. Nobody outside the project has audited Word Up for accessibility, and the limitations listed on this page are proof that automated checking is not the same as being accessible.

More broadly: Word Up is a development project. No independent security, privacy, safeguarding, or accessibility review has been completed, and no release is approved for production use or for storing real participant information. Use synthetic data only.

If something here blocks you

Use Help in the account menu to raise a support request. It asks for a category, a description, and a reply-to email address, and you can attach a screenshot. Say which page you were on and what you were trying to do — the preview limitations above are known, but how much they cost you in practice is not.
