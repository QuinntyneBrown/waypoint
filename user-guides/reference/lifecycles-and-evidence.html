Lifecycles and the evidence they leave

Almost everything in Waypoint has a state, and states move along fixed paths. This page draws every one of those paths: what each state means, who is allowed to move it, where it can go next, and the exact message you get when you try a move that is not allowed. It closes with the evidence side — which actions write a permanent audit record.

Everyone W-UG-16

How to read a lifecycle here

Each section below has the same three parts. The diagram shows the legal path from start to finish. The table gives each state its meaning, the role that can move it, and where it can go. The guard is the message you get if you try to move it somewhere it cannot go — every one of those arrives as a 409, which is the status that means "legal action, wrong moment".

Two things are true of every lifecycle. Nothing skips a state, and a state is never changed silently — the same action that moves a state also writes the audit record described in The evidence trail.

The stored values are shown here exactly as they are stored, because that is what an export or an audit entry contains. Where the screen shows something different — InReview is displayed as In review, for instance — both are given.

Application status

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
New The public form was submitted and nobody has looked at it. No account exists. Program Administrator InReview, NeedsConsent, Approved, Declined
InReview — In review Someone has picked it up and is working through it. Program Administrator NeedsConsent, Approved, Declined
NeedsConsent — Needs consent The applicant is under 18 and no valid guardian consent exists yet. This is a waiting state, not a rejection. Program Administrator, once a guardian has signed Approved, Declined
Approved Placed into a cohort with a mentor. An invitation is issued and the young person's account becomes Active when they accept. Nobody — final Nowhere
Declined Turned down, with an optional decision note of up to 2000 characters. Nobody — final Nowhere

The consent column beside the status is separate and shows Not required (the applicant is 18), Recorded, or Needs consent.

The guards

Trying to decide an application twice: This application already has a final decision. Declining an application whose youth account already exists: A linked youth account must be resolved before declining. Approving when the placement is not available: Choose an available cohort., The selected cohort's track is paused., Choose an active mentor., or The selected mentor is not assigned to this cohort.

Account status

Every person in Waypoint has one of four account states. Three of them can be moved back and forth. The fourth cannot be left.

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
Pending Invited but the invitation has not been accepted. The person cannot sign in. The invited person, by accepting Active
Active A normal, usable account. Only Active accounts can be issued a session. Program Administrator Deactivated, Removed
Deactivated Switched off. The password still works but sign-in is refused with This account is inactive. Please contact your program director. The record stays visible to administrators. Program Administrator Active, Removed
Removed Permanently gone. The person disappears from the people list entirely. Nobody — terminal Nowhere

Removed cannot be undone

Every route back is closed, individually and explicitly. Reactivating gives Removal is irreversible; this person cannot be reactivated. Deactivating gives A removed person cannot be deactivated. Changing the role gives A removed person's role cannot be changed. Sending a password reset gives A removed person cannot receive a password reset. Removing again gives This person has already been removed. Before you remove anybody, be certain that deactivating is not what you actually want.

Two more rules protect the removal path. You cannot remove yourself — You cannot remove your own account. — and a removal that needs a second pair of eyes is refused with Another administrator must approve your removal request. Issuing a session to a non-Active account is refused with Tokens can be issued only for active users.

Changing somebody's role is not a status change, but it has the same immediacy: the new role applies at once and every existing session for that person is revoked, so they are signed out of every device and must sign in again.

Invitation status

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
Pending Issued and not yet used. Valid for 14 days. The invited person (accept); a Program Administrator or the inviting Mentor (revoke, resend) Accepted, Revoked
Accepted Used. The account exists and is Active. The link is dead. Nobody — final Nowhere
Revoked Cancelled before use. The link is dead. Nobody — final Nowhere

The accept page reports five outcomes: valid (proceed to set a password), accepted, revoked, expired, and invalid (the token does not exist). The last four all give This invitation is no longer valid. Please request a new invite. as a 410. There is a request a replacement action on that page so a stranded invitee is not stuck.

The guards

Revoking anything but a pending invitation: Only pending invitations can be revoked. Two people revoking at once: This invitation changed before it could be revoked.

Curriculum publish status

Tracks, modules, lessons, assignments and quizzes all share one publish state. Unlike every other lifecycle on this page, this one is not a one-way path: an author may move content between all three states freely, including pulling published content back to Draft.

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
Draft Being written. Invisible to youth, not assignable, not reportable. Program Administrator InReview, Published
InReview — In review Finished and waiting for a second read. Still invisible to youth. Program Administrator Draft, Published
Published Live. Visible to youth on an enabled track, assignable. Program Administrator Draft, InReview

A track has a second switch beside its publish state: enabled. A disabled track is paused for new enrolments and new assignments even while published, giving This track is paused for new assignments. and This cohort's track is paused for new enrolments.

The guards

Publishing an orphaned assignment: Attach the assignment to a module or lesson before publishing. Editing structure while editing content: A lesson cannot be moved to another module while editing it., A module cannot be moved to another track while editing it., An assignment cannot be moved while editing it., A quiz cannot be moved while editing its settings. Editing an assignment that people have already answered: An assignment with submissions can no longer be edited. Reordering with an incomplete list: The lesson order must contain every active lesson exactly once., The module order must contain every module exactly once., The question order must contain every question exactly once.

Submission status

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
Submitted Handed in for the first time. Waiting for a mentor. Mentor or Program Administrator Approved, NeedsRevision
UnderReview Handed in again after a revision request. Waiting for a mentor. Mentor or Program Administrator Approved, NeedsRevision
NeedsRevision Sent back with a required comment saying what to fix. The ball is with the young person. The youth who owns it, by resubmitting UnderReview
Approved Accepted, with a rubric total. This is what counts towards badges and reports. Nobody — final Nowhere

A mentor's third option, save-draft, deliberately changes nothing the youth can see: it stores partial rubric scores and a comment while leaving the status where it is.

Every submission carries a visible timeline built from four event types: Submitted, Resubmitted, Approved, NeedsRevision. Events are appended, never rewritten, so the whole back and forth stays readable afterwards.

The guards

Submitting twice: This assignment has already been submitted. Resubmitting something that was not sent back: This submission is not awaiting revisions. Resubmitting someone else's work: You may only resubmit your own submissions. Grading a stale copy: This submission changed after you opened it. Review the latest version before saving again. — reload and grade the version you are actually looking at.

Lesson progress

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
NotStarted Never opened. The default for everything. The youth, by opening the lesson InProgress, Completed
InProgress Opened and part read. Reading position and reading size are saved as you go, so you resume where you stopped. The youth Completed
Completed Marked done. This is what feeds module completion, badge rules and every progress percentage in reports. Nobody Nowhere

Coding lessons use the same three states, driven by a checklist instead of a page position: once every step is ticked the lesson is Completed, and saving progress afterwards never drops it back to InProgress.

The guards

Reaching a lesson you have no right to see: This lesson is locked., Published lesson not found., or You are not enrolled in this track.

Quiz attempts

A quiz attempt has three moments rather than a status field, and the middle one is the important one: the instant you submit, the attempt becomes read-only for everybody, including you.

Stage What it means Who can move it Can go to
Draft Started and in progress. Answers and the current question are saved as you go, so you can close the tab and come back. The youth who started it Submitted
Submitted Handed in. An automatic score and a pass/fail are computed and stored. The answers can never change. Mentor or Program Administrator Mentor-approved
Mentor-approved A mentor has accepted the automatic score, or replaced it with an override between 0 and 100. This approved score is the one the youth sees and the one that counts. Nobody — final Nowhere

A quiz allows between 1 and 10 attempts. The score that counts is the best passing attempt within the attempt limit, and running out gives No attempts remaining.

The guards

Answering an attempt you already handed in: This attempt has already been submitted. Approving something not yet handed in: This quiz attempt has not been submitted. Approving twice: This quiz assessment has already been approved. Opening someone else's attempt: This attempt does not belong to you. Reaching a quiz you are not entitled to: This quiz is locked.

Attendance

Attendance is not a progression — it is a value per person per session, and it can be corrected as often as you like. What makes it a lifecycle is the concurrency rule: two people cannot overwrite each other's marks without one of them being told.

State What it means Who can set it Can go to
Present At the session. Program Administrator, Mentor, Volunteer Any of the four
Late Arrived after the start. Program Administrator, Mentor Any of the four
Excused Away with notice. Program Administrator, Mentor Any of the four
Absent Away without notice. Program Administrator, Mentor, Volunteer Any of the four

Volunteers are limited to two of the four and may not write notes about a young person. Attempting either gives Volunteers may only mark youth Present or Absent. or Volunteers may not add youth notes.

The guards

Someone else saved first: This attendance record was changed by someone else. Reload and try again. A stale offline queue with a duplicate: Each youth may appear only once in an attendance save. Marking somebody who is not on the list: One or more youth are outside this session's roster. Taking a register for a session you were not given: This session is not assigned to you.

Preview limitation

The attendance status buttons have no visual selected state. Present, Late, Excused and Absent look identical whether or not you have chosen them; only assistive technology reports the pressed state. Use the tally counts above the list to confirm what you actually marked.

RSVP

A young person's answer to a scheduled session. Like attendance it has no order — you can change your mind as often as you want, until RSVPs close.

State Stored as Who can set it Can go to
Going Going, sent as going The youth Any of the three, while RSVPs are open
Maybe Maybe, sent as maybe The youth Any of the three, while RSVPs are open
Can't attend CantAttend, sent as cant The youth Any of the three, while RSVPs are open

An RSVP may carry a note of up to 1000 characters. There are no capacity limits and no waitlist — an RSVP tells the mentor who is planning to come and nothing more. Mentors and administrators can read the responses for a session; an RSVP is never attendance, and saying Going does not mark anybody Present.

The guard

Answering after the cut-off: RSVPs are closed for this session.

Guardian consent validity

Consent is the one lifecycle with no status field at all. A consent record is either valid right now or it is not, and Waypoint re-decides that on every request. Four conditions must all hold at the moment of asking.

Condition What it means Who can change it What breaks it
Participation granted The guardian ticked participation when signing. This is the one part of the form that cannot be left off — Participation consent is required. Guardian Nothing; it is fixed at signing
Not withdrawn The guardian has not withdrawn it. Withdrawal is a timestamp on the record, not a deletion. Guardian The guardian withdrawing consent
Within its date range Today is between the record's valid-from and valid-until dates. Nobody — time The valid-until date passing
Matches the current term If the organisation requires re-consent each term, the record must belong to the currently Active term. Program Administrator, via org settings and terms A new term becoming Active

Media consent sits on top and is checked separately. It is only ever valid while participation consent is valid, and it is granular: individual photos and video, group photos and video, and first-name captions are three independent permissions. Using an individual photo without the matching permission gives Guardian consent for individual photos or video is required.

A youth with no linked guardian is not gated. If no guardian account is linked to the young person's record, Waypoint treats them as an adult: participation is allowed and they control their own media permissions. Consent gating exists for minors with a guardian on file.

The full picture of what consent covers, how a guardian withdraws it and what happens next is in Privacy, consent and the audit trail.

Competency levels

A competency is a named skill inside a progression stage. Its level is a mentor's judgement, and unlike lesson progress it can move in either direction — a mentor who set it too high can set it back.

Level What it means Who can set it Can go to
NotStarted Not yet assessed. The default. Mentor or Program Administrator Any level
Developing Showing the skill with support. Mentor or Program Administrator Any level
Proficient Doing it reliably without help. Mentor or Program Administrator Any level
Mastered Doing it well enough to help someone else do it. Mentor or Program Administrator Any level

The guards

Setting a level for someone outside your caseload: This youth is not assigned to you. A competency that has been retired: Competency not found. — archiving a competency does not erase the levels already recorded against it.

Data-subject request status

A privacy request has two states and four kinds. Anyone signed in can raise one about their own data; only a Program Administrator can fulfil it.

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
Pending Raised and waiting. Program Administrator Completed
Completed Fulfilled, with the time it was fulfilled recorded. Nobody — final Nowhere
Kind What it asks for
access A copy of everything held about the person.
correction A change to display name, email or pronouns — at least one of them must be named.
removal Deletion of the person's data.
consent-withdrawal Withdrawal of a consent already given.

The guards

Fulfilling twice: This data-subject request has already been fulfilled. A kind that cannot be actioned automatically: This data-subject request type cannot be fulfilled. A correction that would change nothing: A correction must change at least one profile field.

Term status

A term is the block of time a cohort runs for, and it is what consent re-consent is measured against. Waypoint allows at most one Active term at a time, which is what makes "the current term" a meaningful phrase everywhere else.

State What it means Who can move it Can go to
Upcoming Planned, not started. The default for a new term. Program Administrator Active, Completed
Active The current term. Must include today's date. At most one term may be here. Program Administrator Upcoming, Completed
Completed Finished. Consent records tied to it stop satisfying the re-consent rule once a different term becomes Active. Program Administrator Upcoming, Active

The guards

Activating a term whose dates do not cover today: An active term must include today's date. A second active term: Complete the current active term before activating another. A repeated name: A term with this name already exists. Dates the wrong way round: The term end date must be after its start date.

The evidence trail

Every state change above leaves a record. The audit log is the one place in Waypoint where nothing can be edited or deleted — it only ever grows, and each entry is chained to the one before it so that tampering is detectable.

How the chain works

Each entry records the same fields:

Field What it holds
Action What happened, e.g. ApplicationApproved.
Actor Who did it.
Target The kind of thing and the specific record.
At When, in UTC.
Source A coarse device class and a masked network location. Never a raw address or browser string.
Details A short description — but see the redaction rule below.
Sequence and hashes The chain position, the previous hash, and its own.

What the log will not show you

Some entries are marked private, and their details are replaced with [redacted] when the log is read or exported. The content of a mentor's private check-in notes — including prayer requests — is never written into the audit log in the first place. The log records that a check-in was logged and by whom, not what was said.

Who can read it

Only a Program Administrator. The audit log and its CSV and PDF exports sit behind the ViewAuditLog policy, which no other role holds — not mentors, not guardians, not volunteers, and not youth. Reading it is itself an ordinary administrative action; exporting it writes a DataExported entry, so the log records its own extraction.

What the log is for, how it fits with privacy requests and retention, and how a guardian's consent history is reconstructed from it are covered in Privacy, consent and the audit trail. For the refusal messages that produce ProtectedActionDenied entries, see Roles and permissions.
