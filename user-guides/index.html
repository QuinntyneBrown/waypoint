Word Up User Guide

Everything Word Up can do, written for the people who use it

Word Up is a youth leadership platform for Bible study, mentoring, leadership development, and coding. This guide covers the whole product, role by role: what each screen is for, what every message means, and where the current release stops. It describes only behaviour that exists today.

What Word Up does

Word Up brings youth applications, consent and safeguarding, cohort operations, learning, mentoring, attendance, communication, and reporting into one role-aware system. It serves five kinds of signed-in user — youth, mentors, guardians, volunteers, and program administrators — plus members of the public who visit the marketing site without an account.

There are two web applications, and they are separate on purpose.

The public site

Open to anyone, no account needed. It carries program information and the four public forms: apply to a cohort, contact the team, sign up to volunteer, and start a donation pledge.

The portal

Everything behind sign-in. What you see is decided by your role, and for youth it is decided by guardian consent as well. Youth see lessons and their journey; mentors see a caseload and a review queue; administrators see the whole program.

Two terms are worth fixing now, because the rest of the guide uses them constantly. A track is a curriculum stream — Word Up ships two, Bible & Leadership and Coding for Kingdom. A cohort is a group of young people who work through a track together with an assigned lead mentor, over a fixed term.

How a young person moves through Word Up

Almost everything in the product hangs off this one path. Each stage has an owner, and no stage can be skipped.

Applications do not create accounts. Submitting the public application form records an application and nothing more. An account exists only after an administrator approves the application and the resulting invitation is accepted.

Who does what

Word Up has exactly five roles. Every account holds one of them, and it decides both what you can reach and what you can see inside it.

Role Everyday job Can see Cannot
Youth Work through lessons, quizzes and assignments Their own journey, progress, schedule and mentor thread See another young person's work, or reach any staff screen
Mentor Review submitted work and follow a caseload Only the youth assigned to them, and their own cohorts Manage people, cohorts, curriculum or organisation settings
Program Administrator Run the program end to end Everything, including the audit log and privacy requests Recover a person once they have been permanently removed
Guardian Give consent and follow their own child Their child's shared progress, attendance, news and consent record See private reflections, quiz answers, or any other family
Volunteer Take attendance at an assigned session Names only, on sessions they are assigned to Mark anything but Present or Absent, or add notes about a young person

Lead Mentor is a per-cohort designation, not a sixth role: it names the mentor who leads a particular cohort. Public likewise is not a stored role — it simply means a visitor who is not signed in.

What this release does and does not do

Read this before you rely on Word Up for anything real. The limits below are deliberate and current, not a wish list.

Not approved for production or for real participant information. Word Up is a development project. No independent security, privacy, safeguarding, or accessibility review has been completed, and no release is approved for production use or for storing real participant information. Use synthetic data only.

What works today

The whole path described above works end to end, and each part of it is covered by automated acceptance tests: public content and the four public forms; applications and the review pipeline; invitations, sign-in, password reset, lockout and onboarding; guardian consent and its enforcement; cohorts, mentor assignment and organisation settings; curriculum authoring and publishing; lessons, reflections, quizzes, assignments, coding lessons, badges and progression; mentor grading, competencies, check-ins and at-risk flags; sessions, attendance including offline capture, announcements, messaging and notifications; the guardian and volunteer portals; reports, exports and roster import; and privacy requests with an append-only audit log.

Deliberately not in this release

These were considered and left out. They are not bugs, and asking for them is a scope change, not a fix.

  • No light or dark theme in the product. Display preferences cover text size, contrast, motion, underlined links and reading font — nothing else. (The dark toggle in this guide's own header is guide chrome, and does not exist in Word Up.)
  • Offline works for attendance only. There is no offline lesson reading, reflection drafting, quiz taking or submission.
  • No cross-entity search. There is no global search across people, lessons and cohorts.
  • No sign-in with Google or any other provider, and no numeric PIN sign-in or multi-account switcher for shared devices.
  • One language. There is no language selection and no locale-aware formatting.
  • Reports are fixed. There is no custom report builder and no saved presets. Export is CSV and PDF; import is CSV roster migration.
  • No third-party integration. No Google Workspace or Drive connection, and no live Google Sheets sync.
  • Notifications are in-app, with optional email. There is no browser push channel and no quiet-hours scheduling.
  • RSVP is a simple response. There are no capacity limits and no waitlists.
  • No coding project gallery, and no automatically totalled volunteer hours.

Visible but not working

A few controls are present in the interface and do not do what they appear to. Each is marked with a Preview limitation callout on the page where it would otherwise be described as working, and all of them are listed together in Troubleshooting by symptom. The most likely to catch you out:

  • For volunteers, the Attendance navigation item returns you to Today instead of opening a roster.
  • The notification bell in the top bar does not reach a notifications page for administrators, guardians or volunteers.
  • On the attendance screens, the selected status has no visual highlight — the button you chose looks the same as the others.
  • Some icons render as blank space rather than a symbol.
