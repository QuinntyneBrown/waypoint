Cohorts, curriculum and settings

This page builds the structure everything else sits on: the cohorts young people belong to, the mentors who lead them, the tracks and lessons they work through, and the organisation policy and terms that govern the lot. It ends where it matters most — publishing, which is the moment authored content becomes real work for real young people.

Program Administrator WU-UG-11

The order things must happen in

Word Up will not let you build these pieces in the wrong order, so it is worth knowing the order before you start. A cohort needs a track and a mentor. A track only counts once it is published and enabled. A young person can only be placed in a cohort whose track is live.

Everything on this page lives in the sidebar groups People (Mentor Assignments, Cohorts), Curriculum (Curriculum, Badges, Progression) and System (Settings).

Creating a cohort

A cohort is a group of young people working through one track together, with one accountable lead mentor, over a fixed set of dates. Choose Cohorts in the sidebar. The page is headed Cohorts under the eyebrow Program, with the line Create cohorts and manage their membership and lead mentor.

Each cohort is a card showing its track as an eyebrow, then Lead:, Members:, Location: and Dates:. Unset values read Not assigned, Not set or Not scheduled rather than being blank. With no cohorts at all you get No cohorts yet and Create the first cohort to begin placing young people.

  1. Choose New cohort

    A dialog opens headed Create cohort with the subtitle Choose the learning track and accountable lead mentor.

    What you should see

    If either list is empty, an amber notice at the top of the dialog: An enabled published track and an active mentor are required before a cohort can be created. That is the prerequisite stated plainly. Go and satisfy it first.

  2. Fill in the three required fields

    Cohort name * (up to 128 characters; empty gives Enter a cohort name.), Curriculum track * (starts at Choose a track; empty gives Choose a curriculum track.) and Lead mentor * (starts at Choose a mentor; empty gives Choose an active lead mentor.).

  3. Add dates and a location if you have them

    Start date and End date are optional, but if you give both, the end must be later — otherwise End date must be after start date. Meeting location is free text up to 256 characters.

  4. Create

    Create cohort shows Creating… while it saves, and the list then reads Name was created.

Opening a cohort shows Cohort details — curriculum track, lead mentor, meeting location and term — plus Members and Add members side by side. Edit cohort reopens name, lead mentor and location in a dialog whose subtitle is a reminder: Changes are recorded in the audit log. Saving reports Cohort details saved.

Adding members to a cohort

The Add members panel offers a select labelled Add existing youth, starting at Choose a youth and listing each candidate as their name and email. Pick one and choose Add youth; the button reads Adding… while it saves and the page then reports Youth added to the cohort and enrolled in its track.

Two things are happening in that one sentence: cohort membership, and enrolment on the cohort's curriculum track. That second part is why placement matters — it is what decides which lessons the young person sees.

If nobody is available the panel reads No unassigned active youth are available. and offers Invite a youth instead. That is the normal state, because the list only ever offers active youth who are not already placed.

One cohort per young person

A youth belongs to exactly one cohort. Trying to add somebody who is already placed elsewhere is refused with This youth already belongs to another cohort. To move them, change their placement rather than adding them twice.

Two other refusals you may meet here:

  • This cohort's track is paused for new enrolments. — the track has been paused in organisation settings. Existing members are unaffected; new ones cannot join until it is enabled again.
  • Choose an active youth account. — the account is not a Youth, or is not active.

The members list shows each person's name and email with a View record link, and an amber badge for anyone whose status is not Active. Empty, it reads No youth are in this cohort yet.

Mentor assignments and load

Choose Mentor Assignments. The page is headed Mentor assignments under the eyebrow People and coverage, with the line Pair youth, build each cohort team, and balance active mentor caseloads. It does three jobs, in three areas.

Pair a youth

The first card is Pair a youth, noting The change is visible on the mentor's roster after saving. Choose a young person under Youth — each option reads name, cohort (or No cohort) and current mentor (or Unassigned) — then a mentor under Assigned mentor, where each option carries that mentor's current caseload. Assign mentor saves it and reports Mentor assignment saved. The mentor roster and load are updated.

With nobody to pair, the card shows Pairing needs youth and mentors and Add an active youth and mentor before pairing them.

The cohort mentor team

The second card is Cohort mentor team: Set one lead and any number of supporting mentors. Choose the cohort under Cohort team, one person under Lead mentor, and tick as many as you like under Supporting mentors. Each supporting option shows that mentor's load balance beside their name. Save cohort team reports Cohort mentor team saved.

The lead mentor's tick box is disabled, because one person cannot hold both jobs on the same cohort. If the combination is submitted anyway it is refused with The lead mentor cannot also be a supporting mentor.

This matters beyond bookkeeping: cohort team membership is what decides which mentors you may assign an approved applicant to, and what a Mentor may reach at all.

Mentor load

Below both cards sits Mentor load, headed with the current band — Target band: 4–8 active youth. Check-ins and reviews are all-time activity counts. Each active mentor gets a card with their name, email, three figures (caseload, check-ins, reviews) and one of three badges.

Overloaded

Red. Above the band. The card also carries an amber line reading Rebalance this mentor's youth assignments.

Balanced

Green. Inside the band. Nothing to do.

Under target

Amber. Below the band — capacity you can use when placing the next applicant.

These are advisory. Nothing stops you assigning an eleventh young person to an overloaded mentor; the badge just makes sure you know you did.

The curriculum builder

Choose Curriculum. The page is headed Curriculum builder with the line Build tracks, ordered modules, lessons, assessments, and downloadable resources. Content nests four deep: a track holds ordered modules, a module holds ordered lessons, and both modules and lessons can hold quizzes and assignments.

Tracks

A toolbar across the top carries the Curriculum track picker, the track's publish status as a badge, a module count, and Publish track. New track opens a dialog headed Create track asking for Track name, Track key and Track description; creating one reports Track created. With none at all you get No curriculum tracks and Create a track to begin authoring.

Modules

The form under the toolbar is headed Add a module, or Edit module once you are editing one. It takes Module name, Module description, Scripture anchor and Module prerequisite.

Module prerequisite defaults to No prerequisite and otherwise lists the other modules on the track — a module can never be its own prerequisite, so it is left out of its own list. Setting one gates the module behind the earlier one for youth.

Saving reports Module added. or Module updated. Each module card then carries Add lesson, Add quiz, Add assignment, Edit module, Move module up, Move module down and Publish module. Reordering reports Module order saved.

Lessons

Add lesson opens a dialog taking Lesson title, Scripture reference, Estimated minutes, Learning objectives, Teaching body, Reflection prompts and Leader guide notes. Saving reports Lesson created. or Lesson updated.

Each lesson card offers Edit lesson, Move lesson up, Move lesson down, Publish lesson and Archive lesson, plus a file field labelled Upload PDF or MP4 which accepts only those two formats. Uploads report Lesson media uploaded., removals Lesson media removed., archiving Lesson archived.

Quizzes

Add quiz asks for three settings before anything else:

Pass mark

The percentage needed to pass. It is shown on the module assessment row as pass n%.

Attempts allowed

How many tries a young person gets. Their recorded score is the best passing attempt within this limit.

Feedback mode

One of Show correct answers, Show score only or After all attempts. It decides how much a young person sees after submitting.

Two tick boxes follow: Shuffle questions and Required for module completion. Creating a quiz reports Quiz created. Add its questions below. and opens the Quiz questions editor, where the same settings can be re-saved with Save quiz settings.

Questions come in four types: Multiple choice, True or false, Multiple answer and Short answer. The first three take Answer options (One option per line. True/false options are supplied.) and Correct option numbers, given as a list like 1, 3. Short answer instead takes Correct text answers — One accepted answer per line. Add question reports Question added., and reordering reports Question order saved.

Assignments

Add assignment takes Assignment title and Task description, then a group headed Allowed submission types with four tick boxes: Text response, Photo upload, Link and File upload. At least one must be ticked; none gives Choose at least one submission type.

Then Due date, Grace period days, and two tick boxes, Allow late submissions and Require mentor approval.

Finally a Rubric, which is what the mentor actually grades against. Each row has a Rubric criterion and Criterion points; Add rubric criterion adds another and Remove criterion takes one away, though the last remaining row cannot be removed. The points add up to the assignment's total, shown on the assessment row as n points. Saving reports Assignment created. or Assignment updated.

Publishing

Nothing you author reaches a young person until it is published. This is the single most consequential control on the page, so it gets its own section.

Tracks, modules, lessons and assignments each publish separately, and each carries its own status badge which turns green at Published. The buttons are Publish track, Publish module, Publish lesson and Publish assignment, reporting Track published., Module published. and Lesson published. respectively.

Publishing an assignment creates work for real people

Publish assignment reports Assignment published and assigned to enrolled youth. — and it means it. Publishing walks every active youth whose cohort is on that track and creates assigned work for each of them, carrying the assignment's due date. It skips anyone who already has it, so publishing twice is safe, but a wrongly-scoped assignment lands in a lot of inboxes at once. Check the track before you publish.

An assignment that is attached to neither a module nor a lesson cannot be published at all: Attach the assignment to a module or lesson before publishing. That is not pedantry — the attachment is how Word Up works out which track, and therefore which young people, the assignment belongs to.

Publishing a track does not publish what is inside it, and publishing a module does not publish its lessons. Each level is its own decision, so a published track can legitimately contain draft modules that nobody sees yet.

Badges

Choose Badges. The page is headed Badge builder with the line Define automatic milestones and manual recognition that mentors can award. The form is headed New badge definition and warns Archived badges remain on youth records but stop earning.

Give the badge a Badge name, a Badge icon, a Badge description, and then choose its Criteria kind — either Automatic or Manual. That choice is the whole design of the badge.

Automatic badges

An automatic badge names an Automatic rule, one of Module completed, Lesson completed, Quiz passed, Assignment approved or Five-session attendance streak. All but the streak rule also need a Rule target — the specific module, lesson, quiz or assignment that triggers it. Leave it blank and you get Choose the curriculum item that triggers this badge.

The system awards these itself, the moment the condition is met. No one presses anything.

Automatic and manual are mutually exclusive

Only Manual badges get an Award badge button. Attempting to hand out an automatic badge by other means is refused with Automatic badges cannot be awarded manually. If you want staff to be able to give a badge for something the system cannot measure — courage, welcome, turning up for a friend — it has to be created as Manual from the start.

Manual badges

A manual badge has no rule. Its card carries Award badge, which opens a dialog headed Award badge name with a Youth recipient picker starting at Choose a youth. Confirm award reports Badge awarded to Name.

Mapping and archiving

Either kind can carry a Mapped competency, defaulting to No competency mapping and grouped by progression stage. That is what ties a badge to the framework described next.

Saving reports Badge definition saved. Archive badge reports Name archived. and stops the badge being earned, without stripping it from anybody who already has it. With no badges at all you get No active badges and Create the first badge definition above.

The progression framework

Choose Progression. The page is headed Progression framework with the line Keep growth stages in order and define the competencies mentors assess.

A stage is a named step in a young person's growth; a competency is something a mentor assesses within that stage. Mentors rate each competency as Not started, Developing, Proficient or Mastered.

Add stage takes Stage name and Stage description, reporting Stage added. or Stage updated. Each stage card is numbered Stage n and carries Move stage up, Move stage down, Edit stage and Add competency. Reordering reports Framework order saved. — the order is the framework, so it is worth getting right.

Add competency takes only a Competency name and reports Competency added. Remove competency reports Name removed from the active framework. A stage with none reads No competencies in this stage.; an empty framework reads No progression stages with Add the first stage to establish the framework.

Protecting your authoring work

The curriculum builder, badge builder and progression builder all hold work in forms that are not saved to the server until you press save. All three protect it the same way.

Try to navigate away with unsaved changes and the browser asks: You have unsaved authoring changes. Save your authoring draft before leaving? Select OK to save the draft, or Cancel to stay. Choosing OK stores the draft locally and lets you leave; choosing Cancel keeps you on the page. Closing or reloading the browser tab triggers the standard browser warning, and the draft is stored first either way.

When you come back, the draft is put back into the forms and the page reports Authoring draft restored.

One case cannot be restored. If the draft belonged to a track that no longer exists or is no longer available to you, the page reports The saved authoring draft could not be restored because its track is unavailable. and the draft is discarded. There is no way to recover it, so treat a long authoring session the way you would treat any long piece of writing: save as you go.

Organisation settings

Choose Settings. The page is headed Organization settings under the eyebrow Program administration, with the line Manage identity, guardian consent policy, program terms, and available tracks.

Organization identity

Organization name, Display name, Logo URL (with a live preview once set), City and Contact email, whose help text explains where it turns up: Shown to guardians on consent forms and emailed records.

The panel also states a limit up front — The platform colour palette remains fixed for accessibility. You cannot re-skin Word Up, and there is no light or dark theme anywhere in the product.

Guardian consent policy

This is the most important panel on the page: These settings drive term access and youth-media safeguards.

Consent statement

The text a guardian actually reads and signs. It is shown verbatim on the consent form, so whatever you write here is the agreement. Change it deliberately.

Require re-consent each term

A prior-term record will not unlock participation once a new active term begins. With this on, every young person's consent lapses at the term boundary and has to be given again — which will lock youth out of lessons until their guardian signs. Plan the term rollover around it.

Require media consent for youth media

Photos and video require a current applicable guardian permission. This is separate from participation consent; a guardian can grant one and refuse the other.

Save organization settings reports Organization settings saved and recorded in the audit log.

Terms and cohort cycles

The Terms and cohort cycles panel — Maintain date ranges and advance each term through its lifecycle. — takes Term name, Start date, End date and Term status, which is one of Upcoming, Active or Completed. The button reads Create term or, once you have chosen Edit term on a row, Save term, with a Cancel term edit escape beside it. Saving reports Term created and recorded in the audit log. or Term updated and recorded in the audit log.

Three rules govern terms, and all three are enforced when you save:

  • Dates must make sense — The term end date must be after its start date.
  • A term you mark Active must actually be running: An active term must include today's date. You cannot activate next autumn's term in July.
  • There is at most one active term at a time: Complete the current active term before activating another. The rollover is deliberate — mark the old one Completed first, then activate the new one.

Names must also be unique — A term with this name already exists. Each row shows its dates and a status badge, green for Active, blue for Upcoming and dark for Completed. With none you get No terms yet and Create the first program term above.

Track availability

The last panel is Track availability, which states its own behaviour exactly: Pausing a track preserves existing progress but blocks new enrolment and work assignment.

Each track shows its key and publish status, an Enabled or Paused badge, and a button reading Pause track or Enable track. Toggling reports Name paused and recorded in the audit log. or Name enabled and recorded in the audit log.

Pausing is a safe, reversible way to close a track to new intake without disrupting anyone already on it. Young people already enrolled keep their lessons and their progress. What stops is everything forward-looking: you cannot create a cohort on it, cannot add members to a cohort using it (This cohort's track is paused for new enrolments.), and cannot approve an applicant into one (The selected cohort's track is paused.).
