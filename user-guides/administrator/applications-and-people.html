Applications, invitations and people

This page covers the front door of the program: how an application arrives, how you review it against guardian consent, how approving one creates an account, and how you then invite, find, re-role, deactivate or permanently remove the people in your program. Every decision described here is written to the audit log.

Program Administrator Mentor W-UG-10

What this page assumes

You are signed in as a Program Administrator. In the sidebar you have a People group containing People, Import Roster, Mentor Assignments, Cohorts, Applications, Invitations and Users & Roles. Almost everything on this page lives in that group.

Mentors share one screen with you — Invitations — but see a narrower version of it. Where that matters it is called out.

Nothing here works without a cohort and a mentor. Approving an application requires you to name both. If you have not created either yet, read Cohorts, curriculum and settings first and come back.

The public application form

Applications reach you from the public site, at Apply. No account is needed to fill it in, and filling it in does not create one. The page is headed Join a cohort and says Open to young people aged 12–18.

The form captures the following. Fields marked required cannot be left blank.

Field Required What it holds
Who’s filling this out? Always answered One of I’m applying for myself, I’m a parent or guardian or I’m referring someone.
Cohort you’re interested in Yes Free text, e.g. the placeholder For example, Regent Park autumn cohort.
First name / Last name Yes The young person's name, not the person typing.
Age Yes Must be 12 to 18 inclusive. Anything outside that range is rejected on the form.
Neighbourhood Yes Free text; it becomes a column in your applications list.
Track you’re most interested in No No preference yet, Bible & Leadership or Coding for Kingdom.
Why are you interested? Yes Long text. You see it in full during review.
Parent or guardian If under 18 A block headed Parent or guardian with the note Required for applicants under 18. It asks for Guardian name, Guardian email and Guardian phone, all required once it appears.
Acknowledgements Yes Two tick boxes: I confirm a parent or guardian consents to participation. and I’ve read the privacy notice.

Submitting shows Application received with the text Thanks for applying to Waypoint! We’ll review your application and reach out with next steps. You don’t have an account yet — we’ll set one up once you’re accepted into a cohort. That last sentence is literally true, and it is the single most important thing to understand about intake.

A tick box is not consent

The I confirm a parent or guardian consents to participation box is an acknowledgement typed by whoever filled the form in. It is not a guardian consent record and it will not let you approve a minor. A real consent record has to be signed by the guardian. See Give consent and follow progress for how that happens.

The applications list

Choose Applications in the sidebar. The page is headed Applications under the eyebrow Intake, with the line Review consent, place applicants, and keep every decision traceable.

One control sits in the page header: a select labelled Application status. Its options are, in order, All statuses, New, In review, Needs consent, Approved and Declined. Changing it reloads the table immediately; there is no apply button.

The table has seven columns: Applicant, Age, Neighbourhood, Track, Status, Submitted and an unlabelled action column.

The Minor badge

A grey Minor badge appears next to the age of any applicant under 18. It is the signal that this application is consent-gated.

The status pill

Colour-coded: Needs consent is amber, Approved is green, Declined is grey, and New and In review are plain.

Track

Shows the applicant's stated preference, or an em dash if they chose No preference yet. It is a preference, not a placement.

Review

The button in the last column of every row. It is the only way into the review dialog. While the application loads you see Loading application…

If the filter matches nothing you get an empty state headed No matching applications with the line New applications and referrals will appear here for review.

Reviewing an application

Choosing Review opens a dialog headed Review application, with the applicant's name, age and application date underneath. It is a real dialog: Escape closes it and returns focus to the Review button you came from, and Tab stays inside it until you leave.

The consent banner

The first thing in the dialog body is a consent banner, and which one you get decides whether you can approve at all.

Amber

Guardian participation consent required — This minor cannot be approved into active participation until a valid consent record is linked. The Approve button is disabled while this is showing.

Green

Guardian participation consent recorded — This application is eligible for a placement decision.

No banner

Consent is Not required. The applicant is 18, so there is nothing to gate. Approve as normal.

What the applicant told you

Below the banner, a read-only block labelled Submitted application repeats the submission under six eyebrows: Submitted by, Neighbourhood, Cohort interest, Acknowledgements (reading, for example, Participation acknowledged · privacy acknowledged), Why interested, and Guardian / applicant contact if any contact detail was given. Missing contact fields read Name not provided, Email not provided or Phone not provided rather than disappearing.

The decision form

  1. Confirm the track

    Interest track is a free-text box with suggestions drawn from your published tracks. It records intent; it does not place anyone. Placement comes from the cohort you pick next.

  2. Choose the cohort

    Assign cohort * starts at Choose a cohort and is required. Leave it and try to approve and the field shows Choose a cohort before approval.

  3. Choose the mentor

    Assign mentor * starts at Choose a mentor and is also required; its error is Choose a mentor assigned to the cohort. The mentor list is filtered by the cohort you chose, so pick the cohort first. If you change the cohort afterwards and the mentor you had picked is not on the new cohort's team, the mentor field is cleared for you.

    What you should see

    Only mentors attached to the selected cohort in the list. If a mentor you expect is missing, they are not on that cohort's team yet — add them under Mentor Assignments.

  4. Add a decision note if you want one

    Decision note is optional, up to 2000 characters, and placeholder text tells you what it is for: Optional note kept on the applicant record. It is saved with either decision, approve or decline.

If the application already carries a final decision the footer changes: instead of Decline and Approve you see the status as a badge and a single Done button.

Approving and declining

Neither button acts immediately. Both open a small confirmation dialog on top of the review dialog, and both confirmations can be dismissed with Cancel or Escape.

Approve

The confirmation is headed Approve application? and reads Enrol this applicant in cohort with mentor? followed by The applicant contact will receive an approval email. The action button is Confirm approval, and shows Approving… while it runs.

Confirming does five things at once:

  • Creates a Youth account with status Active and no password yet, named from the application's first and last name.
  • Sets that account's cohort and mentor to the ones you chose.
  • Enrols the youth on the cohort's curriculum track.
  • Moves the application to Approved and stamps it with your decision note and the review time.
  • Emails the applicant contact, subject Your Waypoint application was approved, saying the young person has been approved for that cohort with that mentor and that Waypoint staff will follow up with account access details.

Back on the list you get a green line reading Name was approved and enrolled. The new person appears immediately in People.

Approval creates the account; it does not hand over the keys. The approval email deliberately contains no sign-in link. Getting the young person into the portal is a separate, deliberate step — see The account lifecycle for Send password reset.

Approval can still be refused. The messages you may see are:

Message Why
This application already has a final decision. Somebody approved or declined it since you opened the dialog.
Guardian participation consent must be recorded before approval. The applicant is a minor with no valid consent record.
The selected cohort's track is paused. The cohort's track has been disabled in organisation settings. Enable it, or choose a different cohort.
The selected mentor is not assigned to this cohort. The mentor is neither the lead nor a supporting mentor on that cohort.
Choose an active mentor. The mentor's account is no longer active.

Decline

The confirmation is headed Decline application? and asks Decline Name without creating an account? with the note The optional decision note remains on the application record. The action button is Confirm decline, showing Declining… while it runs, and the list then reads Name's application was declined.

Declining creates nothing. No account, no invitation, no cohort membership — only the application record changes, keeping the note and the timestamp. Because Declined is final, a declined applicant who should have been accepted has to apply again.

Sending invitations

An invitation is how somebody who does not yet have an account gets one. Choose Invitations in the sidebar. The page is headed Invitations under the eyebrow People, and for you it reads Invite people by role and track every link.

The left-hand card is Send invitations with the instruction Enter one email per line. Links expire after 14 days.

  1. Pick the role Program Administrator

    Role offers all five stored roles: Youth, Mentor, ProgramAdministrator, Guardian and Volunteer. The invitation creates an account in exactly that role.

  2. Optionally place them

    Cohort (optional) defaults to No cohort yet and Mentor (optional) defaults to Assign later. Both are carried onto the account when the invitation is accepted, so filling them in now saves a placement step later.

  3. Enter the addresses

    Email addresses is a six-row box. One address per line; commas and semicolons work as separators too. Addresses are lower-cased and de-duplicated before sending.

  4. Send

    Send invitation shows Sending… while it works.

    What you should see

    A green line reading Invitation sent. for a single address, or N invitations sent. for several. The email box clears, the status filter snaps back to Pending, and the new rows appear on the right.

Two things stop a send:

  • An empty box gives Enter at least one email address. under the field.
  • A malformed address gives Enter one valid email address per line. at the top of the page.
  • An address that already belongs to somebody gives An account with this email address already exists. Invite people who are new; use the person record for people who are not.

Mentors see a smaller form

A Mentor opening the same page reads Invite youth assigned to your mentoring scope. and gets no role picker and no mentor picker — instead a notice reading Invitations you send create Youth accounts assigned to you. A Mentor may only invite Youth (Mentors may only invite youth.), may only assign those invitations to themselves (Mentors may only assign invitations to themselves.), and may only pick a cohort they are on (That cohort is outside your assignment scope.). A Mentor's list shows only the invitations they created.

Managing invitations

The right-hand card lists what you have sent. Filter it with Search invitations, Status (All, Pending, Accepted, Expired, Revoked) and — for administrators only — Role. The status filter and role filter apply as soon as you change them; the search box needs Enter or the Apply search button. The list opens filtered to Pending, not to everything.

Columns are Email (with by name underneath, naming who sent it), Role & context (with the cohort and mentor, or No cohort / No mentor), Status, Expires and Actions. Twenty five rows per page, with Previous and Next and a Page n · N total readout between them.

How long a link lasts

Every invitation expires 14 days after it is created, and every link is single use. Once someone accepts, the invitation becomes Accepted and can never be used again.

Resend

Resend mints a brand-new token, resets the clock to another 14 days, and emails the new link. The old link stops working the moment you do this — so resending is also the way to invalidate a link that went to the wrong inbox.

Resend refuses in three cases:

  • Only pending invitations can be resent. — the invitation has already been accepted or revoked.
  • An account with this email address already exists. — somebody signed up on that address in the meantime.
  • This invitation changed before it could be resent. — two people acted on the same row at once. Reload and look again.

Revoke

Revoke does not fire straight away. The row switches to Revoke now? with Confirm and Cancel beside it. Confirming kills the link permanently and reports The invitation for address was revoked. Revoking is not reversible; to re-invite the same person, send a fresh invitation.

A revoked or accepted invitation cannot be revoked again — Only pending invitations can be revoked.

Rows that are neither pending nor expired show No actions instead of buttons. An empty list shows No invitations found with Send an invitation or change the filters.

The people directory

Choose People. The page is headed People directory with the line Search and filter everyone in the program. and an Invite people button that jumps to Invitations.

A filter card sits above the table:

Search people

Placeholder Name or email. It filters as you type — there is no search button — and resets you to page one.

Cohort

Defaults to All cohorts.

Role

A row of buttons rather than a menu: All roles, Youth, Mentor, Guardian, Volunteer, Administrator. Exactly one is active at a time and it is filled in solid.

The table shows Name (a link into the person record), Email, Role, Cohort, Status and Last active. Ten rows per page, with Previous, Next and a readout of Page n of N · total people. If nothing matches you get No people found with No one matches the current directory filters. and a Clear filters button.

Users & Roles is a narrower view of the same people, built for one job: re-roling in bulk. It is headed Users & roles with Review active accounts and change access deliberately. and carries a standing notice — Staff roles can access other people’s records. Every role change takes effect immediately and is audit logged. Each row has its own role menu and a Change button that turns into Apply now? with Confirm and Cancel. Confirming reports Name is now Role.

The person record

Selecting a name opens their record. The title is their display name, the eyebrow reads cohort · role (or No cohort), the subtitle is Program record and account lifecycle, and a status pill sits on the right: green for Active, amber for Pending, red for Deactivated or Removed.

Six tabs divide the record.

Tab What is on it
Profile Email, Pronouns, Cohort, Mentor, Onboarding (Complete or Not complete) and Joined.
Guardian & consent Guardian contact — name, relationship, email, phone and emergency contact — beside Consent, which shows a Valid or Not valid pill and then Participation, Media, Signed and Valid until. If no guardian is linked it reads No guardian contact is linked.
Progress n of N lessons complete with a progress bar and a percentage.
Attendance n of N attended and a rate, then every session with its status. Empty, it reads No attendance recorded.
Notes Check-in summaries, prefaced by Operational check-in summaries are shown here. Private prayer requests are never included. That exclusion is enforced, not a convention.
Account Status, role, Last active (or Never signed in) and Created — then the two action panels described below.

A person whose status is Removed carries a standing notice headed Historical record: This person was permanently removed from active use. Their progress and attendance remain available for reporting, but account access cannot be restored. No action panels are shown for them at all, only No account actions are available because permanent removal cannot be reversed.

Changing a role, resetting a password, deactivating

Everything here lives on the Account tab, in two panels: Access and role and Account lifecycle.

Change role

Pick a new value in Account role — the options are Youth, Mentor, Guardian, Volunteer and Program administrator — then choose Change role. The button is disabled until the selection differs from the current role.

The confirmation is headed Change account role? and asks Change this account from old to new? If the new role is a staff role — Mentor, Volunteer or Program administrator — an amber warning follows: This staff role grants access to young people's records and protected program data. The change takes effect immediately and all current sessions will be revoked. For a non-staff role you get the same warning without the first sentence. Confirm with Confirm role change; the record then reports Role changed to Role.

A role change signs the person out

Every current session for that account is revoked the instant you confirm. Whatever they were doing on whatever device, they are signed out and must sign in again. Do not do this mid-session without telling them.

Send password reset

Send password reset emails a reset link to the address on the record and reports Password reset sent to address. This is the normal way to give a newly approved applicant their first sign-in, and the normal fix for somebody locked out after five failed attempts.

Deactivate and reactivate

The Account lifecycle panel explains itself before you press anything: Deactivation signs the person out and blocks sign-in while retaining all records. It can be reversed.

Deactivate account confirms with Deactivate account? and the text This person will be signed out and unable to sign in. Progress, attendance, notes, and reporting records will be retained. You can reactivate the account later. The action button is Confirm deactivation, and the outcome is Account deactivated. Existing sessions were revoked.

A deactivated person's panel instead reads Reactivation restores sign-in eligibility, but previously revoked sessions stay revoked. Reactivate account confirms with This person will be able to sign in again. Previously revoked sessions remain invalid, so they must start a new session. and reports Account reactivated. A new sign-in is required.

Deactivation is the reversible option, and it is almost always the one you want. A youth who has left for the summer, a mentor between terms, a volunteer whose check has lapsed — deactivate. Everything they did stays, everything they can do stops, and you can undo it.

Removing a person permanently

Removal cannot be undone

Removal is irreversible; this person cannot be reactivated. That is the exact message the system returns if anyone later tries. There is no restore, no administrator override and no command-line escape hatch. If you are not certain, deactivate instead — it achieves the same practical result and can be reversed.

Remove person is the red button at the bottom of the Account lifecycle panel, in a bordered danger area. Above it the panel states plainly: Permanent removal revokes access and removes the person from active use. Progress and reporting records are retained, but removal cannot be reversed.

  1. Choose Remove person

    A confirmation opens headed Permanently remove person? with a red callout: Permanent removal cannot be undone. Access will be revoked and the person will be removed from active use; progress and reporting records will be retained.

  2. Type the name

    A field labelled Type display name to confirm must be filled in with that person's display name, exactly. The Permanently remove button stays disabled until it matches character for character. This is deliberate friction; do not paste past it without reading the name.

  3. Confirm

    Choose Permanently remove.

    What you should see

    A green line reading Name was removed. Access cannot be restored; reporting records were retained. The status pill turns red and reads Removed, the Historical record notice appears, and every action panel disappears.

What removal does and does not do

Gone

Sign-in, all current sessions, and any place the person appears as someone you can act on. Their status becomes Removed permanently.

Kept

Progress, attendance and reporting records. Removal is not an erasure request. If somebody has asked for their data to be deleted, that is a privacy request and it follows a different path — see Privacy, consent and the audit trail.

You cannot remove yourself

The Remove person button is not rendered at all on your own record. The other lifecycle actions still are, so it is entirely possible to deactivate your own account and lock yourself out; if you are the only Program Administrator, do not.

Every action on this page — approval, decline, invitation, role change, deactivation, removal — writes an entry to the audit log, with who did it and when. You can read that log under Audit Log in the System group. Nothing here is quiet.
