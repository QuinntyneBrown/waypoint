Youth / 07

Take a quiz and submit an assignment

Lessons are read; quizzes and assignments are marked. This page covers both — how many attempts you get and when a quiz locks, why your score does not appear straight away, and how to submit evidence, follow its review, and revise it if your mentor asks for changes.

Youth WU-UG-07

Two kinds of marked work

Both kinds appear as items on the Curriculum screen, and both are seen by your mentor. They behave differently, and mixing them up causes most of the confusion.

Quiz

A set of questions with a pass mark and a limited number of attempts. Word Up marks the answers, but a mentor decides when the score is released to you.

Assignment

A task you complete and send in as evidence — writing, a photo, a link or a file. A mentor reads it, scores it against a rubric, and either approves it or sends it back for revision.

A rubric is the list of criteria the work is scored against, each worth a stated number of points. You can see the rubric before you start; you see the points you were awarded only after review.

Before you start a quiz

Opening a Quiz item does not begin the quiz. It opens a brief screen headed Knowledge check above the quiz title, with the line Attempt 1 of 3 — the attempt you are about to take, and the total allowed.

Below that are four facts, each in its own small card. Read all four before you start.

Card What it tells you
10 questions How many questions are in this attempt.
15 minutes The author's estimate of how long it takes. It is guidance, not a countdown — nothing expires while you are answering.
80% pass mark The percentage you need to pass.
2 attempts remaining How many attempts you have left, including the one you are about to take.

If the quiz earns a badge, a Badge reward card names it here so you know before you begin.

The button at the foot reads Start attempt, or Resume attempt when you already have one in progress. When you have no attempts left, both are replaced by the line You have used all attempts for this quiz. and there is no way to start another.

Taking the quiz

The attempt shows one question at a time. The heading block carries Attempt 1 of 3, the quiz title, and Question 4 of 10 so you always know where you are.

  1. Answer the question in front of you

    Questions come in four kinds. Most give you options: a single choice, a true/false pair, or a multiple-answer question where more than one option may be right and each option has a tick box rather than a circle. A short-answer question gives you a text box labelled Your answer instead.

    What you should see

    Under a short-answer box, the hint Your answer saves when you leave this field. Typing alone does not save it — move to the next field or press Tab to commit what you wrote.

  2. Watch the answer save itself

    Every choice you make is sent to the server immediately. There is no save button on a question, and there is nothing to lose by closing the tab part way through.

    What you should see

    Near the top of the screen, Saving answer… followed by Answer saved. While Saving answer… is showing, the Next question and Submit quiz buttons are unavailable, so you cannot get ahead of your own answers.

  3. Move between questions

    Next question moves forward and Previous question moves back. Going back and changing an earlier answer is fine and saves the same way. Your position in the quiz is saved along with your answers, so resuming an attempt puts you back on the question you were on.

    What you should see

    Next question stays unavailable until the current question has an answer. There is no skipping: every question is answered before you reach the end.

Submitting a quiz attempt

On the last question, Next question is replaced by Submit quiz. Choosing it raises a browser confirmation box carrying exactly this question: 

Submit this quiz? Your answers cannot be changed for this attempt.

That sentence is literal. Select OK and the attempt is closed: you cannot reopen it, reword an answer, or change your mind about an option. Select Cancel and you return to the last question with everything as it was.

Submitting spends an attempt. Once submitted, the only way back into that quiz is a fresh attempt — and only if you have attempts remaining. Check the attempts remaining card before you confirm.

Your quiz result, and why it is not a score yet

Submitting takes you to a screen headed Quiz result, with Attempt 2 above it. What you find there depends entirely on whether a mentor has reviewed the attempt.

Before a mentor approves it

The screen shows a single card:

Waiting for mentor approval

Your answers are submitted and locked. Your score will appear after review.

No percentage, no pass or fail, no per-question feedback. This is deliberate, not a delay in the system: a quiz score is released to you only after your mentor has approved the assessment. Reloading the page, retaking nothing, and waiting all produce the same screen until that happens.

After a mentor approves it

The same screen fills in.

  • Approved score with your percentage, then Passed or Not passed yet, then Pass mark: 80% for comparison.
  • A Badge earned card, if passing this quiz earned one.
  • Question review, where each question is marked Correct or Needs review and the options are annotated — your answer and — correct answer. Where the quiz author did not release this, you see Question-level feedback is not available for this quiz. instead.
  • Retake quiz, but only when you both have attempts left and are allowed another.

Which score counts

Your recorded score for a quiz is the best passing attempt within the attempt limit. A later attempt cannot pull a passing score down, and an attempt taken beyond the limit does not exist to be counted. If none of your attempts reached the pass mark, there is no passing score to record and the item stays unfinished.

Read the assignment brief

An assignment opens on its brief, headed Assignment brief above the title, with Due and a date where the mentor set one. Three sections describe the work before any form appears.

Task

What you are being asked to do, in the mentor's words.

What you can submit

A list of the evidence types allowed for this particular assignment. Only the types listed here get a field on the form below.

Rubric preview

Each criterion your work will be scored against, with its points, shown as 5 points. This is the same rubric your mentor scores against, so read it as the mark scheme it is.

The four evidence types

Listed as Field on the form Limits
Written response A text box labelled Written response. Up to 12,000 characters.
Photo (JPG or PNG) A file chooser labelled Evidence photo, hinted JPG or PNG, up to 10 MB. JPG or PNG only, 10 MB or smaller.
Evidence link A box labelled Evidence link. A web address. It must start http:// or https://.
File (PDF) A file chooser labelled Evidence file, hinted PDF, up to 10 MB. PDF only, 10 MB or smaller.

Pick the wrong kind of file and you are told immediately — Choose a JPG or PNG image. or Choose a PDF file. Go over the size limit and you get The image must be 10 MB or smaller. or The file must be 10 MB or smaller. Nothing is uploaded in either case, so just choose again.

Submit your assignment

  1. Fill in the evidence you have

    You do not have to use every field the assignment offers, but you must use at least one. Submitting with all of them empty produces Provide at least one of the allowed submission types.

  2. Let files finish uploading

    Choosing a photo or a file uploads it at once, before you submit anything.

    What you should see

    A green line reading Uploading photo… or Uploading file…, replaced by Photo ready: or File ready: and the file name. The Submit assignment button is unavailable while an upload is running.

  3. Attest that the work is yours

    Above the button is a checkbox reading This is my own work and the evidence is truthful. It is required. This is a statement you are making to your mentor and to the program, so read it before you tick it.

    What you should see

    Submitting without ticking it produces Confirm this is your own work before submitting. and nothing is sent.

  4. Confirm the submission

    Submit assignment raises a browser confirmation box carrying exactly this: 

    Submit this assignment? Your submitted copy will be read-only unless your mentor requests a revision.

    Select OK and you are taken to the submission status screen. Select Cancel and nothing is sent; your uploads and text are still on the form.

Once an assignment has a submission, reopening the brief no longer shows the form. In its place is a single button, View submitted work.

Track your submission

The submission screen is headed Submission status above the assignment title, with a badge underneath carrying where it has got to.

What is on the screen

Your submitted copy

Exactly what your mentor received: your text, your note to them if you wrote one, the photo, a Open submitted evidence link link, or a Download link for a file. A large file may show Preparing and its name for a moment first. This copy is read-only.

Timeline

A numbered history of what has happened, using four labels: Submitted, Resubmitted, Approved and NeedsRevision. The stage you are at now carries a Current badge beside it.

Mentor feedback

Your mentor's written comment, when they left one. This is the place to look for what to change — it is not sent to you anywhere else.

Rubric result

Appears once the work has been scored: 18 of 20 points in total, then each criterion with the points you were awarded out of the points available.

Badge unlocked

Appears when the approval earned you a badge, naming it and describing it.

The status badge softens two of the stored labels for reading: you will see Under review and Needs revision in the badge at the top, while the timeline below uses the raw NeedsRevision. They are the same thing.

Revise and resubmit

When the status is Needs revision, and only then, a full-width button appears at the foot of the submission screen: Revise and resubmit.

  1. Read the feedback first

    The revision screen is headed Needs revision above Revise and the assignment title, and repeats your mentor's Mentor feedback at the top so you can work against it.

  2. Edit and send it back

    Two fields are offered: Revised response, pre-filled with what you sent before and required, and Evidence link. Then choose Resubmit assignment. Back to status returns you to the submission without sending anything.

    What you should see

    You are returned to the submission status screen, with Resubmitted added to the timeline and marked Current.

If the submission changed while you were writing

Your mentor can act on the submission at the same time as you are revising it. When that happens, resubmitting is refused and a warning appears at the top of the revision screen carrying exactly this: 

This submission changed on the server. Your revision is still here. Review the latest feedback before resubmitting.

Nothing you typed is lost — that is what Your revision is still here means. Choose Review latest feedback beneath the message to pull in what your mentor did, keeping your draft on the form, then resubmit once you have read it.

Do not retype your work

The temptation on seeing that message is to reload the page and start again. Do not. Reloading discards the revision you were writing; Review latest feedback is the button that keeps it.

Where this leaves you

An approved submission and a passing quiz score both count towards the module percentage on your Curriculum screen and can unlock whatever comes next. Badges earned along the way collect under Badges in the sidebar.

If you want to see the same review from the other side — what your mentor is looking at when they approve or send work back — the mentor page covers it.
