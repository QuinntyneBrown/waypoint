Youth / 06

Work through a lesson

This is the loop you will repeat most often in Waypoint: open the next unlocked lesson, read it, mark it complete, and write a reflection. This page walks the whole loop screen by screen, and states plainly which parts of it stay private to you.

Youth W-UG-06

What you are working through

Your learning is arranged in three levels, and every screen in this page refers to them.

Track

A curriculum stream, such as Bible & Leadership or Coding for Kingdom. You normally follow one. If you have been given more than one, the Curriculum screen shows a Your tracks row of buttons at the top so you can switch between them.

Module

A numbered group of items inside a track, shown as Module 1, Module 2 and so on. Modules run in order.

Item

One thing to do inside a module. Each item is labelled with its kind — Lesson, Coding lesson or Quiz — and, when the author set one, an estimate such as · 20 min.

Only published content reaches you. If an item is still being written, it is simply not on your Curriculum screen at all — it is not hidden behind a lock.

Start from Home

Choosing Home in the sidebar opens your home screen. It is headed Welcome, followed by your first name, with the line Your learning, schedule, and support in one place.

The lime card at the top is the fastest way back into learning. It is headed with your cohort name and the question Ready for your next step?, and it names the exact item you are up to — Continue, the item title, then in and its module, and where an estimate exists, About 20 minutes.

Resume learning

The primary button reads Resume learning when you have already started that item.

Start learning

The same button reads Start learning when you have not opened the item yet. Either way it takes you straight to the item.

View curriculum

The quieter button beside it opens the whole Curriculum screen, which is the subject of the next section. Use it when you want to see the shape of the track rather than jump to one item.

Two other things can appear in place of that card. If you have finished everything published for you, it reads Your published learning is complete. Beautiful work. with a single View my journey button. If you have not been placed in a cohort yet, the whole home screen is replaced by You’re not in a cohort yet and the text Your mentor will place you in a cohort soon. Once you’re placed, your lessons and journey unlock here. Reach out if you have questions. — there is nothing to work through until an administrator places you.

The Curriculum screen and how items unlock

Curriculum in the sidebar opens the full track. It is headed Curriculum with the line Follow your modules in order and see what unlocks next. Each module is a card carrying its number, its name, and a badge on the right.

Reading the badges

The badge is the whole progression story, so it is worth being precise about it.

Where Badge What it means
Module card Locked You cannot start anything in this module yet. Nothing inside it is a link.
Module card A percentage The module is open, and the number is how much of it you have completed.
Item row Locked This particular item is not available yet. Its title is plain text rather than a link, so there is nothing to click.
Item row Its status The item is open. Its title is a link that takes you into the lesson, coding lesson or quiz.

Where a module or an item is waiting on something, the condition is written out on the card in its own words — a module shows it as an information note under the description, and an item shows it as quiet text under the type label. That sentence is the authoritative answer to “why can I not open this?”, so read it before asking anyone.

Locked is not a mistake. Progression is deliberate: the program decides the order, and a locked item becomes available on its own once you meet the condition shown. There is no way to unlock an item yourself, and your mentor does not need to do it manually.

If the whole track is empty you will see This track is not set up yet with the line Published modules will appear here when they are ready. That means nothing has been published to your track — not that something has gone wrong with your account.

Preview limitation

On both Home and Curriculum, some icons render as blank space rather than a symbol — the empty-state icon and the icons beside a few buttons. The words next to them are correct and the controls work normally; read the labels and ignore the gaps.

Read the lesson

Opening a Lesson item gives you the reading screen. The top left carries a Back to curriculum link, and the heading block shows the module name, the lesson title, and a line reading Step 3 of 8 · 20 minutes — your position in the module, then the estimate where one exists.

  1. Set a comfortable text size

    A card above the lesson holds three small buttons. They set small, medium and large reading text, in that order, and the change applies immediately to the lesson body.

    What you should see

    Under the card, the line Saving reading preferences… and then Reading preferences saved. Your choice is stored against the lesson, so it is still in place when you come back.

  2. Bookmark it if you want to

    On the right of the same card is a button reading Bookmark. Choose it and the label becomes Bookmarked; choose it again to clear it. It is a marker for you alone — it does not notify anyone and it does not affect progress.

  3. Read the lesson body

    The lesson is written in fixed sections, and any section the author left empty is simply absent. In order, they are Scripture (the passage the lesson is anchored to), What you’ll learn, Teaching, Optional media, and Think about it.

    What you should see

    Think about it is the prompt you will be asked about in the reflection, so it is worth reading twice. It reappears word for word on the reflection screen.

  4. If a video will not play

    Optional media is optional by design. When a video or resource fails to load, the section is replaced by the message The media could not be loaded. The full lesson text remains available. and a Retry media button. Nothing in the lesson depends on it, so a failed video never blocks you from finishing.

Your scroll position is remembered as you read. Leave part way through, come back later, and the lesson reopens where you stopped.

Preview limitation

The three reading-size buttons all show the same letter A at the same size, so you cannot tell them apart by looking. They are, left to right, small, medium and large; only their labels for assistive technology — Small text, Medium text and Large text — distinguish them. Pick one and judge the result from the lesson text itself.

Mark the lesson complete

At the foot of a lesson you have not finished is a full-width button reading Mark lesson complete. Choosing it raises a browser confirmation box carrying exactly this question: 

Mark this lesson complete? Your next step will be the reflection.

Select OK and Waypoint records the lesson as complete and moves you straight to the reflection screen. Select Cancel and nothing at all is recorded — you stay on the lesson.

Once a lesson is complete, that button is gone. In its place is Continue to reflection, which is how you get back to a reflection you started earlier or never wrote.

Write your reflection

The reflection screen is headed Reflect on followed by the lesson title, with the line Take your time. You can save this as a private draft. A Back to lesson link sits above it, and where the lesson had a Think about it prompt, that same prompt is repeated at the top so you do not have to go back for it.

  1. Say how you are feeling

    The first block asks How are you feeling? and offers four choices: Hopeful, Thoughtful, Unsure and Challenged. Pick one. There is no wrong answer, and Unsure and Challenged are ordinary answers, not warnings.

    What you should see

    If you try to submit without choosing, the message Choose how you are feeling. appears under the options.

  2. Write the reflection itself

    The box labelled Your reflection is the main answer, and its hint says Required when you submit. Drafts can be unfinished. It holds up to 8,000 characters.

    What you should see

    Submitting with this box empty produces Reflection text is required. Saving a draft with it empty is fine.

  3. Add one action, if you have one

    The second box is labelled One action I will take and is marked Optional. It holds up to 4,000 characters. Use it for something concrete you intend to do; leave it empty if nothing comes to mind.

  4. Save a draft or submit

    Two buttons sit at the foot. Save draft keeps an unfinished reflection without validating anything. Submit reflection finalises it, and requires both a mood and reflection text.

    What you should see

    A green line at the top of the screen: Saving draft… then Draft saved., or Submitting reflection… then Reflection submitted. Coming back later reopens what you left, with Draft restored. or Submitted reflection restored. at the top.

Who can see your reflection

This is the part of the lesson loop people most often get wrong, so it is stated on its own. A reflection is private by default. Writing one, saving it as a draft, and submitting it all leave it visible to you and to nobody else.

Sharing is a single deliberate act. Above the buttons is a checkbox labelled Share with my mentor, and directly under it this help text, word for word: 

Off by default. When it is off, this reflection is private to you. Turn it on only if you want your assigned mentor to read it and respond during check-ins.

The box starts cleared on a new reflection. It is never ticked for you, submitting does not tick it, and no other screen turns it on. If you want your mentor to read a reflection, you must tick it yourself before you save or submit.

Two things are separate and worth keeping separate: your reflection is private unless you share it, while your progress — which lessons you have completed — is always visible to your mentor and to a linked guardian. What stays private is what you wrote, not the fact that you finished.

Leaving before you save

Waypoint will not let you wander off a half-written reflection in silence. If you have typed anything since the last save and then navigate away — the sidebar, a link, the back button — a browser confirmation box appears carrying exactly this: 

You have unsaved reflection changes. Save your reflection draft before leaving? Select OK to save the draft, or Cancel to stay.

The two answers do different things, and the wording is easy to read backwards.

OK

Saves what you have written as a draft, then continues to where you were heading.

Cancel

Cancels the move and leaves you on the reflection with your text intact. It does not discard anything.

Closing the browser tab or window with unsaved changes raises your browser’s own “leave site?” prompt instead. That one is drawn by the browser, so its wording is not ours and varies between browsers.

Coding lessons work slightly differently

An item labelled Coding lesson opens a different screen, headed Interactive coding lesson above the title. There is no Mark lesson complete button and no reflection at the end.

Instead you get three things.

Steps and a self-check

A numbered list under Steps on the left. Each step has instructions, a Code sample, and a tick box you mark yourself when you have done it. The card at the top, Self-check progress, shows the percentage of steps you have ticked.

An editor and a preview

Code editor is where you write, and Live preview below it renders what you have written as you go.

A project you send for review

The block headed Send your project for mentor review warns that Your current editor code will become the read-only submitted copy. You may add a Note to your mentor (optional), and you must tick This is my own work. before Submit coding project will go through.

After that, the button becomes View coding project submission and the work is tracked exactly like any other submitted work. Take a quiz and submit an assignment covers what happens next, including how a mentor can send it back for revision.

Where this leaves you

Completing a lesson advances the module percentage on the Curriculum screen, moves the Ready for your next step? card on Home to whatever is next, and may unlock the item after it. My Journey in the sidebar shows the same progress as a roadmap — 3 of 8 modules complete, which module you are in now, and your badge collection — and Badges shows each badge with the criteria that earn it.

Most modules end in something to be marked: a quiz, an assignment, or both. That is the next page.
