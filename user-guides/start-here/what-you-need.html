What you need before you start

Waypoint asks very little of you: a web browser and an account somebody else created for you. This page tells you the addresses to open, why there is no sign-up button anywhere, who is eligible to take part, and — if you are standing up a fresh install — the one command that creates the first account of all.

What Waypoint is

Waypoint is a youth leadership platform. A young person applies to a cohort, a guardian consents, a program administrator approves the application and places the applicant with a mentor, and from then on the young person works through a curriculum of lessons, reflections, quizzes and assignments while their mentor reviews the work, records check-ins and watches for anyone falling behind. Around that core the product carries attendance, a calendar, announcements, messaging, badges, reports, and an append-only audit trail. Everything you can see is decided by your role, and for young people it is decided by guardian consent as well.

A browser and two addresses

Waypoint runs entirely in a web browser. Any current desktop or mobile browser will do; there is nothing to install and nothing to configure.

There are two separate web applications, and they live at two different addresses. On a local development machine those addresses are fixed:

What it is Local address Who opens it
The portal http://localhost:4200 Everyone with an account — youth, mentors, program administrators, guardians and volunteers. This is where you sign in.
The public site http://localhost:4201 Anyone at all, with no account. It carries program information and the four public forms: apply to a cohort, contact the team, sign up to volunteer, and start a donation pledge.

Behind both of them sits the API at http://127.0.0.1:5211. You do not need to open it to use Waypoint, but two of its addresses are worth knowing when something looks wrong:

/swagger

The interactive API browser. Every endpoint the portal and public site use is listed and can be called by hand. See Integration contracts.

/health

A plain liveness check — it answers if the API process is up. There is also /health/ready, which additionally checks that the database is reachable. If a page in the portal is refusing to load anything, check these before you check anything else.

The addresses above are the local development ones. If somebody else set the system up for you, they will have given you a different address — use theirs. Everything else on this page is the same either way.

You cannot sign yourself up

There is no sign-up button, no "create an account" link, and no way to register yourself. This is deliberate: Waypoint works with young people, so every account is created by a named adult who is accountable for creating it.

Every account begins with an invitation. There are two ways in:

A Program Administrator invites you

Administrators can invite anyone, in any role. They can also approve a public application, which creates the young person's account and places them in a cohort with a mentor.

A Mentor invites you

Mentors hold the invite permission too, so a mentor can invite a young person directly into their own cohort without waiting on an administrator.

Nobody else can invite. Guardians, volunteers and young people cannot create accounts for anyone, including themselves.

Applying is not the same as having an account

Filling in the public application form records an application and nothing else. The form says so itself when you submit it: You don’t have an account yet — we’ll set one up once you’re accepted into a cohort. An account exists only once an administrator approves the application.

Invitations arrive by email as a link. That link is single use and expires after 14 days. Accepting it is covered step by step in Opening Waypoint for the first time.

Who can take part

Young people applying to a cohort must be aged 12 to 18 inclusive. The public application form states the range on screen and rejects an age outside it, so there is no point applying on behalf of a younger or older person — the answer will be the same either way.

Applicants under 18 need a parent or guardian. The application form asks for a guardian name, email and phone number, and asks the person filling it in to confirm I confirm a parent or guardian consents to participation. That checkbox is an acknowledgement on a form, not the consent itself: a real guardian consent record has to exist before the applicant can be approved into a cohort, and before the young person can open a single lesson. See Give consent and follow progress.

Adults — mentors, program administrators, guardians and volunteers — are not age-gated. They are invited directly.

The password rule

You choose your own password when you accept your invitation. There is exactly one rule, and it is the same everywhere a password is set — accepting an invitation, resetting a forgotten password, and changing your password in settings:

Use at least 8 characters, including a letter and a number.

There is no maximum length, no required symbol, no forced mixture of upper and lower case, and no expiry. Waypoint does not ask you to change your password on a schedule.

Two limits protect the sign-in form, and both are worth knowing before you meet one:

  • Five failed attempts locks the account for about 15 minutes. The lock lifts on its own; you can also reset your password to get back in sooner.
  • Sign-in and invitation endpoints allow 20 requests per minute per address. Going past that is answered with a 429 until the minute is up.

What each of those looks like on screen is in Messages and error codes.

The very first account

This section is only for whoever is standing up a fresh Waypoint install. If somebody already gave you an invitation, skip it.

A new installation starts with an empty database. There are no demo accounts, no sample cohort and no example curriculum — outside the Testing environment used by the automated test suite, nothing is seeded at all. That leaves a genuine chicken-and-egg problem: every account needs an inviter, and there is nobody to do the inviting.

The way command-line tool breaks the cycle. It creates the first Program Administrator directly, applying any pending database migrations on the way: 

way db seed --email <email> --password <password> --display-name <name>

All three options are required.

--email

The address this administrator signs in with. If an account with that address already exists, the command changes nothing and prints the existing account — it is safe to run twice.

--password

The password to sign in with. It must satisfy the same rule as every other password: at least 8 characters including a letter and a number.

--display-name

The name other people see beside this account throughout the portal.

That account holds the ProgramAdministrator role, which is the only role that can invite in every other role. From there, everyone else is invited from inside the portal. The rest of what the command line can do is in Integration contracts.

The password is on your command line

way db seed takes the password as a plain argument, so it will be visible to anyone who can see your terminal and will normally be written to your shell history. Use a throwaway password for the seed account and change it in settings once you have signed in.

What you do not need

A number of things you might reasonably expect to need are not part of Waypoint at all.

  • No app to install. There is no iOS or Android app, and nothing to download. The portal is a web page and works on a phone browser.
  • No plugin or extension. No Flash, no Java, no browser add-on, no special PDF viewer.
  • No second factor. Waypoint asks for an email address and a password. There is no authenticator app, no SMS code and no hardware key.
  • No Google account, and no sign-in with any other provider. There is no "continue with Google" button, no Google Workspace or Drive connection, and no Google Sheets sync. Your Waypoint password is a Waypoint password.
  • No numeric PIN and no account switcher. Shared-device shortcuts were considered and left out; each person signs in with their own email and password.

Before you rely on this

Not approved for production or for real participant information

Waypoint is a development project. No independent security, privacy, safeguarding, or accessibility review has been completed, and no release is approved for production use or for storing real participant information. Use synthetic data only.

That matters most on the pages where you would naturally type a real name: the public application form, the invitations screen, and roster import. Invent your people. The worked example in One complete walkthrough does exactly that.
