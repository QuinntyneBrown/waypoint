One complete walkthrough

Every screen in Word Up makes more sense once you have seen the whole path once. This page follows a single invented applicant from the public application form to a badge on an administrator's progress board, touching all five roles on the way. Each step is short and points at the page that covers it properly.

Everyone WU-UG-05

The cast and the path

Everyone below is invented. None of these names, cohorts or addresses belongs to a real person or a real program, and you should invent your own the same way — Word Up is not approved for real participant information.

Ondine Fairweather-Blask

The applicant. Fifteen years old, so a minor, which means consent gates her path in two separate places.

Marguerite Fairweather-Blask

Ondine's guardian. Gives consent, then follows progress.

Thaddeus Kestrelbrook

The mentor who will hold Ondine's caseload and review her work.

Perpetua Quillfeather

The Program Administrator who reviews, approves and places.

Willowbank autumn cohort

The cohort she is placed into, running the Bible & Leadership track.

Getting in

  1. Apply on the public site Public

    Marguerite opens the public site, chooses Apply, and fills in the form headed Join a cohort. She answers Who’s filling this out? with I’m a parent or guardian, names Willowbank autumn cohort as the cohort of interest, enters Ondine's name, age 15 and neighbourhood, picks Bible & Leadership as the track, and writes a paragraph under Why are you interested?

    Because the applicant is under 18 a guardian block appears — Required for applicants under 18. — asking for guardian name, email and phone. She ticks I confirm a parent or guardian consents to participation. and I’ve read the privacy notice., then chooses Submit application.

    What you should see

    The form is replaced by Application received, reading Thanks for applying to Word Up! We’ll review your application and reach out with next steps. You don’t have an account yet — we’ll set one up once you’re accepted into a cohort.

    Nothing exists yet except a record. There is no account, no password and no email to click.

  2. Open the application for review Program Administrator

    Perpetua chooses Applications under the People group in her sidebar. The page is headed Applications with the line Review consent, place applicants, and keep every decision traceable. Ondine's row shows her age with a Minor marker beside it. Perpetua chooses Review.

    What you should see

    A dialog headed Review application carrying the whole submission, and at the top an amber callout: Guardian participation consent required — This minor cannot be approved into active participation until a valid consent record is linked. The Approve button is unavailable.

    Applications, invitations and people covers the review dialog field by field.

  3. Record guardian consent Guardian

    Marguerite receives a secure consent link and gives participation consent for Ondine. This is the gate the previous step was waiting on, and it is the same consent that will later decide whether Ondine can open a lesson at all.

    What you should see

    Back in the review dialog the amber callout is replaced by a green one: Guardian participation consent recorded — This application is eligible for a placement decision.

    What the guardian actually reads and signs, and how consent can later lapse or be withdrawn, is in Give consent and follow progress.

  4. Approve and place Program Administrator

    In the same dialog Perpetua sets Assign cohort to Willowbank autumn cohort and Assign mentor to Thaddeus. Both are required — leaving either empty gives Choose a cohort before approval. or Choose a mentor assigned to the cohort. She adds a Decision note and chooses Approve.

    What you should see

    A confirmation dialog headed Approve application? asking Enrol this applicant in Willowbank autumn cohort with Thaddeus Kestrelbrook? and noting The applicant contact will receive an approval email. Choosing Confirm approval sets the application to Approved.

    Approval is the moment Ondine's account is created, enrolled in the cohort's track and assigned to Thaddeus. It has no password yet — the approval email tells the family that staff will follow up with access details, and the invitation in the next step is that follow-up.

    Approve and Decline are both final. Once an application carries either, reopening it offers only Done, and the API refuses a second decision with This application already has a final decision.

  5. Send the invitation Program Administrator

    Perpetua opens Invitations, uses the Send invitations card to pick the role Youth, the cohort and the mentor, types Ondine's address under Email addresses, and chooses Send invitation.

    What you should see

    A new row in the invitations table below, showing the email, the role and context, the status Pending, and an expiry date 14 days out.

    A mentor could have done this themselves — mentors hold the invite permission too. Nobody else can.

  6. Accept and onboard Youth

    Ondine opens the link and lands on a page headed Join Word Up as Youth. She sets a Display name, chooses a password twice, ticks I agree to follow the Word Up community guidelines. and chooses Join Word Up. Then three onboarding steps: Tell us about you, Your cohort — which confirms You’re all set with a cohort and mentor. because she was placed at approval — and Notifications. She chooses Finish.

    What you should see

    The youth home screen, with the sidebar reading Home, My Journey, My Progress, Curriculum, Badges, Schedule, Messages, Announcements, Profile.

    Every screen and every refusal in this step is covered in Opening Word Up for the first time.

Doing the work

  1. Open a lesson Youth

    Ondine chooses Curriculum and opens the first lesson. The page shows the module name above the lesson title, and beneath it Step 1 of 6 with an estimate in minutes. The body runs Scripture, What you’ll learn, Teaching and Think about it. She reads to the end and chooses Mark lesson complete.

    What you should see

    The button is replaced by Continue to reflection. Her position in the lesson was already being remembered as she scrolled, so closing the tab halfway would not have lost it.

    Preview limitation

    The three reading-size buttons above the lesson text all show the letter A at the same size. They are, left to right, small, medium and large; only the label a screen reader reads out tells them apart. There is a separate, permanent text-size control in Profile & settings.

    Full detail in Work through a lesson.

  2. Write a reflection Youth

    The reflection page is headed Reflect on the lesson title, with Take your time. You can save this as a private draft. Ondine picks a mood — the four options are Hopeful, Thoughtful, Unsure and Challenged — writes under Your reflection, and optionally fills in One action I will take.

    She leaves Share with my mentor switched off, which is how it arrives: Off by default. When it is off, this reflection is private to you. Turn it on only if you want your assigned mentor to read it and respond during check-ins. Then Submit reflection.

    What you should see

    A green confirmation at the top of the page. Thaddeus will see that a reflection exists, but not a word of it.

  3. Take the quiz Youth

    The quiz opens on an intro page with the eyebrow Knowledge check and the line Attempt 1 of 3, above four facts: the number of questions, the estimated minutes, the pass mark as a percentage, and the attempts remaining. If a badge is attached, a card headed Badge reward names it. She chooses Start attempt, answers, and submits.

    What you should see

    A result page headed Quiz result — but no score. Instead: Waiting for mentor approval and Your answers are submitted and locked. Your score will appear after review.

    This surprises people, so it is worth stating plainly: a quiz score is not visible to the young person until a mentor approves it. Ondine cannot see how she did until Thaddeus works his queue.

  4. Submit the assignment Youth

    The assignment page carries the eyebrow Assignment brief, a due date, a Task section, What you can submit, and a Rubric preview listing each criterion with its points — so she knows before she starts exactly what she will be marked on.

    Ondine writes under Written response, ticks This is my own work and the evidence is truthful. — leaving it clear gives Confirm this is your own work before submitting. — and chooses Submit assignment.

    What you should see

    Returning to the assignment now offers View submitted work instead of the form. The submission is Submitted and waiting.

    Both of the last two steps in full: Take a quiz and submit an assignment.

Review and outcome

  1. Work the review queue Mentor

    Thaddeus chooses Reviews in his sidebar. The page is headed Reviews with Review pending assignments, coding projects, and quizzes. Ondine's name heads a row naming the item type and title and when it was submitted, with a due badge — or Overdue if it has slipped — and a Review button.

    What you should see when the queue is empty

    No reviews waiting with the line You are caught up with your assigned youth.

  2. Grade against the rubric Mentor

    The review page shows Submitted work on one side and Rubric and outcome on the other. Thaddeus scores each criterion — a running total appears as a fraction of the possible score — writes under Feedback for the youth, and ticks Notify guardian about the outcome.

    Three buttons close the decision: Save draft keeps his marking without telling anyone, Request revision sends the work back, and Approve accepts it. He chooses Approve.

    What you should see

    The submission moves to Approved and the queue loses its row. The quiz score Ondine could not see is now released to her — the reviews page also offers Approve all quiz scores with a count, for clearing several at once.

    Review and grade submitted work covers rubrics, revisions and the submission timeline.

  3. Collect the badge Youth

    Approving the assignment satisfies an automatic badge rule, so the badge is awarded without anyone deciding to award it. Ondine chooses Badges.

    What you should see

    A page headed Badges under the eyebrow Achievements, with a count in the form 1 earned · 7 to unlock, then the sections Earned, Still to unlock and Your milestones. Before the first badge the earned section reads No badges yet — Complete your next learning step to begin your collection.

  4. See it as a guardian Guardian

    Marguerite signs in and chooses My Youth. The page is headed Child progress under the eyebrow Read-only family view, and the words are exact: she can read, and that is all.

    What you should see

    Cards for Modules, Attendance history, Approved quiz outcomes, Leadership competencies, Earned badges, Still to unlock, Upcoming schedule and Shared mentor updates.

    Note what is not there. Ondine's reflection was private, so it does not appear. The quiz shows under Approved quiz outcomes only because Thaddeus approved it — an unapproved score is invisible to the guardian exactly as it is to the young person. And Shared mentor updates means shared: a check-in Thaddeus kept private stays private.

  5. See it as the program Program Administrator

    Perpetua chooses Progress Board under the Progress group. The page is headed Progress board under the eyebrow Learning outcomes: one row per young person, one column per published module, then Quiz average and Overall, with a Cohort average row along the bottom.

    What you should see

    Ondine's row, with the module she finished filled in and the rest not started, and her quiz average now counted because the score was approved. With no published modules the board reads No published modules instead.

    The board, attendance and the exports built on them are in Attendance, the progress board and reports.

What the path teaches

Four rules did most of the work in that story, and they hold everywhere else in the product too.

Consent gates, it does not merely record

A minor cannot be approved without it and cannot open a lesson without it. If it lapses mid-programme, learning stops until it is restored.

A mentor stands between work and its outcome

Quiz scores are withheld from the young person until approved, and badges follow approval rather than submission. Nothing grades itself into visibility.

Private is the default, and sharing is a choice

Reflections start private. Check-ins start private. Both take a deliberate act to share, and sharing a check-in exposes every field on it.

Some decisions do not come back

An approved or declined application is final, and a permanently removed person cannot be reactivated. Both say so before you commit. See Lifecycles and the evidence they leave.
