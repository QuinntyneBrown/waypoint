A tour of the Word Up window

Once you are signed in, every page in the portal sits inside the same frame. This page names each part of that frame — with the exact label a screen reader announces for it — then lists the navigation every role actually gets, and what all of it becomes on a phone. The rest of the guide uses these names without re-explaining them.

Everyone WU-UG-04

The frame

The signed-in portal is one shell wrapped around whatever page you are on. It has five regions. Four of them are always there; the first appears only when you lose your connection.

1Offline banner Hidden until your device goes offline, then pushes everything else down.
2Top bar Menu button (narrow screens only), brand, notification bell, account button. Fixed to the top of the window.
3Main sidebar Your role's navigation, in groups, with Sign out pinned at the bottom.
4Main content The page itself. Everything else on this diagram stays put while this changes.
5 Bottom navigation and toasts The bottom nav appears on narrow screens for three roles. Toasts float above the bottom-right corner at any width.

Two things on that diagram are conditional rather than universal: the offline banner only appears when you are offline, and the bottom navigation only appears on a narrow screen and only for some roles. Everything else is on every page you will ever see in the portal.

Each part, named precisely

The middle column is what assistive technology announces. If you use a screen reader, those are the names you navigate by; if you are writing a bug report, they are the names worth quoting.

Part Accessible name What it does
Offline banner — A strip across the very top reading You’re offline — changes will save when you reconnect. It appears by itself when the browser reports no connection and disappears the moment the connection returns. Attendance is the only thing you can genuinely keep doing while it is showing.
Menu button Menu The leftmost button in the top bar. Hidden on a wide screen; below 960px it appears and opens the Main sidebar over the page.
Brand — The W mark and the words Word Up. It is a link, and it goes to your role's home — youth to Home, mentors to Dashboard, administrators to Dashboard, guardians to Home, volunteers to Today. This is the quickest way back from anywhere.
Notification bell Notifications A bell icon on the right of the top bar. For youth and mentors it opens their Notifications page. For the other three roles, see the limitation below.
Account button Account Your avatar, at the far right. Opens and closes the account menu; while the menu is open the button reports itself as expanded.
Main sidebar Main The vertical navigation down the left. Its contents are decided entirely by your role. The item for the page you are on is marked as the current page.
Bottom navigation Primary A row of large targets fixed to the bottom of a narrow screen, carrying a short subset of the sidebar. Youth, guardians and volunteers get one; mentors and administrators do not.
Main content — The page. Everything the rest of this guide describes — lessons, review queues, rosters, reports — happens here.
Toast host Notifications Where confirmations like Display settings saved. appear briefly. It is a polite live region, so a screen reader announces a toast without interrupting what it was already saying. Each toast has a Dismiss button.

The bell and the toast host share the accessible name Notifications. They are unrelated: the bell is a link to a page of stored notifications, the toast host is a transient corner of the screen. If a screen reader announces "Notifications" twice, that is why.

Preview limitation

For Program Administrators, Guardians and Volunteers, the notification bell does not reach a notifications page. It redirects instead — administrators and guardians land on Announcements, volunteers land on Today. Only youth and mentors have a real Notifications page behind the bell. If you hold one of the other three roles, treat the bell as a shortcut to news, not as an inbox.

The account menu

Choosing the Account button opens a small menu under it. It has exactly four rows, and the first one is not a button.

Your name and role

Your avatar, your display name in bold, and your role label underneath. This row does nothing when you click it — it is there to tell you which account you are using. The role labels are shown below.

Profile & settings

Your profile, notification preferences, display and accessibility preferences, and your own privacy options. See Accessibility and display preferences.

Help

In-product help.

Sign out

Ends your session. The same action is available at the foot of the Main sidebar, so you never have to hunt for it.

The role label under your name is not the role name used elsewhere in this guide. It is a friendlier display string, and the two do not always match:

Role Label under your name
Youth Youth Leader
Mentor Mentor
Program Administrator Director
Guardian Guardian
Volunteer Volunteer

So an administrator sees Director and a young person sees Youth Leader. Nothing about their permissions changes; only the word does. The permissions themselves are in Roles and permissions.

What each role sees in the sidebar

These are the complete navigation lists, in order, exactly as they are labelled. Headings in bold below the role name are group headings in the sidebar — they are text, not links. Sign out is pinned to the foot of all five and is not repeated in each list.

Youth

One flat list, no groups: Home, My Journey, My Progress, Curriculum, Badges, Schedule, Messages, Announcements, Profile.

Profile is the odd one out: it leaves the youth area and opens your settings, the same place the account menu's Profile & settings goes.

Mentor

Seven items, then one group. Dashboard, My Youth, Invitations, Check-ins, Reviews, Attendance, At-risk. Then under the heading Program: Assign work, Calendar, Messages, Notifications.

Note that At-risk is the label in the sidebar for what the rest of the product calls flags, and Assign work sits under Program rather than with the other assessment items.

Program Administrator

By far the longest: one item, then five groups. Dashboard stands alone at the top.

Group heading Items, in order
People People, Import Roster, Mentor Assignments, Cohorts, Applications, Invitations, Users & Roles
Progress Progress Board, Reports, Attendance
Curriculum Curriculum, Badges, Progression
Program Calendar, Announcements
System Settings, Audit Log, Privacy requests

People and Users & Roles are two different screens that both list people: the first is the directory of everyone in the program, the second is where roles are changed. Both are covered in Applications, invitations and people.

Guardian

Four items, no groups: Home, My Youth, News, Consent.

Volunteer

Three items, no groups: Today, Attendance, Schedule. This is the shortest navigation in the product, and deliberately so.

Preview limitation

For volunteers, the Attendance item — in the sidebar and in the bottom navigation — does not open a roster. It returns you to Today. To actually take attendance, start from Today and choose Take attendance on the session you are running. See Run today's session.

Settings inside the shell

Profile & settings opens a page with its own second-level navigation inside the main content area: a small sidebar listing Profile, Notifications, Display and Privacy, and a row of tabs above the content showing the same four.

Preview limitation

The settings sidebar always highlights Profile, whichever settings page you are actually on. If you are editing your display preferences, the sidebar will still be showing Profile as selected. The tabs above the content are correct — use those to tell where you are.

On a narrow screen

The layout changes at 960 pixels wide. Below that — most phones, most tablets in portrait, and a desktop browser window dragged narrow — three things happen.

  1. The sidebar collapses behind the Menu button

    The Main sidebar slides off the left edge and the Menu button appears in the top bar. Choosing it slides the sidebar back in over the page, with a dark scrim behind it; choosing any item, or the scrim, closes it again.

    What you should see

    The same navigation, unchanged and in the same order — the sidebar is hidden, not shortened. Nothing is dropped on a phone.

  2. Three roles gain a bottom navigation

    Youth, guardians and volunteers get a fixed row of large targets across the bottom of the screen — the fastest way between the handful of places they go most. Mentors and Program Administrators get no bottom navigation at all and reach everything through the Menu button.

    Role Bottom navigation, in order
    Youth Home, My Journey, Curriculum, Messages, Profile
    Guardian Home, My Youth, News, Consent — all four sidebar items
    Volunteer Today, Attendance, Schedule — all three sidebar items
    Mentor None
    Program Administrator None

    The youth bottom navigation is a subset, not the whole list: My Progress, Badges, Schedule and Announcements are only in the sidebar.

  3. The page makes room for it

    Where a bottom navigation exists, the main content gains padding at the foot so the last thing on the page is never hidden underneath it.

Moving around by keyboard

Tab moves forward through the shell in the order you would expect: Menu button if it is visible, brand, bell, account button, then the sidebar items top to bottom, then Sign out, then the page content. Shift + Tab goes back.

Preview limitation

The portal has no skip link. The public site has one — Skip to content as the first thing you reach — but the signed-in portal does not, so a keyboard user has to tab through the whole top bar and the whole sidebar to get to the page. On the Program Administrator navigation, which is over twenty items long, that is a real cost on every page load.

Keyboard behaviour inside the page varies, and most of it is ordinary Tab movement. The handful of places where arrow keys, Escape or Ctrl + Enter do something specific are listed in Accessibility and display preferences.
