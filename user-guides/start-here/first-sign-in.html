Opening Waypoint for the first time

Getting in has four parts: the invitation email, the page that turns that invitation into an account, three short onboarding questions, and — if you are under 18 — a consent gate your guardian has to clear before lessons open. This page walks all four, and covers the sign-in page and password reset you will use every time after that.

Everyone W-UG-03

The shape of getting in

You never create your own account in Waypoint. Somebody — a Program Administrator or a Mentor — invites you, and the link in that invitation is what brings the account into existence. Everything on this page follows from that one fact.

Accept your invitation

  1. Open the link in the invitation email

    The email contains a single link carrying a token. Opening it takes you to the accept page, which checks the token before it shows you anything.

    What you should see

    Briefly, the line Checking your invitation… under the Waypoint brand mark, then either the join form or one of the four refusal screens below.

  2. Check the heading names your role

    A valid invitation is headed Join Waypoint as {role} — for example Join Waypoint as Youth or Join Waypoint as Mentor. Underneath, a line confirms which invitation this is: your email address with most of it masked, and, when the invitation carried them, the cohort name and the mentor's name separated by middots.

    If the role is not the one you expected, stop and go back to whoever invited you. The role is fixed by the invitation and you cannot change it here.

  3. Fill in the three fields

    Display name is the name other people see beside your work throughout Waypoint. Leaving it empty gives you Enter the name you want people to see.

    Create password and Confirm password must match each other and satisfy the one password rule. A password that falls short gives you Use at least 8 characters with a letter and a number., and two passwords that differ give you The passwords do not match. at the top of the card. The eye button beside each password field shows and hides what you typed; it is labelled Show password and, once pressed, Hide password.

  4. Agree to the community guidelines

    Tick I agree to follow the Waypoint community guidelines. This is not optional — leaving it clear gives you Agreement is required to join. and the form will not submit.

  5. Choose Join Waypoint

    The button reads Join Waypoint, and changes to Creating account… while it works.

    What you should see

    Your account now exists and you are signed in. If you were invited as a young person you land on the onboarding wizard headed Tell us about you. Every other role lands on their role's home screen.

Accepting is the moment the account is created, so it is also the moment your password first exists. There is nothing to "activate" afterwards and no confirmation email to click.

When the link does not work

Invitation links are single use and expire after 14 days. When one cannot be used, the accept page replaces the form with a short explanation rather than an error code. There are exactly four headings, and which one you get tells you what happened.

Heading What happened What to do
This invite link has expired More than 14 days passed between the invitation being sent and you opening it. Request a new one from the same page.
This invite is no longer active Whoever invited you revoked the invitation before you accepted it. Ask them directly — a revoked invitation was withdrawn on purpose, so request a replacement only if you expected one.
This invite has already been used The link was accepted once already. Your account exists. Sign in normally. This screen adds Your account already exists. Sign in with the password you created. and offers no replacement button, because you do not need one.
This invite link is not valid The token is missing or unrecognised — usually a link that was truncated by an email client, or one pasted incompletely. Re-copy the whole link from the email, then request a new one if it still fails.

All four screens carry the same explanation underneath the heading: For your security, invite links are single-use and expire after 14 days.

  1. Ask for a replacement

    Choose Request a new invite. The button reads Requesting… while it works.

    What you should see

    The button is replaced by If a replacement can be sent, the person who invited you has been notified.

That wording is careful on purpose. You get the same acknowledgement whether or not a replacement can actually be sent, so the page never confirms to a stranger that a particular invitation or address exists. If nothing arrives, contact whoever invited you rather than pressing the button again.

Both screens also offer Back to sign in, which is the right choice if you already have an account.

Onboarding: three short steps

Young people go straight from accepting an invitation into a three-step wizard. The card is headed Set up your profile with a Step 1 of 3 counter opposite it, and Back and Next buttons at the foot.

  1. Step 1 — Tell us about you Youth

    Under the line This is how you’ll show up across Waypoint. there are three fields. Display name is required and is pre-filled from your invitation — leaving it empty gives Please enter a display name. and blocks Next. Pronouns is optional and free text; the field suggests e.g. she/her, he/him, they/them. Profile photo is optional too, described as Optional JPG or PNG, up to 5 MB. Stored privately.

    What you should see

    A chosen photo uploads immediately: Uploading profile photo… then Profile photo ready. and a round preview. A wrong file type gives Choose a JPG or PNG image. and one over the limit gives Profile photo must be 5 MB or smaller.

  2. Step 2 — Your cohort Youth

    This step tells you something; it does not ask you anything. There is nothing to fill in and no choice to make, because cohorts are assigned by your program team — you don’t pick one.

    Which of two messages you get depends on whether you have been placed yet.

    What you should see if you are placed

    You’re placed in a cohort. Your mentor will guide your journey. above a green callout reading You’re all set with a cohort and mentor.

    What you should see if you are not

    The line about cohorts being assigned by the program team, above a blue callout reading You’re not placed in a cohort yet. Your mentor will add you soon — program content unlocks once you’re placed.

    If you get the second message, finishing onboarding is still the right thing to do. Placement happens on the administrator's side and appears without you doing anything.

  3. Step 3 — Notifications Youth

    A list of categories, each with a short description and an on/off switch, under the reassurance You can change these anytime in settings. The categories relevant to you as a young person are Lesson reminders, Mentor messages, Session reminders, Work graded, Badges earned and Announcements.

    Everything actionable starts switched on; Announcements — described as Program-wide news and updates. — starts switched off. These choices are in-app notifications only, and they are saved when you finish.

    The button at the foot now reads Finish.

    What you should see

    Choosing Finish saves your preferences, marks onboarding complete, and lands you on your youth home screen.

Skipping, and coming back

A Skip for now link sits at the bottom of all three steps. It takes you straight to your home screen and marks onboarding as dealt with, so the wizard will not reappear the next time you sign in.

Skipping costs you nothing permanent. Your display name, pronouns and photo live in Profile & settings, and your notification choices live under Notifications in the same place — you can set all of them later. What skipping does not do is change anything about your cohort or your consent status.

If you close the browser mid-wizard, signing in again brings you back to it, with your display name and pronouns as you left them. Onboarding is resumable right up until you finish or skip.

Signing in from then on

Every visit after the first starts at the sign-in page. It is headed Welcome back with the line Sign in to continue your journey. beneath it.

  1. Enter your email and password

    The fields are Email and Password. An address that is not an address gives Enter a valid email address., and an empty password gives Enter your password. — both before anything is sent anywhere.

  2. Decide about Keep me signed in

    The checkbox Keep me signed in keeps your session alive between visits instead of ending it when you close the browser. Ticking it reveals a warning directly underneath: Not recommended on shared or public devices.

    Take that literally. On a shared laptop, a library machine or a family tablet, leave it clear.

  3. Choose Sign in
    What you should see

    Your role's home screen. Young people who have not finished onboarding are taken back to the wizard instead, and young people who need guardian consent are taken to the consent gate described below.

When sign-in refuses

Three refusals look different from each other, and each means something specific.

A red message on the sign-in card

The email or the password is wrong. The wording is identical for an unknown address and a wrong password, on purpose: the page never reveals whether an account exists. Check both, then use Forgot password?

Too many sign-in attempts

A separate page headed Account locked, reading This account is temporarily locked after several failed attempts. Try again in about 15 minutes, or reset your password. Five failed attempts trigger it and the lock lifts by itself.

This account is inactive

A page headed Account inactive, reading Your account isn’t active right now. Please contact your program director to restore access. An administrator deactivated the account. No amount of retrying or password resetting will change this — you have to ask them.

Being signed out again

Waypoint signs you out after 30 minutes of inactivity. You arrive at a page headed You’ve been signed out: For your security we signed you out after a period of inactivity. Any work in progress has been saved — sign in to pick up where you left off.

Preview limitation

There is no warning before the idle sign-out — no countdown, no "are you still there" prompt. You find out by being signed out. Unsaved form content is captured first and restored after you sign back in, but do not rely on that for a long piece of writing: save a reflection draft before you walk away.

Resetting a forgotten password

Reset runs end to end without anyone else's help. It has four screens.

  1. Ask for a link

    Choose Forgot password? at the foot of the sign-in card. The page is headed Reset your password, with Enter your email and we’ll send a link to set a new one. Type your address and choose Send reset link.

  2. Check your email

    You land on a page with the eyebrow Reset link sent and the heading Check your email.

    What you should see

    If an account exists for that address, we’ve emailed a link to reset your password. The link expires in 1 hour. — with your address partly masked in place of "that address" when the page knows it. Two buttons: Resend link and Back to sign in. Resending shows the toast Reset link sent again.

    As with invitations, this page says the same thing whether or not an account exists. Seeing it is not confirmation that your address is registered.

  3. Set the new password

    The link in the email opens a page headed Set a new password, with Choose a strong password you don’t use elsewhere. Fill in New password and Confirm password. The rule is shown under the first field as Use at least 8 characters, including a letter and a number. and turns red if what you typed does not meet it; two that differ give Passwords do not match. The Reset password button stays disabled until both are satisfied.

  4. Sign in with it
    What you should see

    A page with the eyebrow All set and the heading Your password was updated, reading For your security we signed out your other sessions. Sign in with your new password.

    That last sentence is literal: resetting your password ends every other session on every other device. If you were signed in on a phone, you will have to sign in there again.

If the reset link has gone stale

Reset links last one hour and work once. A link that is past its hour, already used, or malformed takes you to a page headed This link is invalid or expired: Reset links can only be used once and expire after an hour. Request a fresh link to continue. Choose Request a new link and start again from the first step.

The guardian consent gate

If you are a young person under 18 with a linked guardian, one more thing stands between you and your first lesson. Waypoint will not let you start learning until a valid guardian participation consent record exists. You can sign in, and you can look around, but the moment you try to open program content you meet the gate.

The page carries the eyebrow Consent needed and the heading A guardian needs to sign off:

Before you can start lessons, a parent or guardian needs to give participation consent. It only takes a minute — send them a secure link, or have them sign now.

There are three buttons, and they do three different things.

Notify my guardian

Sends a secure consent link to the guardian contact already on your record. When it succeeds, a green line appears naming the channel and destination it went to. Use this when your guardian is not with you.

My guardian can sign now

Signs you out and hands the browser to your guardian at the sign-in page, so they can sign in with their own account and land directly on their consent screen. Use this when they are sitting beside you.

Sign out

Leaves Waypoint. Nothing is consented to and nothing is lost.

A young person with no linked guardian is not gated. Waypoint treats them as an adult participant and lessons open normally. The gate exists for minors with a guardian on record.

Consent is not a one-off form — it can lapse or be withdrawn later, and if it does you will meet this same page again mid-programme. What your guardian actually sees and signs, and how withdrawal works, is covered in Give consent and follow progress.

If something else appears

A handful of other pages can interrupt sign-in. They are all short, and none of them is a dead end.

Heading What it means
You don’t have access to that You reached an address meant for a different role. Waypoint will not let a mentor open an administrator screen or a youth open a mentor one.
Something went wrong An unexpected server error — nothing you did caused it. Try again in a moment.
We’ll be right back Waypoint is briefly down for maintenance. Nothing you have done is lost.
No connection right now Your device is offline. Anything already loaded stays available and Waypoint reconnects on its own.
Page not found The address moved or never existed — usually a mistyped or truncated link.

The exact wording of every one of these, along with the HTTP status behind it, is in Messages and error codes. If you would rather start from what you can see on screen, go to Troubleshooting by symptom.
