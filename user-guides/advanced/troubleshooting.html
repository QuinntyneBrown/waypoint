Troubleshooting by symptom

Every heading on this page is something you can see on your own screen, not something the system did behind it. Find the sentence that matches what Word Up is telling you, and you will get the reason and the fix. The last two sections list every known limitation in this preview, and how to reach a person.

Everyone WU-UG-21

How to use this page

Word Up nearly always tells you what is wrong in words, on the screen where it went wrong. Read that sentence first and match it here — it is faster than guessing, and it is usually the whole answer.

For a complete list of every message and its status code, see Messages and error codes. This page covers what to actually do about them.

Two habits that solve most problems. First, read the message rather than skipping it — almost every one names the fix. Second, if you have to report something, include the correlation identifier described in Something went wrong on our end. Without it, nobody can find your request in the logs.

Signing in and getting access

I can't sign in and it says my email or password is wrong

What you see. A red alert on the sign-in screen reading Invalid email or password.

Why it happens. The email and password do not match an account. Word Up says the same thing whether the email is unknown or the password is wrong — on purpose, so that the screen cannot be used to discover whether somebody has an account.

What to do. Check the email address for a typo first; it is the more common mistake. Passwords need at least 8 characters including at least one letter and one number, so if yours is shorter than that you are not remembering the right one. If you are still stuck, use Forgot password rather than guessing again — each wrong attempt counts towards a lockout.

If you have never set a password, you do not have an account yet. An application is not an account: an account exists only once an administrator approves your application and you accept the invitation email.

It says my account is temporarily locked

What you see. Either the message This account is temporarily locked. Try again in about 15 minutes, or reset your password. or a full page headed Too many sign-in attempts under the eyebrow Account locked, reading This account is temporarily locked after several failed attempts. Try again in about 15 minutes, or reset your password.

Why it happens. Five failed sign-in attempts in a row lock the account for about fifteen minutes. This protects the account from someone guessing at it.

What to do. Wait it out, or reset your password — a successful reset gets you back in without waiting. Do not keep trying: further attempts during the lock do not help. If you were not the one attempting to sign in, tell your program administrator, because someone else was.

It says my account is inactive

What you see. The message This account is inactive. Please contact your program director. or the page This account is inactive under the eyebrow Account inactive, reading Your account isn’t active right now. Please contact your program director to restore access.

Why it happens. An administrator has deactivated the account. This is a deliberate act, usually when someone leaves the program or a placement changes.

What to do. Contact your program administrator. Only they can reactivate the account, and no amount of retrying or password resetting will do it. Note that this is not the same as removal: a deactivated account can be restored, whereas a removed one cannot.

My invitation link doesn't work

What you see. Instead of the join form, one of four headings, always followed by the line For your security, invite links are single-use and expire after 14 days.

Heading What happened What to do
This invite link has expired More than 14 days have passed since it was sent. Choose Request a new invite.
This invite is no longer active An administrator revoked it, usually because something about the placement changed. Choose Request a new invite, then check with your program administrator that you should still be joining.
This invite has already been used The account already exists. You see the extra line Your account already exists. Sign in with the password you created. Go to Back to sign in. If you do not know the password, reset it.
This invite link is not valid The link is malformed or unknown — most often a link broken across two lines by an email client, or one that was copied incompletely. Copy the whole link again from the original email. If it still fails, request a new invite.

What to expect after requesting. You get If a replacement can be sent, the person who invited you has been notified. That wording is careful on purpose: it does not confirm whether an invitation exists for your address, so the page cannot be used to probe for accounts. Wait for a fresh email rather than requesting repeatedly.

My password reset link doesn't work

What you see. A red alert on the Set a new password screen reading This reset link is invalid or has expired.

Why it happens. Reset links are single use and time limited. Three things produce this message: the link expired, it was already used, or it is not a link Word Up recognises. If you requested several resets in a row, only the newest link works — the earlier emails are already dead.

What to do. Start again from Forgot password and use the newest email, opening it in full rather than copying part of the address bar. Do not request many resets in quick succession: password-reset endpoints are rate limited at 20 requests per 60 seconds and you will lock yourself out of the recovery path as well.

It says a guardian needs to sign off

What you see. A page headed A guardian needs to sign off under the eyebrow Consent needed, reading Before you can start lessons, a parent or guardian needs to give participation consent. It only takes a minute — send them a secure link, or have them sign now. with three buttons: Notify my guardian, My guardian can sign now and Sign out.

Why it happens. You are a young person with a linked guardian, and there is no currently valid participation consent. That covers four situations: consent was never given, it was withdrawn, it expired, or a new term began while your organisation requires re-consent each term.

What to do. If your guardian is with you, choose My guardian can sign now and let them sign on your device. Otherwise choose Notify my guardian, which emails them a secure link, and check back once they have signed. Access returns immediately after a valid consent is recorded — there is no overnight wait.

This is a gate, not a bug, and nobody in the program can wave you through it. If your guardian says they already signed, the likely cause is a new term. See Consent over time.

I was signed out while I was working

What you see. A page headed You’ve been signed out under the eyebrow Session expired, reading For your security we signed you out after a period of inactivity. Any work in progress has been saved — sign in to pick up where you left off.

Why it happens. Thirty minutes without activity signs you out. Reading counts as inactivity: a long lesson left open on screen, or a form you walked away from mid-sentence, will both trip it.

What to do. Choose Sign in again. Word Up captures what you had typed into ordinary form fields before it navigates you away, and restores it after you sign back in, returning you to the page you were on. Passwords and file selections are deliberately not captured, so you will need to re-attach any file.

Preview limitation

There is no warning before this happens and no countdown — you are simply signed out. Save long pieces of writing as you go. See Idle sign-out gives no warning.

It says I don't have access to that page

What you see. A page headed You don’t have access to that under the eyebrow Error 403, reading This area is for a different role. If you think this is a mistake, switch to the right account or head back to your home.

Why it happens. Your role does not permit that screen. Word Up has five roles and each one sees a different product — mentors cannot reach administration screens, volunteers cannot reach mentoring ones, and only Program Administrators reach the audit log and privacy requests.

What to do. Head back to your home and use the sidebar rather than a saved link; bookmarks and pasted URLs from a colleague with a different role are the usual cause. If you genuinely need that area, ask a Program Administrator — a role change takes effect at your next sign-in, because changing a role ends existing sessions.

Be aware that refused attempts are recorded. Word Up writes an audit entry naming you and the address you tried, so repeatedly poking at an area you cannot reach is visible. That is a reason to ask rather than retry, not a reason to worry about an honest mistake.

See Roles and permissions for exactly which role reaches what.

It says too many requests

What you see. Too many requests. Please try again later.

Why it happens. Sign-in, invitation and password-reset endpoints accept 20 requests per 60 seconds from one address. Beyond that, requests are rejected with a 429 until the window resets. On a shared connection — a youth centre, a school, a church hall — everybody counts towards the same limit, so this can trip through no fault of yours.

What to do. Wait a minute and try once. The response includes a Retry-After value telling a client how long to wait. Repeatedly resubmitting only keeps the window full. If a whole room hits this while signing in together, stagger them rather than having everyone press the button at once.

Learning and submitting

My lesson or quiz is locked

What you see. On Curriculum, a module or item showing the badge Locked instead of a percentage or status, with an explanation of what unlocks it underneath.

Why it happens. Curriculum runs in order, as the page itself says: Follow your modules in order and see what unlocks next. An item stays locked until the work before it is complete. Content that is still Draft or In review is not visible to you at all — only published content appears.

What to do. Read the unlock condition shown with the locked item and finish that first. If the condition looks already met, refresh the page: completion updates as you go, but a page you have had open for a while can be stale. If it still will not open, ask your mentor — they can see your progress record and whether something is genuinely stuck.

I've used all my quiz attempts

What you see. The quiz will not start, and the message is No attempts remaining. Above the quiz you can see your position as Attempt followed by a number of the total allowed.

Why it happens. Each quiz has an attempt limit set when it was authored, and you have used them all.

What to do. Talk to your mentor. Your recorded score is the best passing attempt within the limit, so earlier attempts still count for you. There is no self-service way to reset attempts — that is the point of a limit — and asking your mentor is the correct path, not a workaround.

A related message, This attempt has already been submitted., means something different: that one attempt is closed. Once you submit, answers are locked and cannot be edited.

My quiz result doesn't show a score

What you see. A card headed Waiting for mentor approval reading Your answers are submitted and locked. Your score will appear after review.

Why it happens. This is working exactly as designed. A quiz score is not visible to you until a mentor has approved the assessment. Marking is not purely mechanical — short answers need a human — and the design keeps a person in the loop rather than handing a young person a bare number.

What to do. Nothing. There is no button to press and nothing is broken. Once your mentor approves it, the same page shows Approved score with your percentage, whether you Passed or are Not passed yet, the pass mark, and any badge you earned. If it has been sitting for a long time, message your mentor.

My assignment won't submit

What you see. Either the form refuses to send, the message Confirm this is your own work before submitting., or an upload that is rejected.

Why it happens. Three separate rules, and it is usually the first.

  • The attestation is required. You must tick This is my own work and the evidence is truthful. — on a coding assignment the shorter This is my own work. — before the form will submit. It is not optional and there is no way past it.
  • Only the allowed kinds of evidence. Each assignment lists what it accepts under What you can submit, which can be any mix of Written response, Photo (JPG or PNG), File (PDF) and Evidence link. Only the fields for the allowed kinds appear, so if you cannot find a place to attach a file, that assignment does not accept one.
  • Size and format limits. A photo must be JPG or PNG, up to 10 MB. A file must be a PDF, up to 10 MB.

What to do. Tick the attestation. Check your file against the stated type and size — a photo straight from a modern phone can exceed 10 MB, so scale it down. If the message is This assignment has already been submitted. then your work is already in; open View submitted work to see it. If your mentor asked for changes, use the resubmit path rather than submitting again; a resubmission is refused with This submission is not awaiting revisions. unless the submission is genuinely in that state.

My submission changed while I was reviewing it

What you see. When you save a grade: This submission changed after you opened it. Review the latest version before saving again.

Why it happens. The submission was modified between the moment you opened it and the moment you saved — usually the young person resubmitted, or another mentor graded it first. Word Up refuses the save rather than letting your decision silently overwrite work you have not seen.

What to do. Reload the submission and read it again before deciding. Your feedback was written against the old version, and it may no longer be fair or even relevant. If you and another mentor are both working the same queue, agree who owns which youth — see Review and grade submitted work.

Attendance

My attendance won't save

What you see. One of several notices above the roster, depending on what is happening.

Message What it means
Attendance saved. Saved to the server. Nothing to do.
Saved offline — … attendance updates waiting to sync. You are offline. Your marks are held on the device and will send themselves when the connection returns. A pill reading … pending sync shows the count.
Syncing … attendance updates… The queue is sending now.
All attendance changes synced. The queue is empty. You are done.
A queued attendance change needs review before it can sync. Something in the queue conflicts and is waiting on your decision.

Why it happens. Attendance is the one part of Word Up that works offline, because a hall with poor signal is a normal working condition. Marks are queued on the device and sent when the connection comes back. A conflict occurs when someone else saved that session on the server while you were offline.

What to do about a conflict. You get a choice headed Choose which attendance to keep. For a mentor the explanation is A newer server edit conflicts with your queued change. Review before choosing.; for a volunteer it is Someone saved this session after you went offline. Review the current roster, then apply your queued selections or keep the newer server attendance. Read the current roster first, then choose Apply my changes to overwrite with your queued marks, or Keep server attendance to discard yours and accept theirs — which confirms with Kept the newer server attendance and removed the offline conflict.

Do not guess. If two people took the same register, the person who was in the room should decide.

Preview limitation

You cannot tell by looking which attendance status is selected — the buttons look identical whether or not you chose them. Use the tally counts above the list to confirm what you marked. See Attendance status buttons have no visual selected state.

Also remember that offline support stops at attendance. There is no offline lesson reading, reflection drafting, quiz taking or submission — if you lose connection during any of those, you will see the No connection right now page instead.

Things that look broken

The page says Word Up is down for maintenance

What you see. A page headed We’ll be right back under the eyebrow Scheduled maintenance, reading Word Up is down for a short bit of maintenance. Nothing you’ve done is lost — please check again shortly. A client talking to the API directly gets a 503 whose body reads Word Up is down for scheduled maintenance. with the detail Nothing you've done is lost. Please check again shortly.

Why it happens. An operator has deliberately put Word Up into maintenance mode, usually to deploy an update. Everything is refused while it is on.

What to do. Wait and try again — the response asks clients to retry after two minutes. Nothing you had already saved is affected. Anything you were part-way through typing was not saved, so redo it once you are back in. If it persists for much longer than a deployment should take, contact your program administrator rather than assuming it will clear itself.

Something went wrong on our end

What you see. A page headed Something went wrong under the eyebrow Error 500, reading We hit a snag on our end — nothing you did caused it. Please try again in a moment.

Why it happens. An unexpected failure on the server. The message is honest: it is not something you did, and the page deliberately shows no technical detail — Word Up never puts a stack trace in front of a user.

What to do — and how to report it usefully. Try once more; some failures are transient. If it happens again, report it, and include the correlation identifier. Every response carries an X-Correlation-ID header, and on a 500 the same value comes back in the body as correlationId. It is the single thing that lets someone find your exact request in the server logs.

  1. Capture the identifier

    Copy the correlation identifier if it is shown. If you cannot see it, take a screenshot of the whole window and note the time as precisely as you can — to the minute.

  2. Note what you were doing

    Which screen you were on, which button you pressed, and what you expected to happen. "Grading a submission for a youth in the Tuesday cohort, pressed Approve" is findable; "it broke" is not.

  3. Send it through Contact support

    Use the form described in Getting help and choose A technical problem.

    What you should see

    A confirmation headed Thanks — we’ve got your message with the line Our team will follow up at the email you gave us. We usually reply within a couple of days.

Do not repeat the failing action many times hoping it will take. If it is failing, each attempt adds another log entry to sift and may compound the problem.

The page can't be found

What you see. Page not found under the eyebrow Error 404, reading The page you’re looking for moved or never existed. Let’s get you back on track.

Why it happens. The address does not match any screen. Usually a stale bookmark, a mistyped address, or a link to something that has since been removed.

What to do. Navigate from the sidebar instead of from the address bar. If a link inside Word Up itself led you here, that is worth reporting.

It says I'm offline

What you see. The banner You’re offline — changes will save when you reconnect. across the top of the window, or a page headed No connection right now under the eyebrow You’re offline, reading Check your internet connection. Anything already loaded stays available, and we’ll reconnect automatically.

Why it happens. Your device has lost its network connection.

What to do. Check the connection. You do not need to reload — Word Up reconnects on its own. Anything already on screen stays readable. Only attendance can be captured while offline; everything else waits.

Known preview limitations

These are known, verified and currently true. Each one is a control that appears to work and does not, or an interface behaviour that will mislead you. None of them is worth reporting — they are already known — but each has a workaround, and knowing them will save you a lot of confusion.

The volunteer Attendance nav item goes to Today

Preview limitation

For volunteers, choosing Attendance in the sidebar or the bottom navigation redirects you to Today. It does not open a roster.

Workaround. Reach a roster from Today by choosing Take attendance on the session you are running. See Run today's session.

The notification bell does not reach notifications for some roles

Preview limitation

The bell in the top bar does not open a notifications page for Program Administrators, Guardians or Volunteers. It redirects to Announcements for administrators and guardians, and to Today for volunteers. Youth and Mentors do reach Notifications.

Workaround. If you are an administrator or guardian, read Announcements directly from the sidebar; that is where the content you were looking for lives anyway. Email notifications, where you have them switched on, are unaffected.

Attendance status buttons have no visual selected state

Preview limitation

Present, Late, Excused and Absent look identical whether or not you have chosen them. Only assistive technology reports which is pressed.

Workaround. Use the tally counts above the list. They update as you mark, so a register of twelve should end with the four counts adding to twelve. Count before you save, every time — this is the limitation most likely to produce a wrong record.

Some icons render as blank space

Preview limitation

On several screens an icon renders as an empty gap rather than a symbol. It affects sign-in, forgot password, reset password, guardian consent and guardian home, mentor assign-work and check-ins, volunteer today and schedule, youth curriculum and home, onboarding, and the guardian consent-link page.

Workaround. Go by the text label beside the gap; every control that is missing its icon still has its words. Nothing is disabled — it is only the picture that is missing.

Screen-reader-only status text is visible

Preview limitation

Status announcements meant only for screen readers render as ordinary text. It affects mentor reviews, submission review, quiz review, check-ins, flags, attendance history, youth messages and youth notifications.

Workaround. Ignore the stray line. It duplicates something already on the page and never carries information you cannot see elsewhere.

The settings sidebar always highlights Profile

Preview limitation

Inside Profile & settings, the sidebar highlights Profile even when you are on Notifications, Display or Privacy.

Workaround. Trust the tabs above the content and the page heading, which do show where you really are. If the heading reads Privacy & data rights, you are on the privacy page whatever the sidebar suggests.

Three tab strips do not respond to arrow keys

Preview limitation

On the mentor youth record, the admin reports hub and attendance history, arrow keys do not move between tabs. Arrow keys, Home and End work only on the youth My progress tab strip.

Workaround. Use Tab to move through those three, and Enter or Space to activate.

The three lesson reading-size buttons all show the letter A

Preview limitation

In a lesson, the three reading-size buttons are visually identical — each shows the letter A. Only their accessible labels distinguish them.

Workaround. They are, in order, small, medium and large: left is smallest, right is largest. If you want a text-size change that applies everywhere and persists, use Text size under Display & accessibility instead — see Accessibility and display preferences.

The portal has no skip link

Preview limitation

The signed-in portal has no Skip to content link, so keyboard users must tab through the header and sidebar to reach the main area on every page. The public site does have one.

Workaround. If your browser or screen reader offers landmark navigation, jump straight to the main landmark. Otherwise, expect a run of tab stops through the navigation first.

Idle sign-out gives no warning

Preview limitation

After 30 minutes of inactivity you are signed out with no countdown and no chance to stay signed in.

Workaround. Save long pieces of writing as you go rather than composing in one sitting. Word Up does capture what you had typed into ordinary form fields and restores it after you sign back in, but passwords and attached files are not captured. See I was signed out while I was working.

Getting help

If nothing on this page matches, there are two places to go, and they are both inside Word Up.

The Help centre

Open the account menu and choose Help. The page is headed How can we help? under the eyebrow Help center, with the line Search answers written for your Word Up role. The articles you see are filtered to your role, so a mentor and a youth searching the same word get different answers.

Search using the box at the top. Answers open in place. If nothing matches you get No help articles found with the suggestion Try a different word, or contact the support team. At the foot of the page, Still need a hand? takes you to the support form with Contact support.

The Contact support form

The form is headed Contact support and asks four things. The first is What’s this about?, and the categories are exactly:

Account & sign-in

Lockouts, invitations, password resets, an inactive account — anything that stops you getting in.

A technical problem

Something broke or behaved wrongly. Use this one for a 500, and include the correlation identifier.

A question about the program

Cohorts, sessions, consent, expectations — questions for the people running Word Up rather than the software.

Something else

Anything that fits none of the above.

Then How can we help? for the description — at least 10 characters, or you get Please describe your issue (at least 10 characters). — a Reply-to email, and an Optional screenshot. The screenshot must be JPG or PNG, up to 5 MB. and carries the standing caution Avoid including private information. Choose Send message when you are done.

What to include

A support request that answers these gets solved on the first reply instead of the third.

  • The exact words on screen. Quote the message rather than paraphrasing it. "It says This reset link is invalid or has expired" identifies the problem instantly; "the link is broken" does not.
  • The correlation identifier, for anything that produced an error page.
  • What you were trying to do, in one sentence, and what you expected instead.
  • When it happened, to the minute if you can.
  • Which device and browser, and whether it also happens on another one.
  • Whether it happens every time or only sometimes.

Leave out passwords — nobody supporting Word Up will ever ask for one — and be careful about screenshots of another young person's work, reflections or check-in notes. Crop the picture to the problem.
