Privacy, consent and the audit trail

Waypoint holds information about young people, so it treats data rights, consent and accountability as working features rather than policy text. This page covers what you can ask for about your own data, how a Program Administrator fulfils that request without damaging anyone else's record, what a removal actually erases and what it deliberately keeps, and how the append-only audit log proves who did what.

Everyone Program Administrator W-UG-19

Before you rely on any of this

Everything on this page is implemented and covered by automated tests. That is not the same as being certified.

No independent privacy or safeguarding review has been completed

Waypoint is a development project. No independent security, privacy, safeguarding, or accessibility review has been completed, and no release is approved for production use or for storing real participant information. Use synthetic data only. Do not treat the behaviour described here as evidence of legal compliance.

What you can do about your own data

Every signed-in person, whatever their role, has the same self-service page. Open your account menu, choose Profile & settings, then the Privacy tab. The page is headed Privacy & data rights and its opening line reads Review the account information Waypoint stores and make a trackable request about it.

The stored account data panel

The first card, Your stored account data, lists the account fields Waypoint holds about you: Name, Email, Role, Account status, Pronouns, Joined, Cohort record and Mentor record. Anything you have not filled in shows as Not provided; an unassigned placement shows as Not assigned. The card notes that Placement identifiers are managed by program staff. — you cannot edit your cohort or mentor here, because those are program decisions.

This panel is a summary, not the whole record. The complete export covers far more: reflections, quiz attempts, submissions, attendance, messages, notifications and preferences.

The four request types

The second card is Make a data-rights request. Pick one Request type from the list, add Details if you want to explain, and choose Submit request.

Request type Stored action What it asks for
Access a copy of my data access A comprehensive export of everything Waypoint holds about you.
Correct my information correction A change to your name, email or pronouns. Extra fields appear when you choose it.
Remove my information removal Erasure of your personal data and closure of your account.
Withdraw consent consent-withdrawal Withdrawal of every consent record you gave or that was given for you.

Choosing Correct my information adds Correct name, Correct email and Correct pronouns, plus a checkbox Remove my saved pronouns which empties and disables the pronouns box. Fill in only the fields you want changed; leave the rest blank.

Under the form sits a standing note: Removal and consent requests may require follow-up so safeguarding and legal records are handled correctly. That is not a stalling tactic — see What removal actually does for exactly which records survive and why.

Your reference number

Submitting a request returns a green confirmation reading Request received. Keep reference … for your records. The reference is a short code beginning DSR-, and it is the only thing you need when you follow up. Every request you have ever made is listed under Your requests with its type, reference, date and a status pill reading either Pending or Completed. If you have never made one the list reads You have not made any data-rights requests. Long histories page in twenty-five at a time behind a Load more requests button.

Why the download button is disabled

Under the stored-data panel there is a Download complete data export (JSON) button. Until a staff member has verified and completed an access request for you it stays disabled, with the explanation Submit an access request. The complete export becomes available after program staff verify and complete it.

This is deliberate. A full export bundles a young person's reflections, quiz answers, messages and safeguarding-adjacent records into one file, so it is released only after a human has confirmed who is asking. Once the request is completed the button becomes active and downloads a JSON file; you also get an email headed Your Waypoint privacy request is complete, and the request shows the resolution note Your comprehensive personal-data export is ready in your signed-in privacy settings.

How an administrator fulfils a request

Only a Program Administrator can see or act on data-rights requests. Choose Privacy requests under the System group in the sidebar. The page is headed Privacy requests with the line Review and fulfil trackable data-subject requests.

1Status filter All requests, Pending or Completed. Opens on Pending.
2Requests list Type, subject name and email, reference, date, status pill, and a Review request button on anything not yet completed.
3Fulfilment panel Headed Fulfil followed by the reference. Empty until you choose a request.
4Paging Previous and Next, twenty-five per page, with the running count.
  1. Filter to what needs work Program Administrator

    The Status list offers All requests, Pending and Completed. The page opens filtered to Pending, which is the queue you work.

    What you should see

    A list under Requests. With nothing waiting you get an empty state reading No pending requests with the line Requests will appear here when users submit them.

  2. Open one for review

    Choose Review request. The right-hand panel fills in, headed Fulfil plus the reference, and explains its own limits: Apply only verified corrections. Removal and consent workflows are completed by the server while preserving required de-identified records.

  3. Apply a correction, if that is the request

    For a correction the panel shows Corrected name, Corrected email, Corrected pronouns and Remove saved pronouns, pre-filled with what the person asked for. You are meant to check the values, not trust them. A correction must change at least one field, and an email that already belongs to another account is rejected with That email address is already in use.

  4. Write a resolution note

    The Resolution note box is shown back to the person on their own privacy page, so write it for them. Then choose Complete request. The list reloads and a green banner confirms Request … marked complete.

    What you should see

    The request moves to Completed. A request can only be fulfilled once — a second attempt is refused with This data-subject request has already been fulfilled.

Verified legacy public records

Waypoint's public site accepts contact messages, volunteer signups, donation pledges and applications from people who are not signed in. Those records are not owned by an account, so a data-rights request cannot reach them automatically. The optional Verified legacy public records (optional) section lets you link them by hand.

It takes five lists of identifiers: Contact request IDs, Volunteer signup IDs, Donation pledge IDs, Application IDs owned as applicant and Application IDs owned as guardian. The instruction is Add only record IDs whose ownership you verified independently. Separate GUIDs with commas or new lines. Empty categories are not submitted.

High-risk ownership link

The screen carries this warning, and it means it literally: Linking a public submission can expose or remove another person's information. Verify every reference using evidence outside the submitted contact details. An email-address match alone never establishes ownership.

Three rules are enforced rather than merely suggested.

Well-formed identifiers only

Each entry must be a full, non-zero GUID. Anything else is rejected before submission with Enter full, non-zero GUIDs only. Invalid: …

A 200-record ceiling

A running counter reads … of 200 unique record references selected. Go over and you are stopped with No more than 200 verified records can be linked in one request. The server enforces the same limit independently. Split a larger job across several fulfilments.

One relationship per application

The same application cannot appear in both application lists: The same application cannot be linked as both applicant-owned and guardian-owned. Choose the verified relationship.

The ownership attestation

As soon as you enter a single record ID, a checkbox becomes required: I attest that I independently verified ownership. followed by I verified that this person owns every referenced record using evidence beyond an email-address match. Its help text is Required whenever any legacy record ID is supplied. This decision is audited.

Try to complete without it and you get Explicitly attest verified ownership before linking these records. The server refuses the same request with Verified ownership must be explicitly attested before records are linked. Ticking that box is a named, timestamped act by you, recorded in the audit log alongside the number of records you linked.

The two-person rule for removals

A Program Administrator can fulfil most requests, including their own. Removal is the exception.

You cannot approve your own removal request

If you are the subject of a removal request and you try to complete it yourself, the server refuses with Another administrator must approve your removal request. Removal is irreversible and it erases evidence, so it always takes two people: the one who asks and a different one who carries it out.

Nothing else about the request is special. Any other Program Administrator can open it and complete it in the normal way. If your program has only one administrator account, removal of that administrator's own record cannot be completed until a second administrator exists.

What removal actually does

Removal is not a delete button on a row. It rewrites the account into a de-identified shell and strips personal content from every connected record, while keeping the pseudonymous skeleton the program needs for safeguarding and reporting.

The account itself

The account status becomes Removed. The display name becomes Former participant, the email is replaced with a generated address in the reserved anonymized.invalid domain, the password hash is discarded, pronouns and photo are cleared, the guardian link is broken, and the session counter is advanced so every existing token stops working immediately. Outstanding refresh tokens are revoked and password-reset tokens are deleted.

Personal content that is erased

Record What happens
Reflections Text and mood cleared; the entry itself remains.
Quiz attempts Stored answers cleared; attempt numbers, scores and pass state remain.
Submissions Body text, uploads, links, youth note and mentor comment cleared; status and dates remain.
Check-ins Engagement, goals, prayer requests and action items cleared.
Messages Bodies replaced for a removed Youth or Guardian; the thread structure remains.
Attendance Notes cleared; the Present, Late, Excused or Absent mark remains.
Uploaded files Private files and submission files are deleted outright.
Notifications and preferences In-app notifications, scheduled reminders, notification preferences and display preferences are deleted.
Applications Names become Former and Participant; free-text answers and guardian contact details are cleared.
Linked public submissions Only those linked by a verified ownership claim. Names become Former participant and contact details are replaced.

What is preserved on purpose

Some records are kept in de-identified form, and this is a design decision rather than an oversight. Safeguarding history must survive a removal request, or a program could be made to forget a serious concern simply by asking.

  • The at-risk flag record survives. The reason text is replaced, but the fact that a flag existed, when it was raised and when it was resolved remains.
  • Consent records survive. Guardian name, relationship and signature are replaced, and the consent is marked withdrawn, but the record that consent once existed is kept.
  • The data-rights ledger survives. Each request's reference, type, dates and status remain so the program can show a request was made and handled; the free text and requested values inside it are cleared.
  • Counts, dates, statuses and scores survive so cohort reporting still adds up after a participant leaves. They carry no name.

Removal is irreversible. The account cannot be reactivated, the erased content cannot be recovered, and a removed person cannot be changed or exported afterwards — attempting either is refused with This data subject has already been removed and cannot be changed or exported.

Consent over time

Consent in Waypoint is a gate, not a form. A youth with a linked guardian cannot start lessons until a valid participation consent exists, and loses access again the moment it stops being valid. A youth with no linked guardian is treated as an adult and is not consent-gated at all.

What makes a consent valid

A participation consent is valid only when all of these hold at once: participation was actually granted, it has not been withdrawn, and today falls inside its valid-from and valid-until dates. Miss any one and the youth is sent to the A guardian needs to sign off screen.

Re-consent each term

An organisation setting called Require re-consent each term controls whether a prior consent carries forward. Its own description is blunt: A prior-term record will not unlock participation once a new active term begins. With the setting on, a consent must match the current active term, and its validity runs to the end of that term. With it off, a consent runs for one year from signing. The setting is on by default.

The media sub-permissions

Media consent is separate from participation consent, optional, and broken into independent sub-permissions. On the guardian's Media consent card they are:

Allow media use

The master switch. With it off, no media permission applies regardless of the boxes below it.

All photos and video

Covers both individual and group media.

Group photos only

Covers group media but never an individual photo or video.

First-name captions

Controls naming separately. A photo permission does not imply permission to caption it with a name.

Media permission is checked against a currently valid participation consent. An expired or withdrawn participation consent takes the media permissions down with it. A refused media action returns the message Guardian consent for individual photos or video is required. and the response carries the header X-Waypoint-State: media-consent-required.

Withdrawal takes effect immediately

A guardian withdraws from their own consent card using Withdraw consent. The status pill flips from Active to Withdrawn, the withdrawal time is stamped on the record, and the youth's access to learning stops at once — there is no grace period and no overnight job. A guardian may only withdraw a consent they themselves gave; anything else is refused with You may only withdraw your own consent.

A consent-withdrawal data-rights request is the wider hammer: completing one withdraws every consent record where the subject is either the youth or the guardian.

The consent statement is configurable

The wording a guardian actually agrees to is not hard-coded. A Program Administrator sets it in Settings under Guardian consent policy, in the Consent statement box, alongside the note These settings drive term access and youth-media safeguards. Left empty, Waypoint falls back to I give permission for this youth to participate in Waypoint activities.

Change the statement only between terms, and record why. Existing consent records store what was agreed at the time; editing the setting does not rewrite history, but it does mean two guardians may have agreed to different words.

The audit log

Choose Audit Log under the System group. The page is headed Audit log under the eyebrow Youth safety, and describes itself as An append-only record of who did what and when, with safeguarding details kept private.

Append-only means append-only

There is no edit control and no delete control anywhere on the page, and none exists elsewhere in the product either. Entries are written by a protected writer, in sequence, and are only ever added.

What an entry records

Column What it holds
Who The display name of the person who acted. Actions the system took on its own show as System.
Action A readable label, with the raw action name also present for assistive technology.
Target and detail What was acted on, plus any non-private detail such as a request reference.
When Date and time of the action.
Source Where the action came from, or Not captured when it was not recorded.

Safeguarding details stay private

Some entries are marked private. Those still appear in the log — you can always see that something happened, who did it and when — but their detail column shows Private followed by [redacted] instead of the text. The redaction happens on the server, so the private detail never reaches the browser at all. This is how a safeguarding concern can be accountable without being gossip.

Filters and export

The Audit filters card offers Audit user, Audit action, Audit date from and Audit date to, described as Filter the view and CSV export by user, action, or inclusive dates. Dates are inclusive on both ends. Choose Apply filters to run them or Clear to reset.

Export CSV exports exactly what the filters select, not the whole log. If a filter matches nothing you get No activity for this filter with the line No audit events match the users, actions, or dates selected. Try widening the range.

Who can read it

Only Program Administrators. The audit log sits behind the ViewAuditLog policy, which no other role holds — not mentors, not lead mentors, not volunteers. A mentor who reaches the URL directly gets a 403 and lands on You don’t have access to that.

The integrity check

Every time the log is loaded, Waypoint verifies the whole chain of entries in sequence. If verification fails, a red alert appears above the filters before you see any rows:

Audit integrity check failed

One or more audit records may have been changed, removed, or inserted outside the protected audit writer. Escalate this incident before relying on the log.

Treat that banner as an incident, not a bug report. It means the stored sequence no longer matches its signatures, which is consistent with direct database access outside the application. Do not use the log as evidence while the warning is showing, and do not attempt to "fix" it by re-running anything.

Denied actions are recorded too

The log is not only a record of what succeeded. Whenever a signed-in person is refused a protected action — a mentor reaching for an administrator screen, someone opening the audit log without the policy, a youth blocked by a consent gate — Waypoint writes a ProtectedActionDenied entry naming them, with the method and path they tried.

That means a pattern of probing is visible, and it also means an honest mistake is visible. Filter on that action when you want to see whether someone is repeatedly hitting a wall they should not be hitting — or whether their role is simply wrong.
